# L01 How AI Hears and Speaks | Presenter Script

Course: AI-22 · Video: 5 min · Words: 740

## Hook
Close your eyes and listen to a short advert on the radio. Was the voice a real person in a studio, or a computer? Was the music played by a band, or made by software in seconds? Today, it can be hard to tell.

## Explain
Hi, and welcome to AI Voice, Audio and Music. In this first lesson, we look inside the tools that make these sounds. In this course, we use three families of audio AI, and each one does a different job.

The first family is text-to-speech. It turns written text into spoken audio. You type a sentence, choose a voice, and the tool reads it aloud. Each voice comes from a voice model. That is a system that has learned how one voice, or one style of voice, sounds.

The second family is speech-to-text, also called speech recognition. It does the opposite. It listens to spoken audio and writes down the words. The result is a transcript. When each line of the transcript has timings, so it appears on screen at the right moment, it becomes captions.

The third family is audio generation. It creates new sound from a description. The type we use most is AI music. You describe the mood, genre, tempo and instruments, and the tool makes a short piece. A quiet piece of music that plays under a voice is called a music bed.

All three families work in a similar way. They are trained on many hours of recorded sound. But the system does not store the recordings like a library. It learns patterns. For example, how a voice rises at the end of a question, or how a calm piano piece usually moves.

Think of a language learner who has listened to thousands of speakers on the radio for years. Now they can understand almost anyone. And they can also imitate natural speech, with the right rhythm and tone. They did not memorise every programme. They learned the patterns.

Speech-to-text is the understanding side of this learner. Text-to-speech is the imitating side.

One more term will help you later. The noise floor is the level of quiet background sound in a recording when nobody is speaking. That could be a fan, traffic or an electrical hum. A low noise floor means a clean recording. AI clean-up tools try to lower it without damaging the voice.

## Demonstrate
Let's see how this helps in real work. Tomás produces a weekly community radio programme in Valparaíso, in Chile. He wants AI to save him time. But first, he wants to know which tool does which job. So he writes down his three tasks.

First, he writes a weekly weather and events update, but has no time to record it. Text goes in, and a voice comes out. That is text-to-speech. Second, he interviews local fishermen and wants a written version for the station's website. Audio goes in, and a transcript comes out. That is speech-to-text.

Third, he needs a gentle twenty-second music bed with acoustic guitar under his introduction. A description goes in, and new music comes out. That is audio generation.

Then Tomás lists one thing to check for each task. For the update, he will listen for place names the voice says wrongly. For the transcript, he will check names and numbers by hand. And for the music, he will check that it is calm enough to sit under speech.

Now, a common mistake. Many beginners think an AI voice understands the text. It does not. It predicts how the words probably sound. So it may read S T dot as street when you meant saint. The fix is to write clearly for the ear, and always listen to the full result.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, text-to-speech turns text into voice, speech-to-text turns voice into a transcript, and audio generation creates new sound from a description. Second, these tools learn patterns from many hours of sound, but they do not understand meaning. Third, your key terms are voice model, transcript, captions, noise floor and music bed.
