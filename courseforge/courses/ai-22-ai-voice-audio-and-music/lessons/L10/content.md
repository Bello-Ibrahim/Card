# L10 Capstone: Producing and Reviewing Your Episode

Course: AI-22 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O6, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
You now have every part: a script written for the ear, a voice, clean audio, a music bed, captions and a rights log. The last step is to bring them together, review them with fresh ears, and publish something you can be proud of and can defend if anyone asks where the audio came from.

## Explanation
**The capstone brief.** Produce a 2 to 4 minute podcast episode or narrated video with AI-assisted voice, clean-up and music. Submit it with captions (an SRT file or burned-in captions), a completed rights log and a disclosure line. You choose the topic. It can be a short lesson, a product story, a community update or a piece about your music.

"AI-assisted" means at least two of the following: a TTS voiceover, Whisper captions, AI or plugin-based clean-up, and an AI music bed. You may use your own recorded voice for the main narration. You must not use a cloned voice of anyone else. If you use a clone of your own voice, record that in your rights log [VERSION].

**The final review.** Before you export the final version, review five areas. Listen to the whole piece in one sitting, without stopping to fix things, and take notes. Then fix and listen again.

1. **Clarity:** Can you understand every word on a phone speaker? Is the noise floor low?
2. **Pacing:** Is there time to breathe? Are there long gaps or rushed sections? Is the music too long before the first word?
3. **Captions:** Do the captions match the final audio exactly, including any sentences you cut? Are names and numbers correct?
4. **Consent:** Is every voice either your own, a TTS voice, or a real person who gave written consent for this use?
5. **Licences:** Does every row in your rights log allow this use, and have you added any required credits?

**The disclosure line.** Tell your audience where AI was used, in one or two plain sentences. Put it in the show notes, the video description or the end credits. For example: "The narration uses a text-to-speech voice. Music was generated with an AI music tool. Captions were created with Whisper and checked by hand." Some platforms and countries have their own labelling rules, so check them too [VERIFY] [REGION].

**Analogy:** The final review is like a pilot's checklist before take-off. The pilot has flown many times and knows the plane, but still checks each item on the list. The list catches small problems that experience can miss when you are busy.

## Worked Example
Dilnoza is a geography teacher in Tashkent, Uzbekistan. She makes a 3-minute narrated video about the Aral Sea region for her students, using her own photos.

Her production checklist:

| Stage | Item | Done |
|---|---|---|
| Script | Written for the ear; read aloud once without stumbling | Yes |
| Voice | Own recording, cleaned in Audacity; no cloned voices | Yes |
| Music | AI music bed, instrumental, no artist names in prompt | Yes |
| Mix | Voice on top; fades; loudness set to platform guidance | Yes |
| Captions | Whisper transcript checked by hand; SRT exported | Yes |
| Rights log | Row for every asset with licence summary and date | Yes |
| Disclosure | Line added to video description | Yes |
| Final checks | Headphones and phone speaker; captions match audio | Yes |

Her review finds two problems. First, she cut one sentence in the final mix but forgot to remove it from the SRT, so the captions drift out of time after 1:40. She regenerates the captions from the final audio and checks them again. Second, her rights log shows that the music tool requires a credit, which she had not added. She adds it to the description with her disclosure line.

## Common Mistake
Many learners make captions early and then keep editing the audio. The captions no longer match, and nobody notices until a viewer does. Make or regenerate captions from the **final** audio, after all edits. Another mistake is writing a disclosure line that is vague, such as "AI may have been used". Be specific about what AI did, so your audience can trust you.

## Key Takeaways
1. The capstone is a 2 to 4 minute AI-assisted episode or narrated video, submitted with captions, a rights log and a disclosure line.
2. Review clarity, pacing, captions, consent and licences in one full listen, then fix and listen again.
3. Make captions from the final audio, and write a disclosure line that says specifically where AI was used.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 2: export the final episode or narrated video with captions, and submit it with your completed rights log and disclosure line.
**Tools:** Audacity (free) [VERSION]; a free Whisper app [VERSION]; a free video editor if you make a video [VERSION]; your rights log; the capstone rubric.
**Steps:**
1. Open your L09 draft and fix any notes from your listening checks.
2. Copy the production checklist from this lesson and tick each item only when it is done.
3. Listen to the whole piece once on headphones and once on a phone speaker, taking notes on the five review areas.
4. Fix the problems and export the final audio or video.
5. Create captions from the final audio with Whisper, correct them by hand and export an SRT file.
6. Complete your rights log, checking each licence again and adding required credits.
7. Write your disclosure line and add it to the show notes or description.
8. Submit the final file, the SRT, the rights log, the disclosure line and your completed checklist.
**What good looks like:** A clear, well-paced 2 to 4 minute piece with accurate captions, a complete rights log with dated licence checks, a specific disclosure line and a fully ticked checklist.
**Time:** about 60 minutes
