# L07 Generating Music Beds with AI

Course: AI-22 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O3, O5 · Video: 5 min (screen demo)

## Hook
The first ten seconds of a podcast or video set the mood. A calm piano says "relax and learn". A fast drum beat says "something exciting is coming". Finding the right music used to mean searching libraries for hours. Now you can describe the music you want and hear several options in minutes.

## Explanation
An AI music tool creates new music from a text description, often called a **prompt**. We use "a free AI music tool of your choice" [VERSION]. Free tiers usually limit the number and length of tracks and how you may use them [VERSION]. Note these for your rights log in L08.

For podcasts and narrated videos, we usually need a **music bed**: music that sits quietly under a voice. A good music bed is simple, steady and does not compete with speech. It usually has no singing, because lyrics fight with the spoken words.

Describe the music with five parts:

1. **Mood:** calm, hopeful, warm, focused, playful, serious.
2. **Genre or style:** acoustic folk, ambient, lo-fi, light jazz, cinematic, Latin folk.
3. **Tempo:** slow, medium or fast, or a number of beats per minute (BPM), such as "about 80 BPM" for a relaxed pace.
4. **Instruments:** piano, acoustic guitar, soft strings, marimba, light percussion. Add "instrumental, no vocals".
5. **Length and shape:** "30 seconds, gentle start, clear ending" or "loopable".

A good prompt combines all five: "Calm, hopeful acoustic folk, about 85 BPM, fingerpicked guitar and soft marimba, light percussion, instrumental, no vocals, 30 seconds with a gentle fade-out."

**Never name a living artist or a specific song in your prompt.** Asking a tool to copy a real musician's style can raise legal and ethical problems, and many tools block it or forbid it in their terms [VERIFY]. Describe the sound instead: mood, genre, tempo and instruments.

Always **generate several options**. AI music varies each time. Listen to each option with your voiceover playing at the same time, not alone. Music that sounds lovely by itself may be too busy under speech.

When you compare options, listen for: **mood fit** (does it match the message?), **tempo fit** (does it match the pace of the voice?), **busy parts** (fast melodies or sudden drums where the voice is speaking), and **the ending** (does it finish cleanly or stop suddenly?).

**Analogy:** Choosing a music bed is like choosing a tablecloth for a special meal. It should support the food, not distract from it. A bright pattern may be beautiful alone, but on the table it makes the dishes hard to see.

## Worked Example
Valentina produces an education podcast in Medellín, Colombia, about science for teenagers. She needs a 30-second intro bed that feels calm but curious. Her spoken intro is warm and at a medium pace. The presenter follows her steps on screen:

1. She opens the free AI music tool and checks the free-tier limits and licence page [VERSION].
2. She types her prompt: "Calm and curious, light Latin folk, about 90 BPM, nylon-string guitar and soft marimba, light shaker, instrumental, no vocals, 30 seconds, gentle ending."
3. She generates three options and downloads each one.
4. She imports her voiceover and each option into Audacity on separate tracks, and lowers the music track so she can hear the voice clearly.
5. She plays each option under her voice and makes notes:
   - Option A: good mood, but a loud marimba melody starts exactly when she says the podcast name.
   - Option B: steady, warm guitar, tempo matches her speaking pace, and a clean ending.
   - Option C: too slow and a little sad for a curious science show.
6. She chooses Option B. She writes the prompt, the tool, the plan and the date in her project notes.

## Common Mistake
Many beginners choose the track they like best when they listen to it alone. Then, under the voice, the melody fights with the words and the listener cannot follow. Always judge a music bed with your voiceover playing. A second mistake is a vague prompt such as "nice music", which gives random results.

## Key Takeaways
1. Describe music by mood, genre, tempo, instruments and length, and never by the name of a living artist or a specific song.
2. A good music bed is simple, steady and usually instrumental, so it supports the voice without competing with it.
3. Generate several options and judge each one under your voiceover for mood fit, tempo fit, busy parts and the ending.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Generate 3 short music beds for the same intro and choose one, explaining how its mood and tempo fit your voiceover.
**Tools:** A free AI music tool of your choice [VERSION]; your voiceover from L03 or recording from L06; Audacity (free) [VERSION]; headphones.
**Steps:**
1. Open your chosen free AI music tool and note the free-tier limits and licence terms [VERSION].
2. Write a prompt with mood, genre, tempo, instruments and length. Include "instrumental, no vocals". Do not name any artist or song.
3. Generate 3 options of 20 to 40 seconds and download them.
4. In Audacity, put your voiceover on one track and each music option on another track, one at a time. Lower the music so the voice is clear.
5. Listen to each option under the voice and write notes on mood fit, tempo fit, busy parts and the ending.
6. Choose one option and write 3 to 4 sentences explaining why.
7. Save your prompt, the tool, the plan and the date for your rights log.
**What good looks like:** Three options from one clear prompt with no artist names, notes on each option, and a choice supported by specific reasons, such as "the tempo matches my speaking pace and there are no sudden drums under my words".
**Time:** about 30 minutes
