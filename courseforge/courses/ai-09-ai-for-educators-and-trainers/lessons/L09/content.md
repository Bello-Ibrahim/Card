# L09 Building Quizzes with AI and Google Forms

Course: AI-09 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O5 · Video: 5 min (screen demo)

## Hook
AI can write 20 quiz questions in less than a minute. But how many of them actually test what you taught? Some will be too easy, some will have two correct answers, and some will test something you never taught at all. This lesson shows you how to find and fix them, and then build the quiz so it marks itself.

## Explanation
A quiz is only useful if each question measures the learning outcome. When you ask AI for questions, ask for a mix of three levels:

- **Recall:** remembering a fact or term. "What does 'depreciation' mean?"
- **Understanding:** explaining or recognising an idea in new words. "Which statement best explains why a machine's value falls over time?"
- **Application:** using the idea in a new situation. "A company buys a van for 20,000 and uses the straight-line method over 5 years. What is the yearly depreciation?"

Ask AI for an answer key and a short explanation for each question, so learners get feedback after they answer.

Then **analyse every question** with three checks:

1. **Does it test the outcome?** If the outcome is to calculate, recall questions alone are not enough.
2. **Is there exactly one correct answer?** Read every option carefully. AI often writes options that are partly true.
3. **Are the wrong options believable?** Good wrong options (distractors) come from real learner mistakes. An obviously silly option gives a free point.

Also check for clues: the correct answer is often the longest option, or the only one with the same words as the question. Remove these clues.

Google Forms is a free tool that can turn a form into a self-marking quiz with an answer key, points and feedback. The names of settings and menus change over time, so follow the steps as a guide. [VERSION] Do not collect more learner data than you need. Check your institution's rules before collecting names or email addresses. [REGION]

**Analogy:** A quiz question is like a measuring tool. A kitchen scale is excellent, but it cannot measure temperature. A question can be well written and still measure the wrong thing, such as reading speed or a trick in the wording instead of the skill you taught.

## Worked Example
Chen Wei teaches introductory accounting at a college in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia. His outcome: "Students can calculate straight-line depreciation." He asks the AI for 10 questions at three levels, with answer keys and explanations.

His review finds three weak items. One question has two correct options. One asks for the year a method was invented, which he never taught. One has the correct answer as the only long option. He fixes the first, replaces the second with an application question, and rewrites the options of the third to similar lengths.

The presenter can follow these steps on screen. [VERSION]

1. Open Google Forms and start a blank form. Name it "Depreciation Check".
2. Open **Settings** and turn on **Make this a quiz**. [VERSION]
3. Choose when learners see their marks and which feedback they see. [VERSION]
4. Add the first question as **Multiple choice**. Paste the question and four options. [VERSION]
5. Click **Answer key**, select the correct option and set the points. [VERSION]
6. Add feedback for correct and incorrect answers, using the AI's explanation after checking it. [VERSION]
7. Repeat for all questions, and use **Shuffle option order** if available, so learners cannot copy the order. [VERSION]
8. Use **Preview** to take the quiz yourself and check the marking. [VERSION]
9. Share the link with learners.

## Common Mistake
The most common mistake is to copy AI questions straight into the form because they look correct. Many AI quizzes contain mostly recall questions, even when the outcome is to apply or calculate. Count your levels and compare them with your outcome. A second mistake is to check only the correct answer and not the wrong options. A wrong option that is also partly right makes the question unfair.

## Key Takeaways
1. Ask AI for recall, understanding and application questions, with answer keys and explanations.
2. Analyse every item: does it test the outcome, does it have exactly one correct answer, and are the wrong options believable without clues?
3. Google Forms can make a self-marking quiz with an answer key and feedback; check the live settings and collect only the learner data you need.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 1: generate 10 quiz questions for your unit with AI, remove or fix any weak items, and build the final quiz in Google Forms.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier); Google Forms (free with a Google account).
**Steps:**
1. Write the main outcome of your capstone unit.
2. Prompt the AI: "Write 10 multiple-choice questions for [learners] on [outcome]: 3 recall, 3 understanding, 4 application. Four options each, one correct answer, an answer key and a one-sentence explanation."
3. Check each question with the three checks, plus clues such as a longer correct answer.
4. Fix or replace every weak item and record what you changed in a short table.
5. Build the quiz in Google Forms: turn on quiz mode, add questions, set the answer keys, points and feedback. [VERSION]
6. Preview the quiz, answer it yourself, and confirm the marking works.
7. Save the link and your change table for your unit pack.
**What good looks like:** A working self-marking quiz with 10 questions that match your outcome, including application questions. Each question has one correct answer, believable wrong options, and checked feedback. The change table shows at least 2 improved items.
**Time:** about 40 minutes

## Review Flags
- [VERSION] Google Forms quiz settings must be checked against the live tool before scripting: "Make this a quiz", release of marks, feedback options, the Answer key button, points, Multiple choice, Shuffle option order and Preview.
- [REGION] Rules on collecting learners' names and email addresses in online forms differ by country and institution; the lesson tells learners to check their institution's rules.
