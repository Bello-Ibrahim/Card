# L06 Differentiating Materials for Different Learners

Course: AI-09 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O4 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
In one room you may have a learner who reads fluently, one who is still learning the language of instruction, and one who finishes every task early and waits. One worksheet cannot serve all three well. Making three versions by hand takes a long time. With AI, the first drafts take minutes.

## Explanation
Differentiation means giving learners different routes to the same learning outcome. The content and the outcome stay the same. What changes is the level of support or challenge.

A common approach uses three versions:

- **Support:** shorter sentences, simpler vocabulary, key words explained, a partly completed example, sentence starters, or a glossary.
- **Core:** the standard version for most of the group.
- **Challenge:** more complex texts, fewer hints, an extra question that asks learners to apply, compare or evaluate.

AI can also help learners who are studying in a second language. It can simplify the reading level while keeping key subject terms, add a short glossary, or provide a key-word list in the learner's first language. Check any translation with a fluent speaker if you can, because AI translations can contain errors.

Reading level matters for accessibility too. Plain language, short paragraphs and clear headings help many learners, including those with reading difficulties such as dyslexia. You can ask AI directly: "Rewrite this at a simpler reading level, with sentences under 15 words. Keep these terms: ..."

The key rule: **every version must keep the same key content and outcome.** AI simplification can remove important ideas, change the meaning or introduce errors. After generating versions, compare them against a short list of key points.

Also be careful how you present versions. Labels such as "easy" and "hard" can make learners feel judged. Neutral names, such as colours or "Version A, B, C", are kinder, and you can let learners choose or move between versions.

**Analogy:** Differentiated versions are like different paths up to the same viewpoint on a hill. One path is longer but gentle, with rails to hold. One is the normal path. One is steep and direct. Everyone reaches the same view. If one path leads to a different hill, it is no longer differentiation, it is a different lesson.

## Worked Example
Nadia teaches geography at a secondary school in Dhaka, Bangladesh. Her class of 13-year-olds is studying why rivers flood. Some learners read well in English, and several are still building their English reading skills.

She first writes her key points: (1) heavy rain increases river water; (2) melting snow upstream adds water; (3) hard or paved ground makes water run off faster; (4) floods can damage homes and farms but also bring fertile soil.

She pastes her 200-word core text, which she wrote herself, into the AI with this prompt:

```
Create 3 versions of this text for 13-year-olds:
Support: short sentences, simple words, keep the terms
"run-off" and "upstream" with a one-line explanation, add
a 5-word glossary. Core: the text as it is.
Challenge: add one question asking students to compare
two causes. All versions must keep these 4 key points: ...
```

She checks each version against her four points. The support version has dropped point 4 about fertile soil, so she adds it back in one simple sentence. The challenge version says that floods are "always caused by rain", which is wrong, so she corrects it. She names the versions "Blue", "Green" and "Orange", and lets learners change version if they find theirs too easy or too hard.

## Common Mistake
The most common mistake is to simplify the content instead of the language. A support version that removes the difficult ideas teaches less, not differently, and those learners fall further behind. Keep the ideas and change the route: simpler sentences, more examples, more structure. A second mistake is to trust the challenge version without checking it; adding complexity is also a point where AI adds errors.

## Key Takeaways
1. Differentiation gives learners different routes to the same outcome: support, core and challenge versions.
2. Every version must keep the same key points; check each one against a list and correct anything missing or wrong.
3. Adjust reading level and language support for accessibility and second-language learners, and use neutral names for versions.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Take one short reading or task and ask AI for support, core and challenge versions, check that all 3 teach the same key points, and correct any errors.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier); a word processor or Google Docs.
**Steps:**
1. Choose a short text or task (150 to 300 words) that you wrote or are allowed to adapt. Remove any learner names.
2. Write 3 to 5 key points the text must teach.
3. Prompt the AI for three versions, stating the learners, the key points and what support or challenge to add.
4. Compare each version with your key points in a simple table: point, support, core, challenge (present or missing).
5. Correct any missing points and factual errors.
6. Give the versions neutral names.
**What good looks like:** Three versions that all include every key point, with clearly different levels of language and support. A checking table shows what you found, and at least one correction is recorded. The support version keeps the subject terms and explains them simply.
**Time:** about 30 minutes
