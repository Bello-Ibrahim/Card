# L12 Capstone: Completing and Reviewing Your Unit

Course: AI-09 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O6, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Your unit now has a plan, a quiz and a rubric. The last step is the one that makes it safe and fair to use with real learners: putting all the pieces together and reviewing them as a whole, with the eyes of a careful colleague.

## Explanation
In this final step you complete your **unit pack** and review it. The pack has six parts:

1. **Unit plan** with outcomes, lesson sequence, alignment table and at least 2 full lesson plans (L11).
2. **Differentiated materials:** at least one text or task in support, core and challenge versions (L06).
3. **Accessible visual material:** at least one worksheet, slide set or infographic that passes the 5-point accessibility checklist (L07).
4. **Quiz:** your Google Forms quiz (L09).
5. **Rubric** for the main assessment task (L10).
6. **Unit AI policy:** a traffic-light table for the tasks in this unit (L04).

Then you run a **final review** in four areas:

- **Accuracy:** check every fact, figure, definition and answer key against a reliable source.
- **Bias and representation:** use the checklist from L08. Look across the whole pack: do names, roles and examples show a range of people, or does one group appear again and again?
- **Accessibility:** clear fonts, contrast, alt text, simple layout, plain-language instructions and suitable reading levels, including for second-language learners.
- **Alignment:** every outcome is taught, practised and assessed, and every activity supports an outcome.

Last, write a short **AI-use note**. It tells a reader where you used AI, what you checked, and what you changed. This is the same honesty you ask of learners in an amber task. Keep it short, for example in a table.

**Analogy:** The final review is like a pilot's checklist before take-off. The pilot knows how to fly, but the checklist catches the one small thing that is easy to miss when you are busy. Your checklists do the same for facts, fairness and access.

## Worked Example
Rosa teaches at a technical institute in Cusco, Peru. Her unit, "Responsible Tourism", has four lessons for adult students training to become tour guides.

She collects all six parts in one shared folder. Then she reviews.

- **Accuracy:** One reading text, drafted with AI, gives a visitor number for a local site. She cannot confirm it in an official source, so she removes the number and uses a general statement instead.
- **Bias:** Across the pack, every tourist in her examples is from Europe or North America, and every local person is a seller. She changes several examples: a tourist from Brazil, a group from Japan, a local guide who manages a business.
- **Accessibility:** Her infographic uses green and red to show "good" and "bad" practice. She adds a tick and a cross symbol, because colour alone does not work for every learner. She also adds alt text to two photos she had missed.
- **Alignment:** Her outcome on "handling a difficult visitor" is taught in lesson 3 but is not in the rubric. She adds a criterion.

Her AI-use note has three columns: part, how AI helped, what she changed. For example: "Quiz: AI drafted 12 questions; I removed 3 and rewrote 4 wrong options."

## Common Mistake
Many educators review each part separately and never look at the pack as a whole. Some problems only appear across the whole unit: the same type of person always in the same role, an outcome that is taught but never assessed, or a policy that marks a task red while a lesson plan tells learners to use AI for it. Read the whole pack in order, as a learner would experience it. Another mistake is to write a vague AI-use note, such as "I used AI a bit". Be specific.

## Key Takeaways
1. The unit pack has six parts: unit plan, differentiated materials, accessible visual material, quiz, rubric and unit AI policy.
2. Review the whole pack for accuracy, bias and representation, accessibility and alignment, not only each part separately.
3. A short, specific AI-use note shows where AI helped and what you checked and changed.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 4: complete your unit pack, review it with the checklists from L07 and L08, and write a short note explaining where you used AI and what you changed.
**Tools:** Google Docs or a folder in Google Drive; Canva; Google Forms; Claude or ChatGPT (free tier).
**Steps:**
1. Put all six parts of the unit pack in one document or folder.
2. Check every fact and answer key against a reliable source.
3. Review the whole pack with the 5-part bias and accuracy checklist from L08.
4. Review every learner-facing material with the 5-point accessibility checklist from L07.
5. Complete the alignment table again and fix any gaps.
6. Check that your unit AI policy matches the tasks in your lesson plans.
7. Confirm that no learner names, grades or personal data appear anywhere in the pack.
8. Write your AI-use note as a table: part, how AI helped, what you changed.
**What good looks like:** A complete pack with all six parts, a record of the review with at least one fix in each area, a consistent AI policy, and a specific AI-use note. A colleague could use the pack with real learners after a quick read.
**Time:** about 60 minutes
