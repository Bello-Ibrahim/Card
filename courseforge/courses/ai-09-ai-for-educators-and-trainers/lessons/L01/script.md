# L01 How AI Can Support Educators and Trainers | Presenter Script

Course: AI-09 · Video: 5 min · Words: 739

## Hook
Think about last week. How many hours did you spend writing examples, adapting a text, making questions, or typing the same feedback over again? Some of that work can now start with an AI assistant. The question is which parts, and which parts must stay with you.

## Explain
Hi, and welcome to AI for Educators and Trainers. Whether you teach in a school, lecture at a university or run training at work, this course is for you. In this first lesson, we look at where AI really helps in teaching work, and where it does not.

An AI assistant, such as Claude or ChatGPT, is a tool that produces text in answer to your instructions. It has learned patterns from a very large amount of writing, so it can draft many kinds of teaching material in seconds. But it does not know your learners, your timetable or your institution.

AI assistants are usually helpful for five kinds of work. Planning, such as suggesting activities or timings for a workshop. Creating examples, like word problems or role-play situations. Adapting texts to a simpler or harder level. Writing first drafts of questions. And drafting feedback, by turning your short notes into clear, polite comments.

They also have clear limits. First, exact facts. An AI assistant can state a wrong date or definition in a confident voice. Second, your specific learners. It does not know who needs extra support. Third, local details, like your national syllabus or your company's procedures. It may invent them. And fourth, fairness, because its examples can repeat stereotypes.

Here is a simple way to picture it. An AI assistant is like a teaching assistant who works very fast but has never met your class. You can ask for ten example problems before lunch, and they will deliver.

But they do not know that half your group struggles with fractions, or that your company changed its refund policy last month. You give the direction, and you check the work before it reaches learners.

A simple way to decide what to give the AI is to sort your tasks into three groups. AI can draft, where the AI makes a first version and you edit it. AI can help me check, where you do the work and ask the AI to look for gaps. And keep fully human, for tasks that need your judgement, your knowledge of the learners, or personal data.

## Demonstrate
Let's see this in practice. Mariana teaches history at a secondary school in Curitiba, in Brazil. On Sunday evening, she lists her tasks for the coming week and sorts them into the three groups.

A starter activity about the causes of a historical event? AI can draft. She will ask for five options and choose one. A timeline worksheet? AI can draft too, but she will check every date against the textbook, because dates are exact facts.

Instructions for a group project? AI can help her check. She writes them herself, then asks the AI which steps might confuse a fourteen-year-old.

Marking thirty essays? Keep fully human. The essays contain learners' names and personal writing, and the grade is her professional judgement. A reply to a parent about a learner's progress? Also fully human, because it involves personal information and a relationship.

Mariana notices a pattern. The draft tasks are the repetitive ones that take time but not much judgement. The fully human tasks are the ones where her knowledge of each learner matters most.

One common mistake is to believe that if AI writes something quickly and fluently, it is probably correct. Fluent language is not the same as accurate content. A well-written worksheet can still have a wrong definition, or a question with two correct answers. So treat every output as a first draft.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, AI assistants can help with planning, examples, adapting texts, writing questions and drafting feedback. Second, their main limits are exact facts, knowledge of your learners and local details, so you always check and adapt their work. Third, sort your tasks into three groups, and keep anything with personal data or final judgement fully human.
