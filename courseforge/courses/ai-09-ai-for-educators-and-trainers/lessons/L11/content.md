# L11 Capstone: Planning Your Unit

Course: AI-09 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O3, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
A single good lesson is useful. A unit where every lesson builds on the last, and the assessment checks exactly what was taught, is much more powerful. In this lesson you bring together everything from the course and plan your own unit of study.

## Explanation
A **unit of study** is a short sequence of lessons, usually 3 to 5, that leads learners to a set of connected outcomes. You plan it with the same backward-design method as a single lesson (L05), at a larger scale:

1. **Unit outcomes:** 2 to 4 observable outcomes for the whole unit.
2. **Assessment:** where and how learners show each outcome. In this course, your quiz (L09) and your main task with its rubric (L10).
3. **Lesson sequence:** which outcome each lesson teaches, in a logical order.
4. **Key activities and materials:** for each lesson, including your differentiated and accessible materials (L06, L07).

An **alignment check** makes sure every outcome is taught and assessed. A simple alignment table has one row per outcome and columns for "taught in" and "assessed in". AI is good at this check: paste your plan (with no learner data) and ask, "Which outcomes are not taught or not assessed? Which activities do not support any outcome?" You then decide what to change.

**Analogy:** Planning a unit is like planning a short course of physical training. You set the goal first, for example running 5 kilometres. Each week builds a part of that goal, and the final run shows whether it worked. If one week trains only arm strength, it may be good exercise, but it is not aligned with the goal.

**The capstone brief.** Build a complete AI-assisted unit of study with lesson plans, differentiated materials, a quiz, an assessment rubric and a unit AI policy, reviewed for accuracy, bias and accessibility. You have already built the quiz (step 1) and the rubric (step 2). Today is step 3: the unit plan. Step 4 (L12) completes and reviews the pack. The capstone rubric scores: lesson planning and alignment, differentiated and accessible materials, quiz and rubric quality, review for accuracy, bias and policy, and the complete unit pack with your AI-use note.

Use this template:

```
UNIT PLAN
Title:            Learners (group only, no names):
Setting:          Number of lessons and length:
Unit outcomes:    O1 ...  O2 ...  O3 ...
Assessment:       Quiz (link) - outcomes: ...
                  Main task + rubric - outcomes: ...
LESSON SEQUENCE
Lesson | Outcome(s) | Key activity | Materials | Check
1      |            |              |           |
ALIGNMENT
Outcome | Taught in | Assessed in
AI POLICY (traffic light): task - colour - reason

```

## Worked Example
Samuel teaches mathematics at a secondary school in Kisumu, Kenya. His unit is on percentages for 14-year-olds, four lessons of 40 minutes.

His unit outcomes: (1) convert between fractions, decimals and percentages; (2) calculate a percentage of an amount; (3) solve percentage problems about prices and discounts.

He drafts the lesson sequence with AI, using the template headings. The AI proposes five lessons, including one on compound interest. Samuel removes it, because it is outside his syllabus for this age group, and merges two short lessons to fit four.

He pastes his draft plan into the AI and asks for an alignment check. The AI points out that outcome 3 is taught in lesson 4 but his quiz has only one question on it. Samuel adds two application questions about market prices to his Google Forms quiz. He also notices himself that lesson 2 has no check for understanding, so he adds a three-question exit ticket.

Finally, he writes full plans for lessons 1 and 4 using the timed-table format, adapting timings for his large class and replacing the AI's shopping examples with prices from a local market, all invented.

## Common Mistake
A common mistake is to plan the lessons first and add the assessment at the end. This often leaves outcomes that are taught but never assessed, or assessed but never taught. Fill in the alignment table before you write full lesson plans. Another mistake is to let AI decide the scope of the unit. It does not know your syllabus or your learners' starting point.

## Key Takeaways
1. A unit plan starts with 2 to 4 outcomes, then the assessment, then a lesson sequence with activities, materials and checks.
2. An alignment table shows where each outcome is taught and assessed; AI can check it, but you decide the changes.
3. Use the unit-plan template to organise your capstone, and adapt AI drafts to your syllabus, learners and context.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 3: write your unit plan with outcomes, a lesson sequence and full lesson plans for at least 2 lessons, drafted with AI and edited by you.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier); Google Docs or a word processor; your quiz from L09 and rubric from L10.
**Steps:**
1. Copy the unit-plan template into a document.
2. Write 2 to 4 observable unit outcomes.
3. Add your quiz and rubric to the Assessment section and note which outcomes each covers.
4. Ask AI to suggest a sequence of 3 to 5 lessons for your outcomes, learners (as a group) and time.
5. Edit the sequence to fit your syllabus or training plan, and complete the alignment table.
6. Ask AI to check alignment, and record what you changed.
7. Write full timed plans for at least 2 lessons, with at least 3 of your own changes in each.
**What good looks like:** A completed template with observable outcomes, 3 to 5 lessons, and an alignment table where every outcome is both taught and assessed. Two full lesson plans have timings that match the lesson length, a check for understanding, and clearly marked edits.
**Time:** about 60 minutes
