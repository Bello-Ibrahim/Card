# L05 Assessing Your AI Readiness

Course: AI-04 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O2, O3 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Two organisations buy the same AI tool. One is using it well after three months; the other has stopped. The tool was the same, so the difference was the organisation. How ready is yours?

## Explanation
AI readiness is how prepared your team or organisation is to adopt AI safely and usefully. It has five parts.

**1. Data.** Is the data you need accurate, complete, accessible and allowed to be used for this purpose?

**2. Skills.** Do staff know how to use AI tools, check their outputs and recognise their limits? Is there someone who can manage vendors and technical questions?

**3. Culture.** Do people feel safe to try new tools and report problems? Is there fear or strong resistance?

**4. Leadership support.** Do senior leaders give time, budget and clear priorities? Do they speak about AI in a consistent way?

**5. Governance.** Are there rules for which tools staff may use, what data may go into them, who owns each system and how decisions are reviewed?

Rate each part from 1 to 5 using the checklist below. Be honest. A high score that is not true will lead you to choose projects you cannot deliver.

**CertifAI AI Readiness Checklist.** Score 1 if the statement is not true at all, 3 if it is partly true, and 5 if it is fully true.

- **Data:** We know where the data for our priority use cases is stored, it is reasonably accurate, and we are allowed to use it.
- **Skills:** At least some staff use AI tools regularly and can check outputs; we have a person who can lead AI projects.
- **Culture:** Staff are willing to try new ways of working and feel safe to report mistakes.
- **Leadership support:** A senior leader sponsors AI work with time and budget, and priorities are clear.
- **Governance:** We have written rules for AI tool use, data handling and human review, and each AI system has a named owner.

Readiness does not stop you from starting. It changes where you start. A weak area points you towards use cases that need less of that area:
- **Low data:** start with generative AI tasks that use public or low-risk information, such as drafting, and fix data for later projects.
- **Low skills:** choose simple tools with a human check, and invest in training first.
- **Low culture:** start with a task staff dislike, and involve them in the choice.
- **Low leadership support:** start small, measure carefully and build evidence.
- **Low governance:** write a simple acceptable-use policy before any pilot.

**Analogy:** Readiness is like a health check before training for a long race. It does not tell you to stay at home. It tells you whether to begin with short runs or longer ones, and which weakness to work on first.

## Worked Example
Lucía Quispe is executive director of a hypothetical non-profit organisation in Peru that runs clean-water projects in rural areas. She scores her 30-person team.

- **Data: 2.** Project reports are in many formats, and some are only on paper.
- **Skills: 3.** Several staff use free AI chat tools for drafting, but nobody has been trained.
- **Culture: 4.** Staff are open to new tools and share ideas freely.
- **Leadership support: 4.** The board has asked for better use of staff time.
- **Governance: 1.** There are no rules on AI tool use. Staff sometimes paste community members' names into free tools.

Lucía had planned to use AI to analyse all past project reports. With data at 2 and governance at 1, she changes her plan. She starts with drafting donor reports from summary notes that contain no personal data, which needs little data and suits her staff's skills. Her one action for the lowest area is to write a one-page AI use policy within a month, including a clear rule not to paste personal or confidential data into AI tools. Report digitisation becomes a longer-term goal.

## Common Mistake
Leaders often rate readiness as an average and feel reassured by a score of 3. An average hides the weakest area, and the weakest area is where projects fail. Always look at the lowest score first and plan one concrete action for it.

## Key Takeaways
1. AI readiness has five parts: data, skills, culture, leadership support and governance.
2. Rate each part honestly from 1 to 5, and focus on the lowest score, not the average.
3. Weak areas change which use cases you start with; they do not mean you cannot start.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Complete the CertifAI AI Readiness Checklist for your team, rate each of the five areas from 1 to 5, and write one action for the lowest-rated area.
**Tools:** The checklist in this lesson, on paper or in a document.
**Steps:**
1. Read each checklist statement and score your team from 1 to 5.
2. Write one piece of evidence for each score, for example "no written AI policy".
3. Ask one colleague to score the same checklist separately, then compare.
4. Identify the lowest-rated area.
5. Write one specific action for it, with an owner and a date.
6. Check your top two use cases from L04. Does either depend on your weakest area? If so, adjust your choice.
**What good looks like:** Five scores with evidence, a comparison with a colleague's view, one clear action with owner and date, and a short note on whether your use-case choice changes.
**Time:** about 25 minutes
