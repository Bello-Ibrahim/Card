# L02 Where AI Creates Value | Presenter Script

Course: AI-04 · Video: 5 min · Words: 683

## Hook
Many organisations start with one question. Where can we use AI? A better question is this. Which of our tasks cost the most time, create the most errors or slow down our customers? Value comes from the task, not from the technology.

## Explain
In the last lesson, we learned the five terms leaders need. Now let's ask where AI actually creates value. In most organisations, it does so in three ways.

The first way is automating routine tasks. This is work that is repetitive, high in volume and low in judgement, such as sorting documents or routing requests. The value is saved time and fewer manual errors. People stay responsible for the exceptions.

The second way is supporting people's decisions. AI gives a person better information, but the person still decides. Think of flagging unusual transactions or forecasting demand. This is often the safest place to start, because a human checks every important outcome.

The third way is enabling new products or services, such as a service available at any hour. The value is growth or new reach. But it is also the most uncertain, because it depends on customers accepting something new.

A good task for AI has four features. It happens often. It follows a pattern. There is data about it. And a mistake can be caught and corrected. A rare task, or one where a single error is severe, usually needs strong human control, or no AI at all.

Value also has a price. Every use case needs data, people who can use and check it, changes to the workflow and ongoing cost. We cover these costs in the next lesson. For now, remember this. A use case only creates value if the benefit is larger than all of those costs together.

Here is a useful way to think about it. AI is like a new team member with a very specific skill set. You would not hire a person and then look for work to give them. You find the work first, then choose who fits. AI should be chosen the same way.

## Demonstrate
Let's look at three fictional organisations, one for each type of value. First, automate. Katarzyna Nowak runs operations at a freight company in Poland. Her team types details from hundreds of scanned delivery notes every day.

An AI document tool now reads the notes and fills in the system. Staff check only the notes the tool marks as uncertain. The value is time saved and fewer typing errors, and the control is a daily sample check.

Second, support. Wanjiru Kamau leads administration at a public hospital in Kenya. A forecasting tool uses past patient arrival records to predict busy days one week ahead. Her team still builds the staff rota, but with better information. Patient data stays inside the hospital's approved systems.

Third, enable. Rafael Souza is head of digital services at a regional bank in Brazil. The bank adds a chat assistant that answers simple questions in Portuguese at any hour. This is a new service, not only a faster old one. Rafael limits it to general questions, and hands anything about a customer's account to a human.

Notice that each leader chose the level of AI control based on the risk of a mistake, not on what the technology could do. And avoid a common mistake. New products sound strategic, but most organisations get faster, safer results by first automating or supporting tasks they already know well.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, AI creates value in three main ways: automating routine tasks, supporting decisions and enabling new products or services. Second, good tasks for AI happen often, follow a pattern, have data and allow mistakes to be caught. Third, start from the problem and its cost, not from the technology.
