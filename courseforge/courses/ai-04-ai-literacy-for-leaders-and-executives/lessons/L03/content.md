# L03 The Real Costs: Data, People and Process

Course: AI-04 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O2 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
A vendor quotes a monthly subscription that fits easily inside your budget. Six months later, the project has used far more money and staff time than planned, and the tool is barely used. Where did the cost go?

## Explanation
The licence or subscription is often only a small part of the total cost of an AI project. The larger costs usually sit in four areas.

**Data.** AI needs data that is available, accurate and allowed to be used. Teams often discover that the data sits in several systems, uses different formats or has gaps. Cleaning, joining and labelling data takes time from people who know the business. You may also need permission or a legal review before data can be used for a new purpose.

**People.** Staff need training to use the tool well and to know its limits. Someone must own the project, answer questions and handle problems. Some staff will worry about their jobs, and managers need time to listen and explain. If people do not trust the tool, they will work around it, and the value disappears.

**Process.** An AI tool changes how work flows. Who checks the output? What happens when the tool is unsure? Which steps are removed, and which new steps are added? Documents, handover points and performance targets may all need to change. If the old process stays the same, AI often adds work instead of removing it.

**Oversight.** AI outputs must be checked, especially at the start. Models can become less accurate when conditions change, so someone must monitor results over time, review errors and decide when to retrain, adjust or stop. This is an ongoing cost, not a one-time task.

A simple rule for leaders: before approving an AI project, ask for an estimate under each of the four headings, in staff days as well as money. If the team can only describe the licence cost, the plan is not ready.

**Analogy:** Buying a gym membership does not make you fit. Results come from routine, coaching and time. An AI subscription is the membership. Clean data, trained staff, changed workflows and regular checking are the routine that produces results.

## Worked Example
Aroha Tane is director of customer services at a hypothetical regional council in New Zealand. She wants to use an AI tool to summarise and sort thousands of public comments the council receives on local plans.

The subscription for the tool is modest. Aroha asks her team to list the hidden costs.

- **Data:** comments arrive by email, web form and paper letters. Paper letters must be scanned. Comments include names and addresses, so the team must remove personal details or confirm the tool is approved for this data. Estimate: several weeks of one staff member's time at the start.
- **People:** five officers need training on how to read the summaries and when to go back to the original comments. Two officers are worried that their analysis role will disappear. Aroha plans a meeting to explain that their role will move to checking and deeper analysis.
- **Process:** the report template for councillors must change. A new step is added: an officer checks a sample of comments against each AI summary before publication.
- **Oversight:** every consultation, a senior officer reviews whether any group's views were missed or misrepresented, for example comments written in te reo Māori.

Aroha estimates that oversight and data preparation together will cost more than the subscription over the first year. She still approves the project, because it will free officers for deeper work, but her budget is now honest.

## Common Mistake
Leaders often compare the subscription price with the salary cost of the task and declare the project "cheap". This ignores data preparation, training, process change and monitoring. A realistic cost view protects the project, because it prevents early disappointment and the decision to stop a useful tool too soon.

## Key Takeaways
1. The licence or subscription is often only a small part of the total cost of an AI project.
2. Hidden costs sit in four areas: data, people, process and oversight.
3. Ask for cost estimates in staff time and money under all four headings before you approve a project.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** For one AI idea in your organisation, list the hidden costs under four headings (data, people, process and oversight) and estimate which is the largest.
**Tools:** A document or spreadsheet with four columns. Optional: Claude or ChatGPT to suggest costs you may have missed, using a general description with no confidential details.
**Steps:**
1. Choose one AI idea, for example from your L02 task list.
2. Under **data**, list where the data lives, its quality and any permission needed.
3. Under **people**, list training, ownership and concerns staff may have.
4. Under **process**, list the steps that change, including who checks the outputs.
5. Under **oversight**, list how results will be monitored and how often.
6. Estimate the staff days for each heading and mark the largest.
**What good looks like:** At least two concrete costs under each heading, a rough estimate in staff days, a clear choice of the largest cost area and one sentence on how you would reduce it.
**Time:** about 25 minutes
