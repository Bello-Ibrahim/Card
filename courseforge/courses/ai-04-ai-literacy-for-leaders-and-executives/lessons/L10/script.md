# L10 Your 90-Day AI Adoption Roadmap | Presenter Script

Course: AI-04 · Video: 5 min · Words: 697

## Hook
You now have use cases, a readiness score, a risk register, a policy draft, vendor questions and a pilot plan. On their own, they are six documents. Joined in the right order, they become a plan your team can follow from Monday.

## Explain
In the last lesson, you wrote your pilot plan. In this final lesson, we turn all your work into a ninety-day roadmap, in three phases of thirty days each.

Days zero to thirty are for preparing. Name a sponsor and a project owner. Share the acceptable-use policy. Train the pilot team, check the data and take the baseline. And finish vendor due diligence and contract terms.

Days thirty-one to sixty are the pilot. Run it and measure it. Hold weekly check-ins, log problems and review risks against your register. Adjust small things quickly, but do not change the success measures.

Days sixty-one to ninety are for deciding and scaling. Review results against the baseline. Make the go, adjust or stop decision. Share what you learned, and plan the next step. Before you scale, confirm with your legal team that the wider use still fits the rules where you operate.

Here is the CertifAI roadmap template. It has one column for each phase. For every phase, you fill in six rows: goals, key actions, owners, risks and controls, success measures, and communication.

Bring your staff with you. Involve them in choosing and testing use cases. Be honest about how roles may change. Train people before you expect results. And give a clear way to raise concerns, then show that you act on them.

Then communicate at every phase. Say why you are doing this, what will happen, what it means for each group, and how people can give feedback. Tell the board what you will decide, and when. And explain risks openly, because hiding them damages trust later.

A roadmap is like a ship's voyage plan. You prepare the crew and supplies before you leave, check your position during the trip, and decide at the port whether to go further. The plan can change, but everyone knows the route and who is steering.

## Demonstrate
Here is a roadmap in action. Tran Minh Anh is managing director of a fictional food manufacturer in Vietnam, with two hundred staff. Her priority use case is AI support for forecasting weekly ingredient orders.

In the first thirty days, the supply chain manager owns the project. The IT manager cleans two years of order data, and the team completes the vendor questions. The main risk is poor data quality, so there is a data check before the pilot. The baseline of ordering errors is recorded.

In the pilot, buyers use the forecast as advice, and still place every order themselves. The risk is that buyers trust the forecast too much, so large differences are reviewed every week. The team measures ordering errors, waste and buyer satisfaction.

In the last phase, the leadership team reviews the results and decides to adjust and extend the pilot to a second factory. Minh Anh presents the results to all staff, and starts preparing the next use case.

Avoid one common mistake. Many roadmaps are just lists of technology to buy. A strong roadmap is about people, decisions and evidence. If yours has no owners or decision dates, it is a wish list, not a plan.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, a ninety-day roadmap has three phases: prepare, pilot, and decide and scale. Second, every phase needs goals, owners, risks and controls, success measures and communication. Third, bringing staff with you is part of the plan, not an extra.
