# L08 Asking Vendors the Right Questions

Course: AI-04 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O5 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Every AI vendor demonstration looks impressive. The demo data is clean, the examples are well chosen and the presenter knows exactly what to type. Your job is to find out what happens with your data, your staff and your contract after the demo ends.

## Explanation
Vendor due diligence means asking structured questions before you buy, and getting the important answers in writing. Pricing models and data terms change often and can differ between free and business plans, so always check the current version of the contract. [VERSION]

Use this CertifAI vendor question list. Ask every vendor the same questions so you can compare answers fairly.

**CertifAI Vendor Question List**

*Data*
1. Where is our data stored, and in which countries?
2. Is our data used to train or improve your models? Can we switch this off, and is that in the contract?
3. Who at your company, or at your suppliers, can access our data?
4. How long do you keep our data, and how do we delete it?

*Accuracy and evidence*
5. What evidence of accuracy can you show on data like ours, not only on demo data?
6. What are the known limits and common errors of the product?
7. Can we test it on our own sample data before we commit?

*Security and compliance*
8. Which security and compliance standards do you meet, and can we see current evidence? [VERIFY]
9. How do you protect against misuse, such as hidden instructions in documents?
10. How will you tell us about a security incident, and how fast?

*Cost and exit*
11. How does pricing grow as our use grows: by user, by volume or by something else?
12. What costs are not in the quoted price, such as setup, integration or support?
13. How can we leave, and in what format do we get our data back?

*Support and responsibility*
14. Who do we contact when outputs are wrong, and how fast do you respond?
15. How do you tell us about model changes that could affect results?

Listen for three warning signs: answers that avoid the question, claims without evidence and a refusal to put important commitments in writing.

**Analogy:** Choosing an AI vendor is like hiring a senior employee. A confident interview is not enough. You check references, ask for examples of past work, agree the terms in a contract and plan a trial period. The vendor question list is your structured interview.

## Worked Example
Ingrid Solberg is procurement director at a hypothetical public transport authority in Norway. Her team is evaluating two vendors of an AI assistant that answers passenger questions.

Both demos are strong. Ingrid's team asks all 15 questions and records the answers in a table.

- **Vendor A** says customer conversations may be used to improve its models on the standard plan, but this can be switched off on the business plan. It offers a two-week test on the authority's own anonymised sample questions. It shows current security evidence and explains that pricing grows with the number of conversations.
- **Vendor B** says data "is fully secure" but does not say where it is stored. It shows accuracy results only on its own demo data. Its exit terms are unclear, and it will not confirm in writing whether data is used for training.

Ingrid chooses Vendor A for a pilot. She lists four answers she needs in the contract: training opt-out, data location, incident notice time and data return on exit. She also asks her finance team to model costs at double the expected volume, because pricing grows with use.

## Common Mistake
Many leaders judge vendors mainly on the demonstration and the price. A demo shows the best case, not your case. The most important differences between vendors are often in data use, evidence on real data, costs at scale and exit terms, and these only appear when you ask directly.

## Key Takeaways
1. Ask every vendor the same structured questions about data, accuracy, security, cost, exit and support.
2. Ask for evidence on data like yours, and test on your own sample before you commit.
3. Get important answers in writing, especially whether your data is used to train their models and how you can leave.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Ask Claude or ChatGPT to play a vendor pitching an AI product to you, question it with the CertifAI Vendor Question List, and note which answers you would need in writing.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free plan), and the vendor question list in this lesson.
**Steps:**
1. Do not paste confidential company data or personal data into the tool. [VERSION]
2. Ask: "Play the role of a sales representative for an AI product that [describe one of your use cases]. Stay in role and answer my questions as a real vendor might, including some vague answers."
3. Ask at least ten questions from the list, covering all five groups.
4. For each answer, mark it "clear", "vague" or "missing".
5. List the answers you would need in writing in a contract.
6. Write two follow-up questions for the weakest answers.
**What good looks like:** At least ten questions asked across all five groups, each answer rated, a clear list of contract requirements and sharp follow-up questions for vague answers.
**Time:** about 30 minutes

## Review Flags
- [VERSION] Vendor pricing models and data-use terms, including whether customer data is used for training, change often and differ between free and business plans. The same applies to the free plans of Claude and ChatGPT used in the exercise; keep the reminder not to paste confidential data.
- [VERIFY] A reviewer may wish to add examples of commonly requested security and compliance evidence in question 8, after checking them; the lesson names no specific standards.
