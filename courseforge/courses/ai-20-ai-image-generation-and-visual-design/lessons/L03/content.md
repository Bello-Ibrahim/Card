# L03 Your First Images in Gemini and Firefly

Course: AI-20 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O3 · Video: 5 min (screen demo)

## Hook
You now know how to write a strong prompt. Today you will put the same prompt into two different tools and watch them produce two different images. By the end of this lesson you will know where the main controls are and which tool suits which job.

## Explanation
This course uses two free-first generators.

**Gemini** is Google's AI assistant. You create images by chatting: you write a request, see the result, then ask for changes in plain language, such as "make the background lighter". Google AI Studio is a more technical Google workspace where you can choose a model and see more settings. Both can create images, but the model names and features change often. [VERSION]

**Adobe Firefly** is Adobe's image tool. It works more like a control panel: you write a prompt, then choose settings such as aspect ratio, content type (photo or art) and style effects from menus. The free tier gives you a limited number of generative credits each month. [VERSION]

The main differences for a beginner:

- **Controls:** Gemini changes images through conversation. Firefly gives you buttons and menus. [VERSION]
- **Aspect ratio:** in Firefly you usually choose it from a menu. In Gemini you often ask for it in the prompt, for example "in a wide 16:9 format". [VERSION]
- **Output:** each tool returns a different number of images per request, and each has its own default look. [VERSION]
- **Downloads:** both let you download results, but file size and format can differ. Some tools add visible or invisible labels that show the image was made with AI. We discuss this in L10. [VERIFY]

**Analogy:** Gemini is like asking a helpful assistant to draw for you and giving feedback in conversation. Firefly is like working at a studio desk with labelled switches. Both can produce good work; they simply feel different to use.

**Safety note:** Do not upload photos of real people, client files or confidential material while you practise. Use your own simple prompts.

## Worked Example
The presenter follows these steps on screen. Menu names and positions may differ in the live tools, so check each one before recording. [VERSION]

We create an image for Efua, who runs a small bakery in Accra, Ghana. The prompt is:

```text
A wooden tray of freshly baked sugar bread loaves and meat pies on a bakery counter,
product photograph, eye-level close-up, soft warm light from the left,
golden brown and cream colours with a small green plant in the background
```

**Part A: Gemini**
1. Open Gemini in a web browser and sign in with a Google account. [VERSION]
2. Start a new chat. If there is an image option or tool menu, select image creation. [VERSION]
3. Paste the prompt and add: "Make it a square 1:1 image." Press send.
4. Wait for the result. Point out which details from the prompt appear.
5. Type a follow-up: "Keep everything the same, but make the background slightly darker." Show how the image changes.
6. Open the image and use the download option to save it. [VERSION]

**Part B: Adobe Firefly**
1. Open the Adobe Firefly website and sign in with a free Adobe account. [VERSION]
2. Choose the text-to-image option. [VERSION]
3. Paste the same prompt, without the aspect-ratio sentence.
4. In the settings panel, set the aspect ratio to square (1:1) and the content type to photo. [VERSION]
5. Point out the credit counter, if it is visible, and explain that each generation uses credits. [VERSION]
6. Click Generate. Compare the results side by side with the Gemini image.
7. Hover over the best result and download it. [VERSION]

**Part C: Compare.** Place both images next to each other. In this demonstration, the presenter names two real differences seen on screen, for example the lighting style, how the pastries look, or how much of the counter is visible.

## Common Mistake
Beginners often decide that one tool is simply "better" after one test. One image from each tool tells you very little, because every generation is random. Compare several results, and judge them against your brief, not against your first impression. A second mistake is to use all your free credits on quick experiments. Plan your prompt first, then generate.

## Key Takeaways
1. Gemini works through conversation, while Adobe Firefly works through a panel of settings such as aspect ratio and content type.
2. The same prompt gives different results in different tools, so judge the results against your brief.
3. Free tiers have limits that change often, so plan your prompts before you generate.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Create one image for a small bakery in Accra in both tools from the same prompt, and note 2 differences in the results.
**Tools:** Gemini (free) or Google AI Studio; Adobe Firefly free tier; a notes app. [VERSION]
**Steps:**
1. Use the bakery prompt from the Worked Example, or write your own for the same bakery with at least 5 parts of the L02 formula.
2. In Gemini, generate the image in a square 1:1 format and download your best result.
3. In Adobe Firefly, paste the same prompt, set the aspect ratio to 1:1, choose the photo content type and generate. [VERSION]
4. Download your best Firefly result.
5. Place the two images side by side.
6. Write 2 differences you can see, and one sentence about which tool you would use for this bakery and why.
**What good looks like:** Two downloaded images from the same prompt, two specific visible differences (such as "Firefly's light is softer" or "Gemini shows more of the counter"), and a short reason for the tool choice linked to the bakery's needs.
**Time:** about 20 minutes
