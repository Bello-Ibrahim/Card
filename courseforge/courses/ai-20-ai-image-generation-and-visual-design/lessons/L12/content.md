# L12 Capstone: Producing and Reviewing the Set

Course: AI-20 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O5, O6, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
You have a plan and three test images that match. Now comes the real work: nine more images, each checked, edited and finished, and a style guide that lets someone else continue your campaign next month.

## Explanation
Produce the set in four stages.

**1. Generate in batches.** Work through your shot list in batches of 3 or 4 images. Use the exact style block from L11 in every prompt. Add your best test image as a reference where your tool allows it (L06). [VERSION] After each batch, place all images so far side by side and check for drift before you continue.

**2. Keep a prompt log.** Record every final image as you go. It is your evidence for the rubric, and it shows your own creative decisions (L10). Use this template:

```text
PROMPT LOG
# | Shot (from list) | Tool + model/settings | Aspect ratio | Full prompt (subject + style block) | Reference used | Edits made | Checklist result | Final file name
01 | Tea tin close-up | Gemini, default | 1:1 | "A round green tea tin..." + style block | none | removed extra leaf (fill) | pass | hm_01_tin.png
```

**3. Check and fix each image.** Use the five-point checklist from L09: hands and faces, text and logos, reflections and shadows, repeated patterns, and style drift. Choose the smallest fix: crop, edit the area with generative fill or expand (L07), or regenerate.

**4. Finish in Canva.** Add real headlines, logos you own and exact hex colours (L08). Resize images to their planned formats and check every size by eye.

**Final review.** Before you submit, review the whole set with four questions:

- **Consistency:** do all 12 images share the palette, lighting, framing and recurring elements?
- **Brand fit:** does each image serve its planned use and leave space for text where needed?
- **Rights:** did you check the commercial-use terms of every tool you used? Did you avoid real people's likeness, other brands' logos and copyrighted characters? [VERIFY] [VERSION]
- **Disclosure:** how will you tell viewers the images were made with AI? Note any watermark or content credential your tool adds, and do not remove it. [VERIFY]

**Analogy:** Producing the set is like a kitchen during a busy dinner service. The recipe (style block) is fixed, each plate is checked before it leaves the kitchen, and the head cook writes down any change so the next shift can cook the same dish.

## Worked Example
Mateo and his colleague Valentina are producing a 12-image set for "Sunbird Juice", a fictional juice bar in Cartagena, Colombia. They have a tested style block with a bright coral, mango yellow and palm green palette.

In batch 1 (4 images), the checklist finds a straw that bends through a glass and a sign with broken letters. Mateo fixes the straw with generative fill and removes the sign lettering, planning to add real text in Canva. In batch 2, the light direction drifts in two images. Valentina adds the batch 1 hero image as a reference, and the next results match.

They log every final prompt, including the edits. In Canva they add the headline "Fresh from the coast" in exact brand hex colours, then make story and banner versions.

In the final review they notice that all 5 images with people show only young adults. They regenerate two shots with "an older couple sharing juice at a beach café" and "a father and daughter at the counter", with no identifiable real people. They record the tool terms they checked, with dates, and add a line to the style guide: "All images created with AI; disclosed in campaign captions."

## Common Mistake
Many learners do all the checking at the very end. By then, a drift in batch 1 has been copied into 11 images. Check after every batch. Another mistake is a style guide that lists only the prompts. A useful guide also records settings, references, edits and the terms you checked, so another person can repeat your work.

## Key Takeaways
1. Generate in small batches with a fixed style block, and check for drift after every batch.
2. Keep a prompt log with prompts, settings, references and edits; it is both your evidence and your guide for future images.
3. Finish every image in a design tool, then review the full set for consistency, brand fit, rights and disclosure.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 2: produce and finish all 12 images, then write a one-page style guide listing prompts, settings, edits and the tool terms you checked.
**Tools:** Gemini (free) or Google AI Studio; Adobe Firefly free tier; Canva (free plan); a document tool. [VERSION]
**Steps:**
1. Generate the remaining images from your shot list in batches of 3 or 4, using your exact style block.
2. After each batch, compare all images so far and fix any drift.
3. Check each image with the L09 checklist and apply the smallest fix.
4. Finish each image in Canva with real text, exact colours and the planned format.
5. Complete your prompt log for all 12 images.
6. Write a one-page style guide: the completed L11 template, your style block, key settings, a summary of edits, the tool terms you checked with dates, and your disclosure plan.
7. Do a final review of the full set with the four questions and fix any problems.
**What good looks like:** Twelve finished images that clearly belong to one campaign, a complete prompt log, and a one-page style guide that another person could use to create a thirteenth image in the same style.
**Time:** about 60 minutes

## Review Flags
- [VERIFY] [VERSION] Commercial-use terms of Gemini, Adobe Firefly and Canva must be checked against current official terms before recording; the lesson only tells learners to check them.
- [VERIFY] Whether and how each tool adds watermarks or content credentials must be confirmed (see L10).
- [VERSION] Reference image options and free-tier limits in each tool must be checked against the live tools.
