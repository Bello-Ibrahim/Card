# L11 Capstone: Planning Your Brand Visual Set | Presenter Script

Course: AI-20 · Video: 5 min · Words: 687

## Hook
Twelve images that look like one campaign do not happen by luck. They happen because someone made a plan before the first prompt. In this lesson, you write that plan.

## Explain
Here is the capstone brief. You produce a consistent set of twelve images for a real or fictional brand campaign, with a one-page style guide. The guide records your prompts, settings, edits and the tool terms you checked. You plan today, and produce in the next lesson. The rubric on the course page shows how your work is assessed.

A good plan has four parts. First, a moodboard. Collect six to ten images that show the feeling you want, like your own photos, earlier AI tests, or colour swatches. Use it only for your own direction, and do not upload other people's images without permission. It helps you see the feeling before you write a single word.

Second, the style guide. This is the heart of the plan. The template is on screen. It fixes your palette, one lighting setup, framing rules, the medium, the mood, two or three recurring elements, and a list of things to avoid, like text, logos and real people.

From the guide, you write one style block, as in lesson six. Describe colours in words in the prompt, and use the exact colour codes later in Canva. Third, the shot list. List all twelve images before you generate, with the subject, the format and where each one will be used.

Mix the formats, like square posts, tall stories and wide banners. And mix close-ups, medium shots and wide shots, so the set feels varied but consistent. Fourth, test images. Generate three very different shots. If they look like one set, the style block works. If not, fix it now.

Planning the set is like drawing a floor plan before you build a house. Changing a line on paper takes seconds. Moving a wall after it is built takes days.

## Demonstrate
Let's look at an example. Wanjiru is creating a fictional tea brand from Kericho, Kenya, called Highland Morning. Her campaign is called Tea at sunrise.

Her style guide sets a palette of deep leaf green, warm gold and cream. The lighting is low golden sunrise light from the left. The framing is eye level, with the subject in the left third and empty space on the right. And two elements recur, fresh green tea leaves and a woven sisal texture.

Her style block is on screen. It joins all of this into one paragraph of prompt words, and it describes the colours in words, not codes.

Her shot list includes a tea tin close-up as a square post, a steaming cup on a windowsill, tea fields at sunrise as a wide banner, and hands holding a cup, with no face, as a tall story. Eight more shots cover packaging, a breakfast table, loose leaves and seasonal offers.

She tests shots one, three and four. The wide field image has almost no gold light. So she moves low golden sunrise light to the start of the style block, and the next test matches. Three tests use far fewer credits than twelve images.

A common mistake is to start generating straight away and plan afterwards. You end up with thirty attractive images that do not form a set. And remember, consistency means a shared style, not twelve identical pictures.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, a campaign set starts with a plan: a moodboard, a style guide, a style block and a twelve-image shot list. Second, the style guide fixes palette, lighting, framing and recurring elements, and the style block turns it into prompt words. Third, test three very different shots first, and fix the style block before you produce the full set.

## CTA
Now it is your turn. This exercise is step one of your capstone. You will write a one-page campaign plan with a style block, a palette and a twelve-image shot list, then generate three test images. It takes about forty minutes. In the final lesson, Capstone: Producing and Reviewing the Set, you bring it all together. See you there.
