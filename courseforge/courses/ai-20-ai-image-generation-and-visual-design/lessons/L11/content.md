# L11 Capstone: Planning Your Brand Visual Set

Course: AI-20 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O3, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Twelve images that look like one campaign do not happen by luck. They happen because someone made a plan before the first prompt. In this lesson you write that plan.

## Explanation
**The capstone brief:** produce a consistent 12-image visual set for a real or fictional brand campaign, with a one-page style guide that documents your prompts, settings, edits and the tool terms you checked. You plan in this lesson and produce in L12. The rubric on the course page shows how your work is assessed.

A good plan has four parts.

**1. A moodboard.** Collect 6 to 10 images that show the feeling you want: your own photos, your earlier AI tests, or colour swatches. Use it only for your own direction. Do not upload other people's images as references unless you have permission.

**2. A style guide.** This is the heart of the plan. Use this template:

```text
BRAND STYLE GUIDE – [Brand name] – [Campaign name]
Palette:   [3–4 colours: name + hex code, e.g. "deep leaf green #1E5B3A"]
Lighting:  [one light setup, e.g. "soft morning light from the left"]
Framing:   [camera and layout rules, e.g. "eye level, subject in left third,
           empty space on the right for text"]
Medium:    [e.g. "natural product photograph, light film grain"]
Mood:      [2–3 words, e.g. "fresh, warm, confident"]
Recurring elements: [2–3 items that appear in every image,
           e.g. "a green tea leaf, a woven basket texture"]
Avoid:     [e.g. "no text in images, no logos, no real people's likeness"]
```

From the guide, write one **style block** (L06): a paragraph that joins palette words, lighting, framing, medium, mood and recurring elements. Describe colours in words in the prompt; use the hex codes later in Canva (L08).

**3. A shot list.** List all 12 images before you generate. For each one, write the subject, the format and where it will be used. Mix formats, for example 1:1 posts, 9:16 stories and 16:9 banners, and mix close-ups, medium shots and wide shots, so the set feels varied but still consistent.

**4. Test images.** Generate 3 very different shots from the list. If those 3 look like one set, the style block works. If not, fix it now, before you make 12 images.

**Analogy:** Planning the set is like drawing a floor plan before you build a house. Changing a line on paper takes seconds. Moving a wall after it is built takes days.

## Worked Example
Wanjiru is creating a fictional tea brand from Kericho, Kenya, called "Highland Morning". Her campaign is "Tea at sunrise".

Her style guide sets the palette as deep leaf green #1E5B3A, warm gold #E3A72F and cream #F4EBDD; the lighting as "low golden sunrise light from the left"; the framing as "eye level, subject in the left third, empty space on the right"; and two recurring elements: fresh green tea leaves and a woven sisal texture.

Her style block:

```text
Natural product photograph with light film grain,
low golden sunrise light from the left, long soft shadows,
eye level, subject in the left third with empty space on the right,
palette of deep leaf green, warm gold and cream,
fresh green tea leaves and a woven sisal texture visible in the scene,
fresh, warm and confident mood
```

Her shot list includes: 1) tea tin close-up (1:1); 2) steaming cup on a windowsill (1:1); 3) tea fields at sunrise, wide (16:9); 4) hands holding a cup, no face (9:16); and 8 more shots covering packaging, a breakfast table, loose leaves and seasonal offers.

She tests shots 1, 3 and 4. The wide field image has almost no gold light, so she moves "low golden sunrise light" to the start of the style block. The next test matches.

## Common Mistake
Many learners start generating straight away and plan afterwards. They end up with 30 attractive images that do not form a set. Another mistake is a shot list with 12 very similar images, for example 12 product close-ups. Consistency means a shared style, not identical pictures.

## Key Takeaways
1. A campaign set starts with a plan: a moodboard, a style guide, a style block and a 12-image shot list.
2. The style guide fixes palette, lighting, framing and recurring elements, and the style block turns it into prompt words.
3. Test 3 very different shots first, and fix the style block before producing the full set.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 1: write a one-page campaign plan with a style block, colour palette and a 12-image shot list, then generate 3 test images.
**Tools:** Gemini (free) or Google AI Studio, or Adobe Firefly free tier; a document or slide tool. [VERSION]
**Steps:**
1. Choose a real or fictional brand and a campaign idea. Do not include confidential client information in any AI tool.
2. Make a small moodboard of 6 to 10 images you own or created.
3. Complete the style guide template.
4. Write your style block from the guide.
5. Write a 12-image shot list with subject, format and use for each.
6. Generate 3 very different shots with the style block and compare them.
7. Adjust the style block if needed and record what you changed.
**What good looks like:** A one-page plan with a complete style guide, a style block, a 12-shot list that mixes formats and framings, and 3 test images that clearly belong together.
**Time:** about 40 minutes
