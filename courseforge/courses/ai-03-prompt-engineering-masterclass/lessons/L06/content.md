# L06 Writing and Rewriting: Emails, Reports and Messages

Course: AI-03 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O3 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Most people spend a large part of their working week writing: emails, updates, short reports. An AI tool can produce a first draft in seconds. The real skill is getting a draft that sounds like you and says exactly what you mean.

## Explanation
For everyday writing, four prompt patterns cover most needs.

**1. Draft from notes.** Give your rough notes and ask for a finished message. Your notes keep the facts correct, and the AI handles the wording.

**2. Shorten.** Paste a text and give a clear limit. "Shorten to under 80 words. Keep the deadline and the price."

**3. Change the tone.** "Rewrite this to be more formal", "warmer", "more direct". Name what must stay the same.

**4. Adapt for a different reader or culture.** A message for a senior director is different from a message for a new team member. A message for a partner in another country may need clearer structure, fewer local expressions and a different level of formality. Tell the AI who the reader is and ask it to avoid idioms and local references.

Two instructions often help with every pattern:

- **"Do not add new facts."** When rewriting, AI tools sometimes add promises, figures or details that were not in your text.
- **"Keep these points: ..."** List the facts that must survive: dates, amounts, names of documents, decisions.

After any rewrite, **compare the meaning**. Read your original and the new version side by side and check that every key point is still there and nothing new has appeared. A polished sentence that changes a promise can cause real problems with a client.

**Analogy:** Using AI to rewrite is like using a translator for a business meeting. A good translator makes you sound natural in the other language, but you still check that they did not promise a discount you never offered.

## Worked Example
Sofia is a sales manager at a hypothetical solar-panel company in Madrid. She has a formal proposal summary for a partner company in Singapore. Her original text is long and uses expressions that are common in Spanish business writing but may be unclear to her partner.

```text
Rewrite the proposal summary below for a procurement manager at a
partner company in Singapore. They read English well but it is not
their first language.
- Use clear, formal but friendly business English.
- Use short paragraphs and a bullet list for the three main benefits.
- Avoid idioms and references that only people in Spain would know.
- Keep these points exactly: 18-month pilot, 3 sites, price valid
  until [DATE], installation team provided by us.
- Do not add any new facts, promises or figures.
- Under 200 words.

Summary: [PASTE SUMMARY]
```

The new version is clearer and better organised. Sofia then compares it with her original, point by point. The four key points are all present. But she notices one new phrase: "with full technical support at no extra cost". Her original offer did not include free support. She deletes the phrase and adds a line to her prompt for next time: "Do not describe support terms."

Sofia's check took two minutes. Without it, she might have sent a promise her company could not keep.

## Common Mistake
Many learners accept the first draft because it reads well. Good grammar is not the same as correct content. The most common problems are added facts, lost details, and a tone that is too enthusiastic, for example words like "thrilled" or "delighted" in a message about a problem. Always read the output as the reader will, and compare it with your original meaning.

## Key Takeaways
1. Four patterns cover most everyday writing: draft from notes, shorten, change tone, and adapt for a different reader or culture.
2. Tell the AI what must stay the same and what it must not add.
3. Always compare the rewritten text with your original meaning before you send it.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Draft one real, non-confidential work message with AI, ask for a shorter and a more formal version, and choose the best one with a one-line reason.
**Tools:** Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini (free tier) [VERSION]; a notes app.
**Steps:**
1. Choose a real message you need to write, such as a meeting request, a reminder or a short update. Replace names and confidential details with placeholders.
2. Write your notes in 3 to 5 lines and ask the AI to draft the message. Name the reader and the tone.
3. In the same chat, ask: "Now write a shorter version, under [NUMBER] words. Keep [KEY POINTS]."
4. Ask: "Now write a more formal version. Do not add any new facts."
5. Compare all three versions with your notes. Mark any added or missing facts.
6. Choose the best version and write one line explaining why.
**What good looks like:** Three versions, a note of any added or missing facts, and a clear reason, such as "The short version keeps the date and the room number and is easy to read on a phone."
**Time:** about 20 minutes
