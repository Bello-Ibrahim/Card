# L08 Contracts, Invoices and Admin Help

Course: AI-10 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O2, O4 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Writing a polite message to a client who has not paid is uncomfortable. Checking what must appear on an invoice is confusing. AI can take away much of the discomfort and the blank page. But there is one line it cannot cross: it is not your lawyer and it is not your tax adviser.

## Explanation
Admin work is repeated and text-heavy, so AI can help a lot with:

- **Contract checklists**: a list of topics your agreement should cover, such as scope, deliverables, number of changes, deadlines, payment terms, late payment, ownership of the work, confidentiality and how either side can end the project.
- **Invoice text**: a clear layout and wording for your invoice template, for example the service description and payment instructions.
- **Payment reminders**: polite, firm messages for different stages, such as a friendly reminder, a second reminder and a final notice.
- **Short client updates**: weekly progress notes from your own bullet points.

What AI **cannot** do is tell you reliably what the law or tax rules require in your situation. Contract law, consumer rules, invoice requirements and tax rules differ by country, and sometimes by region or type of business. [REGION] For example, the details that must appear on an invoice, whether you must charge a sales tax or value-added tax, and what late-payment charges you may add all depend on local rules. [REGION] AI tools may give answers that sound confident but are out of date, from another country, or simply invented.

So use this rule: **AI drafts are not legal or tax advice.** Use AI for structure and wording. Check the legal and tax content with an official source, such as your national tax authority's website, or with a qualified professional such as an accountant or lawyer. [REGION]

Also remember confidentiality. Do not paste a client's signed contract, bank details or tax number into an AI tool. Use a blank template or an anonymised version.

**Analogy:** AI is like a helpful friend who has read a lot of contracts from many countries. The friend can help you remember what topics to include and can make your wording clearer. But you would not let that friend decide what your country's tax law says. For that, you ask the official office or a professional.

## Worked Example
Ingrid is a freelance illustrator in Bergen, Norway. A hypothetical publishing client is 14 days late with a payment. She asks ChatGPT:

> "Write three payment reminder emails for a freelance illustrator: a friendly first reminder, a firmer second reminder after 7 more days, and a final notice after 14 more days. Keep each under 100 words. Use placeholders for the invoice number, amount, due date and my payment details. Do not mention any legal penalties or interest."

She asks it not to mention penalties because she does not yet know what she is allowed to charge. She edits the tone of the first reminder to sound more like her.

Then she asks for an invoice template with the usual sections. The AI includes a line for a tax registration number and a tax rate. Ingrid does not know whether these apply to her. She writes three points to check:

1. Which details must appear on an invoice for a freelancer in Norway. [REGION]
2. Whether she must charge value-added tax on this service and at what threshold. [REGION]
3. Whether she may add interest or a fee to late payments. [REGION]

She checks these on the official tax authority website and plans to ask an accountant about the second point.

## Common Mistake
Many freelancers ask an AI tool, "What must my invoice include?" or "Is this contract clause legal?", and treat the answer as correct. The answer may describe another country's rules or rules that have changed. Use AI to prepare good questions and a clear draft, then confirm the legal and tax parts with an official source or a qualified professional in your country.

## Key Takeaways
1. AI can quickly draft contract checklists, invoice text, payment reminders and client updates.
2. AI drafts are not legal or tax advice; contract, invoice and tax rules differ by country, so check them with an official source or a qualified professional.
3. Never paste signed contracts, bank details or tax numbers into AI tools; use blank or anonymised templates.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Use AI to draft a polite payment reminder and an invoice template for your service. List 3 points in your contract or invoice that you need to check with an official source in your country.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier); a spreadsheet or word processor (free); your national tax authority's official website. [REGION]
**Steps:**
1. Adapt Ingrid's prompt to your service and ask for a friendly first payment reminder with placeholders.
2. Edit the reminder so it sounds like you. Remove any legal threat or penalty you have not confirmed.
3. Ask the AI for an invoice template with placeholders. Do not paste real client or bank details.
4. Highlight every part of the template that may be a legal or tax requirement, such as tax numbers, tax rates or payment terms.
5. Write 3 points you must check with an official source or professional in your country. Find the official website where you will check them.
6. Save the reminder and template for the capstone.
**What good looks like:** A short, polite reminder with placeholders and no unconfirmed legal claims; an invoice template with clear sections; and a list of 3 specific points to check, each with the official source you will use.
**Time:** about 30 minutes

## Review Flags
- [REGION] Contract terms, consumer rules and what freelancers may include in contracts differ by country; the lesson states clearly that AI drafts are not legal advice.
- [REGION] Invoice content requirements, value-added or sales tax registration and thresholds, and rules on late-payment interest or fees differ by country and type of business; the lesson states that AI drafts are not tax advice.
- [REGION] The Norway example names topics to check but deliberately gives no Norwegian rules; a reviewer should confirm no country-specific rule is implied.
