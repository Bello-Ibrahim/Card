# L02 Sharpen Your Offer and Profile

Course: AI-10 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O3 · Video: 5 min (screen demo)

## Hook
"I do translation." "I do graphic design." "I do bookkeeping." Thousands of people could say the same sentence. A potential client reads it and has no reason to choose you. In this lesson you will use AI to turn a general description into a clear offer.

## Explanation
A clear offer answers three questions in one sentence:

- **Who do you help?** A specific type of client, not "everyone".
- **What problem do you solve?** Something the client already worries about.
- **What result do you deliver?** The change the client gets, not only the task you do.

A simple pattern is: *"I help [type of client] [get result] by [what you do]."*

Most people find it hard to write this about themselves. AI is useful here, not because it knows your business, but because it can **ask you good questions** and then organise your answers. The best method is to ask the AI to interview you, one question at a time, and only then write drafts. If you ask it to "write my offer" without information, it will produce general text that could describe anyone.

From your offer you can build two short pieces:

1. **A profile** of 60 to 100 words for your website or professional network profile.
2. **A one-page service sheet**: your offer, who it is for, what is included, how the process works, a price range or "from" price, and how to contact you. You can design this in Canva with a free template. [VERSION]

**Analogy:** Working with AI on your offer is like working with a good interviewer for a magazine article. The interviewer does not know your story, but the right questions help you say things you already knew and never put into words. The final article is still about you, and you approve it.

Check every claim the AI writes. AI often adds impressive phrases, such as "award-winning" or "10 years of experience", that are not true. Remove anything you cannot prove.

## Worked Example
Joon-ho is a freelance Korean–English translator in Busan, South Korea. His old profile said: "I translate documents from Korean to English."

He opens Claude and types:

> "Interview me to help me write a clear offer for my freelance service. Ask me 8 questions, one at a time. Wait for my answer before asking the next. After the 8th answer, write 3 versions of a one-sentence offer and an 80-word profile."

The questions cover his best past clients, the work he enjoys, the problems clients mention, and what makes his translations different. His answers show a pattern: his favourite clients are small export companies that need product descriptions and packaging text for English-speaking markets. They worry about sounding unprofessional abroad.

One of the AI's drafts is: "I help small Korean export companies sell abroad with clear, natural English product text and packaging copy." Joon-ho edits it and removes "guaranteed higher sales", which he cannot promise.

**Screen demo steps (Canva):** [VERSION]
1. Open Canva and search the templates for "service sheet" or "one-page flyer" in A4 or Letter size.
2. Choose a simple template with space for a heading and 4 to 5 short sections.
3. Replace the heading with the one-sentence offer.
4. Add sections: "Who this is for", "What you get", "How it works" (3 steps), "Price" (a "from" price), and "Contact".
5. Change the colours and fonts to match your own brand, and add your own photo or logo.
6. Download the sheet as a PDF and check it on a phone screen for readability.

## Common Mistake
Many freelancers ask AI to "make my profile sound more professional" and accept the result. The text becomes long, full of general words such as "passionate", "innovative" and "solutions", and loses the specific details that make a client trust you. A strong offer is short and specific. Use AI for questions and structure, then keep your own concrete details: the type of client, the real problem and the real result.

## Key Takeaways
1. A clear offer says who you help, what problem you solve and what result you deliver, in one sentence.
2. Ask AI to interview you first; drafts written without your real information are general and forgettable.
3. Remove any claim you cannot prove, then turn your offer into a short profile and a one-page service sheet.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Ask Claude or ChatGPT to interview you with 8 questions about your service, then write a one-sentence offer and a short profile. Design a one-page service sheet in Canva.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier); Canva (free plan). [VERSION]
**Steps:**
1. Paste the interview prompt from the Worked Example into your AI tool and answer all 8 questions honestly. Do not mention real client names or confidential project details.
2. Ask for 3 versions of your one-sentence offer. Choose one and edit it until it sounds like you.
3. Ask for an 80-word profile based on your chosen offer. Remove any claim you cannot prove.
4. In Canva, follow the six screen demo steps to build your one-page service sheet.
5. Show the sheet to one person you trust and ask: "Who is this for, and what would I do for you?" If they cannot answer, make the offer more specific.
**What good looks like:** A one-sentence offer that names a specific client type and result, a profile under 100 words with no invented claims, and a readable one-page PDF service sheet.
**Time:** about 40 minutes
