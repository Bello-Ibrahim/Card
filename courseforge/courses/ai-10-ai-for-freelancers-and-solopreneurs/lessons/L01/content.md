# L01 Your AI-Assisted Freelance Business

Course: AI-10 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O1 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
When you work alone, you are the salesperson, the expert, the project manager and the accounts department. Most freelancers say the client work is not the hard part. The hard part is everything around it. What if some of that work could start from a draft instead of a blank page?

## Explanation
Every freelance business, whatever the trade, moves through the same cycle:

1. **Find**: decide who you help and let them know you exist.
2. **Win**: talk to a potential client, send a proposal and agree a price.
3. **Onboard**: collect information, set expectations and start the project.
4. **Deliver**: do the work, check it and hand it over.
5. **Invoice**: send the bill and follow up on late payment.
6. **Follow up**: ask for feedback, a testimonial or the next project.

AI chat tools such as Claude or ChatGPT can help at every stage, but mostly with two kinds of work: **drafting** (first versions of messages, proposals, reports and templates) and **organising** (turning messy notes into lists, tables, checklists and summaries). In Module 3 you will also meet **automation** tools, such as Zapier or n8n, which move information between apps without you copying it by hand.

What AI does not replace is the reason clients hire you: your expertise, your judgement and your relationship with them. A client pays a translator for correct meaning, a designer for good taste and a bookkeeper for accurate numbers. AI can make a fast first draft, but it can also be confidently wrong. You stay responsible for everything the client sees.

**Analogy:** Think of AI as a very fast assistant who has just joined your business. The assistant can write a first draft in seconds and never gets tired. But the assistant does not know your clients, sometimes makes things up, and must never sign a contract or send work without your approval. The assistant works for you, not in place of you.

A useful way to find AI opportunities is to look for tasks that are **repeated**, **text-heavy** and **low-risk if the first draft is imperfect**. Writing the fifth welcome email of the month is a good candidate. Deciding whether a client's tax situation is legal is not.

Throughout this course, one safety rule applies: do not paste client secrets, personal data or confidential documents into an AI tool. Use example data or remove names and details first. You will write a full policy for this in L07.

## Worked Example
Leila is a freelance bookkeeper in Casablanca, Morocco. She works with eight small shops and cafés. She writes down everything she did last week and how long each task took:

| Task | Stage | Time | AI help? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Replying to two new enquiries | Win | 1.5 h | Yes: first draft of replies |
| Writing a quote for a bakery | Win | 1 h | Yes: structure and wording |
| Chasing clients for missing receipts | Onboard | 2 h | Yes: polite reminder templates |
| Entering and checking figures | Deliver | 12 h | Limited: she must check every number |
| Writing monthly summary notes | Deliver | 3 h | Yes: first draft from her own bullet points |
| Sending invoices and reminders | Invoice | 1.5 h | Yes: templates, later automation |
| Posting on a professional network | Find | 1 h | Yes: ideas and drafts |

Her five best candidates are the summary notes, receipt reminders, enquiry replies, invoice reminders and the quote. Together they take about nine hours a week. She does not expect AI to remove all nine hours. She expects to start each task from a good draft and check it, instead of writing from nothing. Her core work, checking figures, stays with her.

## Common Mistake
Many new users start with the most impressive-sounding task, such as "let AI do my client work". This usually fails, because the core work needs the expertise the client is paying for, and AI mistakes there are costly. Start instead with the small, repeated tasks around the work: emails, templates, summaries and checklists. The time saved is real, and the risk is low.

## Key Takeaways
1. A freelance business has six stages (find, win, onboard, deliver, invoice, follow up), and AI can help with drafting and organising in each one.
2. The best first tasks for AI are repeated, text-heavy and low-risk; your expertise and client relationships stay human.
3. AI is a fast assistant that works for you: you check everything before a client sees it, and you never paste confidential client data into it.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** List every task you did for your business last week (or would do, if you are just starting) and mark the 5 that take the most time and could get AI help.
**Tools:** A spreadsheet or notes app (free). Optional: Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) to help you sort the list.
**Steps:**
1. Write every business task from last week, including small ones such as replying to emails. If you have no clients yet, list the tasks you expect for one typical project.
2. Next to each task, write its stage (find, win, onboard, deliver, invoice, follow up) and an estimate of the time it took.
3. For each task, ask: Is it repeated? Is it mostly text? Is a first draft low-risk? Mark "Yes", "Limited" or "No" for AI help.
4. Choose the 5 tasks with the most time and a "Yes".
5. Optional: paste your task list (without client names) into an AI tool and ask, "Which of these tasks could AI help me draft or organise, and which should stay fully human? Explain why."
6. Keep your list. You will use it again in L09.
**What good looks like:** A table with at least 10 tasks across at least 4 stages, a time estimate for each, and 5 clearly marked AI candidates. Core expert work is marked "Limited" or "No", with a short reason.
**Time:** about 25 minutes
