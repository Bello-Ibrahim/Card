# L06 Delivering Work Faster with Prompt Templates

Course: AI-10 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O4 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
If you type a new prompt every time you need a report, a brief or a first draft, you get different results every time. Some are good; some are not. The freelancers who save the most time do not write better prompts each day. They write good prompts once and reuse them.

## Explanation
A **prompt template** is a saved prompt with fixed instructions and blanks that you fill in for each client or task. A good template has five parts:

1. **Role and context**: who the AI should act as, and for what type of client.
2. **Task**: exactly what to produce.
3. **Inputs**: the blanks you fill in, marked clearly, for example [CLIENT TYPE], [DATA] or [KEY POINTS].
4. **Format**: length, headings, tone and language.
5. **Rules**: what the AI must not do, such as "do not invent figures" or "mark anything uncertain with [CHECK]".

Start with your most common deliverables: for example first drafts, research notes, design briefs, lesson plans or monthly reports. Test each template three times with different example inputs, and improve it when the output is weak.

How much help should you expect? In routine work, a good template can produce a first draft that covers much of the standard structure and wording. The share varies a lot by task, by trade and by how good your inputs are, so do not rely on any general figure. Measure it for your own work. What stays with you is the part clients pay for: the expert decisions, the judgement about what matters, and the final check.

**Analogy:** A prompt template is like a recipe card in a busy restaurant kitchen. The chef does not invent the sauce again every evening. The recipe gives the same good base each time, and the chef still tastes it and adjusts it before the plate leaves the kitchen.

Keep your templates in one document or notes app, with a name, the date you last changed them, and an example of good output. Never put real client secrets or personal data into the inputs; use summaries or anonymised figures instead.

## Worked Example
Mateus is a social media consultant in Porto Alegre, Brazil. Every month he writes a performance report for each of his six small-business clients. Each report used to take him about two hours.

He writes this template:

> "You are an assistant to a social media consultant who works with small local businesses. Write a monthly report for a [BUSINESS TYPE] client. Inputs: this month's figures [FIGURES], last month's figures [PREVIOUS FIGURES], and my notes [MY NOTES]. Format: 1) a 3-sentence summary in plain language; 2) a table comparing this month and last month; 3) three observations; 4) two recommendations, based only on my notes. Rules: use only the figures I give; do not calculate percentages unless I ask; mark anything uncertain with [CHECK]; no marketing jargon."

He removes client names and account details from his inputs, and pastes only the figures he exported and his own bullet-point notes.

He records his times. With the template, the first draft takes about 10 minutes. He then spends about 35 minutes checking every figure against his source, rewriting the recommendations in his own words and adding one idea specific to each client. His total time is about 45 minutes per report, measured on his own work. The AI never decides what to recommend; it organises what Mateus already concluded.

## Common Mistake
Many freelancers build a template and then stop checking the output, because the first few results were good. Templates reduce variation, but AI can still invent a figure, change a number or add a claim that is not in your inputs. Keep the "use only my inputs" rule in every template, and check every number and fact against your source, every time.

## Key Takeaways
1. A prompt template has five parts: role and context, task, inputs, format and rules.
2. Templates give you a consistent first draft for routine work; how much time they save depends on your task, so measure it yourself.
3. You keep the expert decisions and the final check, and you never put confidential client data into the inputs.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Write and test 3 prompt templates for your most common deliverables. Record how long each task takes with and without the template.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier); a notes app or document to store your templates; a timer.
**Steps:**
1. Choose your 3 most common deliverables. Use your task list from L01 if it helps.
2. For each one, write a template with the five parts from the Explanation. Mark the blanks clearly.
3. Test each template with 3 different example inputs. Use example or anonymised data only.
4. After each test, improve the template where the output was weak, too long or included invented details.
5. For one real or example task, record the time without the template (from memory or a fresh attempt) and the time with the template, including your checking time.
6. Save the final templates in one document with a name, the date and an example of good output.
**What good looks like:** Three tested templates with all five parts, each including a rule against invented facts. A simple table shows the time with and without each template, including checking time.
**Time:** about 45 minutes
