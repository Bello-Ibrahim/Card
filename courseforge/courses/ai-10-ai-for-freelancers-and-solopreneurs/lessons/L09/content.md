# L09 Mapping Your Client Workflow

Course: AI-10 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O5 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Most freelancers could not describe, step by step, what happens between a new enquiry and a paid invoice. The steps live in their head, and they change a little with every client. You cannot improve, or automate, a process you have never written down.

## Explanation
A **workflow map** is a simple list or diagram of every step in your client process, in order, from first contact to final invoice. For each step you write who does it, what tool you use, how long it takes and how often it happens.

Then you give each step one of three labels:

- **AI draft**: AI writes a first version and you check and finish it. Examples: proposals, welcome emails, reports.
- **Automation**: a tool such as Zapier or n8n does it without you, following fixed rules. Examples: copying form answers into a spreadsheet, sending a standard confirmation, creating a task.
- **Human only**: you do it yourself, because it needs judgement, trust or expertise. Examples: discovery calls, pricing decisions, final quality checks, difficult conversations.

**Good automation candidates** are steps that are **repeated** (they happen for every client), **rule-based** (the same input always leads to the same action), and **low-risk** (a mistake is easy to notice and fix). If a step needs you to think, decide or be personal, it is not an automation candidate, though AI may still help you draft it.

Here is a template you can copy into a spreadsheet or document. It is also the basis of your capstone.

| # | Stage | Step | Tool now | Time | How often | Label | Why this label |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find | ... | ... | ... | ... | AI draft / Automation / Human only | ... |
| 2 | Win | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 3 | Onboard | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 4 | Deliver | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 5 | Invoice | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 6 | Follow up | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

Under the table, add two lines: "Automation candidate 1: ..." and "Automation candidate 2: ...", each with the trigger (what starts it) and the action (what should happen).

**Analogy:** A workflow map is like a map of the route you drive to work every day. You know the way without thinking, so you never notice the slow junction or the unnecessary turn. When you draw it on paper, you suddenly see where you lose time and where a shortcut is possible.

When you write your map, describe steps in general terms. Do not add real client names or personal details to a document you will share or paste into AI.

## Worked Example
Meera is a freelance interior designer in Pune, India. She works with home owners on single-room projects. She maps her process:

| # | Stage | Step | Time | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Win | Enquiry arrives through website form | 5 min | Automation |
| 2 | Win | Copy details into client spreadsheet | 10 min | Automation |
| 3 | Win | Discovery call | 45 min | Human only |
| 4 | Win | Write proposal from call notes | 1 h | AI draft |
| 5 | Onboard | Send welcome email and questionnaire | 20 min | AI draft (template) |
| 6 | Deliver | Design concept and material choices | 8 h | Human only |
| 7 | Deliver | Weekly progress update | 20 min | AI draft |
| 8 | Invoice | Send invoice | 15 min | Human only (check), template |
| 9 | Invoice | Payment reminder when overdue | 15 min | Automation (later) |

She sees that steps 1 and 2 happen for every enquiry, follow a fixed rule and are easy to check. They become her two automation candidates: "When a new form entry arrives, add a row to my client spreadsheet" and "then send a standard thank-you email". The design work and the discovery call stay fully human, because clients hire Meera for her taste and advice.

## Common Mistake
Many learners label too many steps as "Automation" because it sounds efficient. A proposal or a quality check may be repeated, but it is not rule-based: it needs your judgement. Automating it would send unchecked work to clients. Start with one or two simple, rule-based steps, and label judgement steps as "AI draft" or "Human only".

## Key Takeaways
1. A workflow map lists every step from first contact to final invoice, with the tool, time and frequency of each.
2. Label each step "AI draft", "Automation" or "Human only", and give a reason.
3. Good automation candidates are repeated, rule-based and low-risk; judgement steps stay human or become AI drafts that you check.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 1: map your full client workflow from proposal to invoice. Mark each step and choose 2 repeated steps to automate.
**Tools:** A spreadsheet or document (free, such as Google Sheets or LibreOffice Calc); your task list from L01. Optional: Claude or ChatGPT to suggest steps you may have missed.
**Steps:**
1. Copy the workflow map template from the Explanation into a spreadsheet.
2. List every step for one typical project, from the first enquiry to the paid invoice and follow-up. Aim for 10 to 15 steps. If you have no clients yet, use a realistic example project.
3. Fill in the tool, time and frequency for each step.
4. Label each step "AI draft", "Automation" or "Human only", and write a one-line reason.
5. Optional: describe your steps in general terms to an AI tool and ask, "Which steps have I missed?" Add only the ones that are true for you.
6. Choose 2 automation candidates. For each, write the trigger and the action.
7. Save the map. You will build one automation in L10 and review every label in L11.
**What good looks like:** A complete map with 10 to 15 steps covering all stages from proposal to invoice, each with a label and reason, and 2 clearly described automation candidates that are repeated, rule-based and low-risk.
**Time:** about 40 minutes
