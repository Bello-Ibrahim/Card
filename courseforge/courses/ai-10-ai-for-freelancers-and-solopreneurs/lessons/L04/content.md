# L04 Proposals and Quotes That Win Work

Course: AI-10 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O3 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
You had a great call with a potential client. They said, "Send me a proposal." Now it is 10 p.m., your notes are messy, and you are staring at a blank page. AI can give you a strong first draft in minutes, but only you can make sure it promises the right things.

## Explanation
A clear proposal follows six parts. Clients should be able to find each one quickly.

1. **The client's problem**: in their words, from the call. This shows you listened.
2. **Your approach**: how you will solve it, in three to five steps.
3. **Deliverables**: exactly what they will receive, with quantities (for example, "5 page designs", "2 rounds of changes").
4. **Timeline**: key dates or weeks, and what you need from the client and when.
5. **Price**: the total, what it includes, and payment terms.
6. **Next step**: one clear action, such as "Reply to confirm and I will send the agreement."

AI is very good at turning messy notes into this structure. It is not good at knowing your real prices, your available time or what the client actually agreed. It will often **fill gaps with invented details**: an extra deliverable, a faster timeline or a round number for the price. Each of these becomes a promise once the client reads it.

So the method is: **you provide the facts, AI provides the structure, and you check every promise.** Before you send any proposal, read it once only for deliverables, dates and prices, and ask: "Can I really deliver this, at this price, by this date?"

**Analogy:** An AI-drafted proposal is like a builder's plan drawn by an assistant from your sketch. It may look professional, but if the assistant added an extra room you never discussed, you will have to build that room at the price you agreed. Check the plan before the client signs it.

Remove confidential client details from your notes before pasting them into AI. Use "the client" instead of names, and leave out financial or personal information you do not need for the draft.

## Worked Example
Aisha is a freelance web designer in Dubai, United Arab Emirates. After a call with a small hypothetical yoga studio, her notes say:

> Studio owner. Old site slow, not mobile-friendly. Wants class timetable + online booking link (already uses a booking app). 5 pages. Arabic and English. Launch before new term in 6 weeks. Budget "flexible but careful". Photos: they have some, need a few more?

She removes the studio name and asks Claude:

> "Turn these call notes into a 2-page proposal with 6 sections: problem, approach, deliverables, timeline, price, next step. Use a friendly, professional tone. Use [PRICE] and [DATE] placeholders. Do not add any deliverables that are not in my notes."

The draft is well organised. When Aisha checks it, she finds three problems:

- It says "custom booking system". The client already has a booking app; Aisha will only add a link. She corrects it.
- It includes "professional photography". She does not offer photography, so she changes it to "advice on which extra photos to take".
- It offers "unlimited revisions". She changes this to "2 rounds of changes per page".

She then adds her own price, a 6-week timeline with the client's deadlines for content, and a 50 percent deposit term that she normally uses. The final proposal is two pages and every promise is one she can keep.

## Common Mistake
Many freelancers trust the AI draft because it looks professional. But professional-looking text is not the same as correct text. The most common problems are invented deliverables, unrealistic timelines and vague phrases such as "ongoing support" with no limit. Tell the AI not to add anything outside your notes, use placeholders for prices and dates, and always do a final check focused only on promises.

## Key Takeaways
1. A clear proposal has six parts: problem, approach, deliverables, timeline, price and next step.
2. You provide the facts and AI provides the structure; tell the AI not to add anything that is not in your notes.
3. Check every deliverable, date and price before sending, because each one becomes a promise you must keep.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Write notes from a real or example client call and use AI to draft a proposal from them. Check every deliverable, date and price, and remove anything you cannot promise.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier); a word processor such as Google Docs or LibreOffice Writer (free).
**Steps:**
1. Write 8 to 12 lines of call notes. If you have no recent call, invent a realistic example client. Remove names and confidential details.
2. Paste the notes into your AI tool with the prompt from the Worked Example.
3. Read the draft once for structure: are all six parts there?
4. Read it again only for promises. Highlight every deliverable, date, price, quantity and guarantee.
5. For each highlight, ask: "Is this in my notes, and can I deliver it?" Correct or remove it.
6. Add your own price, payment terms and dates.
7. Save the final version as your proposal template. You will reuse it in the capstone.
**What good looks like:** A proposal of about two pages with all six parts, clear quantities for deliverables, realistic dates, your own price, and no promise you cannot keep. You can point to where each deliverable came from in your notes.
**Time:** about 35 minutes
