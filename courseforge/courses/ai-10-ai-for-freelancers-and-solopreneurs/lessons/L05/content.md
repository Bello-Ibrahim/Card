# L05 Onboarding Clients Smoothly

Course: AI-10 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O4 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
The client said yes. Then, three weeks later, you discover they expected a feature you never discussed, and they have not sent the files you needed on day one. Many project problems do not start in the middle. They start in the first week.

## Explanation
**Onboarding** is everything that happens between "yes" and the start of the real work. A good onboarding process does three things:

- It **welcomes** the client and confirms what happens next.
- It **collects** the information and files you need, all at once.
- It **sets expectations**: how you communicate, how feedback works, and what is and is not included.

Most freelancers do this again and again by hand, with slightly different emails each time. That is a perfect task for AI, because it is repeated and text-heavy. You create three reusable pieces once, then adapt them for each client in a few minutes:

1. **A welcome email**: thanks, a short summary of the project, the next three steps, your working hours and how to reach you.
2. **An onboarding questionnaire** of 8 to 10 questions: the information you always need, such as goals, audience, deadlines, access to accounts, examples they like, and who gives final approval.
3. **A project checklist**: every step from kick-off to handover, with who does what.

AI helps you in two ways. First, it drafts the pieces quickly. Second, and more usefully, it can **find gaps**. Ask it: "What questions do freelancers in my trade often forget to ask at the start of a project?" You decide which suggestions are relevant.

Keep the templates in one place, such as a folder or a document, with blanks such as [CLIENT NAME] and [START DATE]. Never paste a client's completed questionnaire into an AI tool if it contains personal or confidential information. If you want help summarising answers, remove names and sensitive details first.

**Analogy:** Onboarding is like giving a guest clear directions before they arrive at your home. If you tell them the address, where to park and which door to use, they arrive calm and on time. If you do not, they phone you three times from the street, and the visit starts badly for both of you.

## Worked Example
Nadia is a freelance online maths tutor in Kraków, Poland. She works with parents of secondary school students. She used to start every new student with a long email thread about times, topics and exam dates.

She asks Claude:

> "I am an online maths tutor for secondary school students. Draft a reusable onboarding pack: (1) a warm welcome email to a parent, under 150 words, with placeholders; (2) a questionnaire of 10 questions for the parent and student; (3) a checklist of steps from the first lesson to the first progress report. Use clear, simple English."

The first draft is useful. Then she asks: "What do tutors often forget to ask at the start?" The AI suggests questions about the student's exam board, any learning support needs, and the cancellation policy. Nadia adds the exam question and a polite question about learning support, worded so the parent can share only what they want. She adds her cancellation policy to the welcome email herself, because it is her business rule, not something the AI should decide.

She saves the three pieces with placeholders in one folder. Starting with a new student now takes her about 15 minutes instead of several emails over a week.

## Common Mistake
Many freelancers let AI create a very long questionnaire of 25 or 30 questions, because more questions seem more professional. Clients do not finish long forms, or they answer quickly and badly. Keep only the questions you truly need to start the work, about 8 to 10. You can ask the rest later, at the moment they become relevant.

## Key Takeaways
1. Onboarding welcomes the client, collects everything you need at once and sets clear expectations.
2. Use AI to draft a reusable welcome email, questionnaire and checklist, and to suggest questions you may have forgotten.
3. Keep your own business rules, such as cancellation or payment terms, in your hands, and never paste clients' personal answers into AI tools.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Create a reusable onboarding pack with AI: a welcome email, a questionnaire of 8 to 10 questions and a project checklist. Adapt all three to your own service.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier); Google Docs, Google Forms or any word processor (free).
**Steps:**
1. Adapt the prompt from the Worked Example to your own trade and type of client.
2. Ask the AI: "What do freelancers in my trade often forget to ask at the start of a project?" Add only the relevant suggestions.
3. Cut the questionnaire to 8 to 10 questions. For each one, ask yourself: "Do I need this answer before I can start?"
4. Add your own business rules to the welcome email: working hours, response time, payment or cancellation terms.
5. Replace client-specific details with placeholders such as [CLIENT NAME] and [DEADLINE].
6. Optional: turn the questionnaire into a free online form. You will connect a form to an automation in L10.
7. Save all three pieces in one folder called "Onboarding pack".
**What good looks like:** A welcome email under 150 words with clear next steps, a questionnaire of 8 to 10 necessary questions, and a checklist that covers the project from start to handover. All three use placeholders and include your own rules.
**Time:** about 35 minutes
