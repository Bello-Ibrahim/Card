## Hook
Every missed appointment is a lost chance to care for someone, and an empty slot another patient could have used. A model can predict who is likely to miss a visit. The harder question is what you do with that prediction.

## Explanation
Operational AI supports how a hospital or clinic runs. Common uses are:

- **Scheduling:** matching appointment slots to demand and staff time.
- **Missed-appointment prediction:** estimating which bookings are at higher risk of a no-show.
- **Bed management:** forecasting admissions and discharges to plan capacity.
- **Supply planning:** forecasting demand for medicines, tests and equipment.
- **Documentation support:** drafting letters or summaries that clinicians review and sign (see L08).

These tools do not make clinical decisions, but they still affect patients. A prediction becomes harmful when it is used in an unfair way. Suppose a model finds that people who live far away miss more appointments. One clinic might use this to **overbook** their slots, so that if they do arrive, they wait longer or are sent home. Another clinic might use the same finding to offer **transport support or a phone consultation**. Same prediction, opposite effect on equity.

A second point: in operational data, a factor that is linked with missed appointments is not proof that it causes them. Distance may stand in for cost, work hours or poor transport. Staff who know the community should interpret the pattern before any action is chosen.

**Analogy:** A missed-appointment model is like a weather forecast. A forecast of rain does not tell you to cancel the market; it tells you to bring shelter. A good clinic uses a risk forecast to prepare support, not to shut people out.

## Common Mistake
Many teams treat a risk score as a decision. They see "high risk of no-show" and automatically overbook, move or cancel the booking. This shifts the cost onto patients who already face barriers. Decide the action with staff and, where possible, patients, and check whether the action reduces or increases differences between groups.

## Key Takeaways
1. Operational AI supports scheduling, missed-appointment prediction, bed and supply planning, and documentation that clinicians review.
2. A prediction is not a cause, and it is not a decision; staff who know the community should interpret it and choose the action.
3. Use operational predictions to offer support, such as reminders or transport help, not to penalise groups who already face barriers.
