# L14 Capstone: Writing the AI-Assisted Design Case Study

Course: AI-23 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O6, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Two designers show the same polished app. One says, "I used AI." The other shows exactly where AI helped, where she overruled it and which risks she checked. Which one would you hire?

## Explanation
Your capstone is a brand identity and a key app screen flow, designed with an AI-assisted process and documented as a case study. You have already built most of it: guidelines (L10), screens (L11), prototype (L12), and checklist and asset log (L13). Now you tell the story.

A case study is not a gallery. It shows how you think. Use this template, laid out as a Figma page or a set of frames:

1. **Problem.** The client or project, the audience and the goal, in 3 to 4 sentences. Include one success measure from your brief.
2. **Process.** The main stages, from research to prototype, with 1 or 2 images each. Show drafts and dead ends, not only the final result.
3. **AI use log.** A table of where you used AI: stage, tool, what you asked for, what you kept, what you changed or rejected. Include at least one case where you **overruled** AI, and why.
4. **Decisions.** The 3 to 5 most important design decisions and the reasons: why this name, this direction, this palette, this flow. Include the risk decisions from your asset log: which assets are clear, which need checks and which you removed.
5. **Outcomes.** The final brand identity and key screen flow, a link to the prototype, what the one-person test found and what you changed. Add what you would do next and what you learned.

**Be honest and specific about AI.** Clients and employers increasingly ask how work was made. [VERIFY] Vague statements such as "AI-powered design" are not useful, and hiding AI use can damage trust. Specific statements are useful: "Claude grouped anonymised interview notes into themes; I verified every quote and removed one theme." Never claim a legal result you did not get: write "trademark search recommended", not "trademark cleared". [REGION]

Protect data in the case study too. Use anonymised research, no participant names or faces without consent, and no confidential client details unless the client approved publication.

**Analogy:** A case study is like a cooking programme, not a restaurant photo. The photo shows the dish. The programme shows the ingredients, the steps, the mistakes and the choices, so the viewer trusts that the cook can do it again.

## Worked Example
Fatima Bello is a brand and product designer in Lagos, Nigeria. For her capstone, she creates a fictional drinking-water delivery brand for apartment buildings.

Her AI use log includes:

| Stage | Tool | Asked for | Kept | Changed or rejected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Research | Claude | Themes from 10 anonymised notes | 4 themes | Removed 1 theme with a changed quote |
| Naming | Claude | 20 names in 4 categories | "Tidewell" shortlisted | Rejected 2 names with negative meanings in Yoruba |
| Moodboards | Image generator | 3 directions | Direction B | Removed images with text artefacts |
| Logo | Image generator, Figma | 10 concepts | Wave-and-drop idea | Full vector redraw; no traced shapes |
| UI | Figma AI features | Draft order screen | Layout structure | Rebuilt with components; fixed contrast |
| Microcopy | Claude | Error messages | 3 lines, edited | Rejected a message that blamed the user |

Her "Decisions" section explains why she overruled Claude's suggested bright blue palette: two text pairs failed contrast and the colour looked too close to many existing water brands. Her "Outcomes" section links the prototype and reports that her tester could not find the delivery time; she moved it to the confirm screen. The asset log shows the logo as "needs check: trademark search recommended".

## Common Mistake
Many designers write a case study only about the final visuals, or they list AI tools without saying what the tools did. Both hide your judgement, which is the most valuable part. Show the process and the decisions, including the moments when you rejected AI output.

## Key Takeaways
1. Structure the case study as problem, process, AI use log, decisions and outcomes.
2. Be honest and specific about AI use, including where you overruled it, and never claim legal clearance you do not have.
3. Protect data in the case study by using anonymised research and only publishing client details with approval.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 5: write and lay out a case study in Figma covering your brand identity, key screen flow and AI-assisted process.
**Tools:** Figma (free plan); Claude (free plan) for feedback on clarity; your work from L02 to L13.
**Steps:**
1. Create a "Case Study" page with 5 sections: problem, process, AI use log, decisions, outcomes.
2. Write the problem in 3 to 4 sentences with one success measure.
3. Add process images, including at least one draft or rejected direction.
4. Build the AI use log table, with at least one case where you overruled AI.
5. Write 3 to 5 decisions with reasons, including risk decisions from your asset log.
6. Add the final brand, key screens, a prototype link and your test result.
7. Ask Claude to point out unclear sentences only, and edit them yourself.
8. Check the rubric and submission checklist before you submit.
**What good looks like:** A clear case study that a client could read in 5 minutes, with specific AI use, honest risk status and visible design decisions.
**Time:** about 60 minutes

## Review Flags
- [VERIFY] The statement that clients and employers increasingly ask how work was made is general and should be confirmed or softened before scripting.
- [REGION] Trademark clearance and rules on publishing research or client material differ by country; the lesson asks learners not to claim legal results they do not have.
- The name "Tidewell" in the worked example is fictional; the Yoruba meaning check is hypothetical.
