# L07 Moodboards and Visual Direction with Image Generators

Course: AI-23 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O3 · Video: 5 min (screen demo)

## Hook
A client says, "We want it to feel warm but modern." What does that look like? In this lesson you will show three different answers on screen in less time than it takes to search a stock library.

## Explanation
A moodboard sets the visual direction before any logo or screen exists. Image generators make moodboards faster and more specific, because you can create images for the exact mood in your brief instead of searching for something close.

**Prompt with visual qualities, not names.** Describe what you want to see:

- **Materials and textures:** raw clay, brushed aluminium, recycled paper, woven cotton.
- **Colour:** "muted terracotta and sage green", "high-contrast black with one electric blue accent".
- **Light:** soft morning window light, hard midday shadows, neon at night.
- **Era and context:** "1970s domestic interior", "contemporary market stall".
- **Composition:** close-up, flat lay, wide shot, a lot of empty space.

Never prompt with the name of a living artist, a photographer or an existing brand. It is unfair to the people whose work is copied, it can create rights problems, and it makes your direction look borrowed. Qualities give you better control anyway.

**Create contrast, not variations.** One direction with 18 images tells the client nothing. Three clearly different directions for the same brief start a real conversation. Change one or two big variables between directions (for example, the light and the era) and keep the brief the same.

**Know the tool's limits.** Free image generators differ in daily limits, image quality, resolution, whether images are public by default and what the terms allow for commercial use. These limits and terms change often. [VERSION] Read the current terms before you show images to a client, and label moodboards as "AI-generated reference, not final artwork".

**Analogy:** A moodboard prompt is like a recipe written by ingredient, not by restaurant. "Tastes like that famous restaurant" gives the cook nothing to work with and copies someone else. "Smoked paprika, charred peppers, lime" describes the result you want, and the dish becomes yours.

## Worked Example
Diego Hernández is a brand designer in Oaxaca, Mexico. His hypothetical client is a small ceramics studio that sells tableware online. The brief says: "handmade, calm, contemporary, for young professionals setting up their first home."

The presenter follows these steps on screen. Interface labels may differ in the tool the reviewer chooses. [VERSION]

1. Open the free image generator in a browser and sign in. [VERSION]
2. Check the settings for image visibility (public or private) and the aspect ratio. Choose a square format for moodboard tiles. [VERSION]
3. Type the Direction A prompt: "Handmade ceramic bowls on a pale oak table, soft morning window light, muted terracotta and cream, a lot of empty space, calm contemporary kitchen, close-up photograph."
4. Generate a set of images. Keep the 2 strongest and note why.
5. Change the prompt to vary the subject but keep the direction: "Hands shaping raw clay, same light and palette." Generate and keep 2 more.
6. Type the Direction B prompt: "Glazed ceramic plates in deep cobalt and white, hard midday shadows, bright outdoor courtyard, bold geometric composition."
7. Type the Direction C prompt: "Matte charcoal ceramics on dark slate, a single warm lamp at night, quiet and minimal, wide shot."
8. Generate 6 images for each direction and download them. [VERSION]
9. Open Figma, create three frames named "A: Morning Studio", "B: Courtyard Colour" and "C: Evening Quiet", and place 6 images in each.
10. Under each frame, write one sentence on the idea and add the label "AI-generated reference".

The client sees three clearly different answers to "warm but modern", and chooses Direction A with the cobalt accent from B.

## Common Mistake
Many designers generate dozens of images from one prompt and call it a moodboard. The result shows one idea many times. Another mistake is to prompt "in the style of" a well-known artist or brand to get a quick result. Describe materials, colour, light and era instead; it is more ethical and gives you more control.

## Key Takeaways
1. Prompt image generators with visual qualities such as materials, colour, light, era and composition, never with names of living artists or existing brands.
2. Create three contrasting directions for the same brief by changing one or two big variables, not many small variations.
3. Free image generators change their limits and terms often; check them and label moodboard images as AI-generated references.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Create 3 contrasting moodboards of 6 images each for your capstone brand, and write one sentence on the idea behind each.
**Tools:** A free image generator (chosen by your course page) [VERSION]; Figma (free plan).
**Steps:**
1. Read your brief and choose two big variables to change between directions, such as light and era.
2. Write 3 prompts using materials, colour, light, era and composition. Do not name any artist or brand.
3. Check the tool's visibility and usage terms. [VERSION]
4. Generate images for each direction and keep the 6 strongest per direction.
5. In Figma, create 3 frames, place the images and name each direction.
6. Write one sentence on the idea behind each board and label it "AI-generated reference".
7. Save your prompts in a text file for your case study.
**What good looks like:** Three boards that a client can tell apart at a glance, each with a clear name, a one-sentence idea, saved prompts and no artist or brand names.
