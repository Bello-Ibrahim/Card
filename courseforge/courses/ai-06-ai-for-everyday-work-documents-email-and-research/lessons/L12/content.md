# L12 Measure Time Saved and Present Your Redesign

Course: AI-06 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O6, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
You may have heard claims that AI will save you many hours every week. Maybe it will, and maybe it will not. The only honest way to know is to measure your own work, including the time you spend checking what the AI produced.

## Explanation
Real time savings differ from person to person and from task to task. A fast writer may save little on emails but a lot on summaries. A task with many facts may need so much checking that AI saves no time at all. That is why this course makes no general claims. You measure your own.

**How to measure honestly:**

1. **Before time:** how long the task took the usual way. You recorded this in L10.
2. **After time:** how long the task takes with AI. Include **all** of it: writing or filling in the prompt, waiting, reading the output, checking it with the 4-step checklist, editing and correcting.
3. **Time saved:** before time minus after time. It can be zero or negative. That is a useful result, not a failure. It tells you that this task, or this step, is better done without AI.

Measure more than once if you can. The first time with a new prompt is usually slower.

**Time is not the only result.** Also judge **quality** (was the result as good as, or better than, your usual work?) and **safety** (did you follow the rules on data and your employer's AI policy?). [REGION] A task that is faster but less accurate is not an improvement.

**Review every output with the checklist from L04:** facts and numbers, names and dates, tone for the reader, and confidential content added or lost. Record what you found and fixed.

**Analogy:** Measuring time saved is like testing a new route to work. You do not trust someone else's claim that it is faster. You time the old route and the new route yourself, on normal days, and include the time to find parking. Then you decide.

## Worked Example
Lucía is a hypothetical office administrator at an accounting firm in Santiago, Chile. She redesigned three weekly tasks. Her results:

| Task | Before | After (incl. checking) | Saved | Quality | Checks found |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly team update | 40 min | 22 min | 18 min | Same, clearer headings | 1 wrong date |
| Summary of a public tax guidance page for colleagues | 50 min | 35 min | 15 min | Good after corrections | 1 changed condition |
| Replies to routine client questions | 30 min | 32 min | -2 min | Same | Tone too formal each time |

Her third task was slower with AI, because each reply needed a lot of editing to sound personal. She decides to keep doing those replies herself and to use AI only to check them for clarity. She also notes her safety rules: client names and figures were never pasted; she used her firm's approved tool for internal notes. [REGION]

**The capstone.** Your capstone brings together L10, L11 and this lesson. You submit:

- your 3 reusable prompts from L11, with first and improved versions;
- a results table like Lucía's, with before and after times, quality notes and checklist findings;
- a one-page summary of the time saved, the quality of the results and the safety rules you followed, with your decision for each task: keep, change or stop using AI.

## Common Mistake
The most common mistake is timing only the moment the AI writes the answer, and forgetting the prompt writing, checking and editing. This makes AI look much faster than it really is. Another mistake is hiding tasks where AI did not help. The correction: time the whole task from start to finish, and report every result honestly, including zero or negative savings.

## Key Takeaways
1. Measure time saved yourself: before time minus after time, and include the time spent writing prompts, checking and editing.
2. Judge quality and safety as well as time, and review every output with the 4-step checklist.
3. A zero or negative result is useful: it tells you where AI does not help, so you can decide to keep, change or stop.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 3: do each of your 3 redesigned tasks with AI, time them, review each output with the checklist from L04, and write a one-page summary of the time saved, the quality of the results and the safety rules you followed.
**Tools:** Your prompt library from L11; Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) or your employer's approved AI tool; a timer; a spreadsheet or word processor.
**Steps:**
1. Do each of your 3 tasks with your reusable prompts, following your safety notes from L10.
2. Time each task from start to finish, including prompt writing, checking and editing.
3. Review each output with the 4-step checklist and record what you found and fixed.
4. Build a results table: task, before time, after time, time saved, quality note and checklist findings.
5. For each task, decide: keep using AI, change the method, or stop using AI. Give one reason.
6. Write a one-page summary covering time, quality, safety rules followed and your decisions.
**What good looks like:** A complete results table with honest times for all 3 tasks, including checking time, a checklist record for each output, and a clear one-page summary with a decision and reason for each task. Any zero or negative results are reported.
**Time:** about 40 minutes, plus the time to do your 3 tasks
