# L09 Organising Your Work: Plans, Lists and Templates | Presenter Script

Course: AI-06 · Video: 5 min · Words: 686

## Hook
It is Monday morning. You have fifteen things to do, some urgent, some large, and some you have avoided for weeks. An AI assistant can turn that list into a neat plan in seconds. But only you know which task really cannot be late.

## Explain
This is week three, where we redesign your work week. In this lesson, AI helps you organise your work in three ways. First, it can turn a messy list into a plan. It can group similar tasks, suggest an order, and spread the work across the days.

Second, it can suggest checklists for tasks with many small steps, such as preparing a meeting or welcoming a new team member. You add the steps it missed and remove the ones that do not apply.

Third, it can build reusable templates for tasks you repeat, such as a weekly status update or a meeting agenda. The template has headings and spaces to fill in, so you do not start from a blank page each week.

Here is a useful planning prompt. It asks the assistant to group the tasks, suggest a priority order, and spread them across the week, with your available hours per day. It also asks for a table with a column called why this order.

That why column matters. It shows the assistant's reasoning, so you can see where it guessed. Remember, it does not know your deadlines, your manager's priorities, or which task depends on another team. It may put an easy task first because it looks quick.

So here is the rule for this lesson. AI can suggest an order, but you decide the priorities.

Think of a planning partner who arranges the pieces on the table. They put the pieces in a neat order quickly. But you know which pieces matter most, because you know the deadlines and the people.

And before you paste any task list, replace the names of clients, colleagues and projects with general labels, such as client A report or project X. As always, follow your employer's AI policy.

## Demonstrate
Let's see an example. Oksana is a programme assistant at a non-profit organisation in Lviv, in Ukraine. Her list for next week has twelve tasks, including a report for a donor, booking a venue for volunteer training, and the Friday team meeting agenda.

She replaces the donor's real name with donor A, and pastes the list with the planning prompt. The AI puts update contact list on Monday morning, because it is quick and easy. It puts the donor report on Thursday.

Oksana changes two things and writes down why. First, she moves the donor report to Monday and Tuesday. The AI did not know that her manager must review it on Wednesday, before it goes to the donor on Thursday.

Second, she moves the venue booking to Monday. Venues in her city book up quickly at this time of year, and she knows the training date cannot change.

Then she asks for a reusable agenda template for the Friday team meeting, with headings for updates, decisions and actions. She edits it once and saves it to use every week.

A common mistake is to accept the AI's plan because it looks organised. But a neat plan is not the same as a correct plan. Read the why behind each choice, change what conflicts with what you know, and write down your reason.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, AI can turn a messy list into a weekly plan, suggest checklists and draft reusable templates. Second, the assistant does not know your deadlines, dependencies or people, so you decide the final priorities. Third, remove client, colleague and project names before pasting a task list, and ask the AI to explain its order so you can see where it guessed.
