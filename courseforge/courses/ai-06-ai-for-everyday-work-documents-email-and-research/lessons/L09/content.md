# L09 Organising Your Work: Plans, Lists and Templates

Course: AI-06 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O3, O5 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
It is Monday morning. You have a list of 15 things to do, some urgent, some large, some you have been avoiding for weeks. An AI assistant can turn that list into a neat plan in seconds. But only you know which task will cause real problems if it is late.

## Explanation
AI assistants can help you organise work in three ways.

**1. Turning a messy list into a plan.** Paste your task list (with sensitive details removed) and ask for a prioritised plan for the week. The assistant can group similar tasks, suggest an order and spread the work across the days.

**2. Creating checklists.** For a task with many small steps, such as preparing a meeting or starting a new team member, AI can suggest a checklist. You then add the steps it missed and remove those that do not apply.

**3. Building reusable templates.** For tasks you repeat, such as a weekly status update or a meeting agenda, AI can draft a template with headings and spaces to fill in. You reuse it each week and save thinking time.

Here is a useful planning prompt:

```text
Here is my task list for next week. Group similar tasks, suggest a priority order and spread them across Monday to Friday. I have about 5 hours per day for this work. Show it as a table with Day, Task and Why this order.
```

Asking for "Why this order" is important. It shows you the assistant's reasoning, so you can see where it guessed.

The assistant does not know your deadlines, your manager's priorities, which colleague is waiting for you, or which task depends on another team. It may put an easy task first because it looks quick, or place a task on a day you are on leave. **AI can suggest an order, but you decide the priorities.**

Before pasting a task list, replace names of clients, colleagues and projects with general labels, such as "Client A report" or "Project X". Follow your employer's AI policy. [REGION]

**Analogy:** An AI assistant in planning is like a planning partner who arranges the pieces on the table. It can put the pieces in a neat order quickly. But you know which pieces matter most, because you know the deadlines and the people. You make the final decisions.

## Worked Example
Oksana is a hypothetical programme assistant at a non-profit organisation in Lviv, Ukraine. Her task list for next week has 12 items, including "donor report (Donor A)", "book venue for volunteer training", "update contact list", "reply to partner emails" and "prepare Friday team meeting agenda".

She removes the donor's real name and pastes the list with the planning prompt. The AI's plan puts "update contact list" on Monday morning because it is "quick and easy", and the donor report on Thursday.

Oksana changes two things and writes down why:

1. **She moves the donor report to Monday and Tuesday.** The AI did not know that her manager must review it on Wednesday before it goes to the donor on Thursday.
2. **She moves the venue booking to Monday.** Venues in her city book up quickly in this season, and she knows the training date cannot change.

Then she asks the AI for a reusable agenda template for the Friday team meeting, with headings for updates, decisions and actions. She edits it once and saves it to use every week.

## Common Mistake
Many people accept the AI's plan as it is, because it looks organised and logical. But a neat plan is not the same as a correct plan. The assistant cannot see dependencies, people or deadlines you did not tell it. The correction: always read the "why" behind each choice, change anything that conflicts with what you know, and write down your reason, so you learn what to include in the prompt next time.

## Key Takeaways
1. AI can turn a messy list into a weekly plan, suggest checklists and draft reusable templates.
2. The assistant does not know your deadlines, dependencies or people, so you decide the final priorities.
3. Remove client, colleague and project names before pasting a task list, and ask the AI to explain its order so you can see where it guessed.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Paste a list of your real tasks for next week (with sensitive details removed) into a free AI assistant, ask for a prioritised plan, then change at least 2 of its choices and write down why.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier); a notes app, spreadsheet or calendar.
**Steps:**
1. Write a list of 8 to 15 tasks for next week.
2. Replace names of clients, colleagues and projects with general labels, such as "Client A" or "Project X". Remove any confidential details.
3. Paste the list into a free AI assistant with the planning prompt from this lesson, including your available hours per day.
4. Read the plan and the reason for each choice.
5. Change at least 2 choices that do not match your real deadlines, dependencies or priorities. Write one sentence for each change explaining why.
6. Optional: ask for a reusable template for one task you repeat every week.
**What good looks like:** A weekly plan with at least 2 changes you made, each with a clear reason based on information the AI did not have, such as "My manager needs this report on Wednesday". No real names or confidential details were pasted.
**Time:** about 20 minutes
