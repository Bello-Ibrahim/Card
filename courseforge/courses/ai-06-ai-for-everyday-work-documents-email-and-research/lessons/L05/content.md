# L05 Drafting and Replying to Emails

Course: AI-06 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O3 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Some emails take two minutes to write. Others take thirty: the reminder to a late supplier, the reply to a long thread, the message where every word matters. AI can help you start those difficult emails faster, as long as the final version still sounds like you.

## Explanation
AI assistants can help with three common email jobs.

**1. Drafting a new email from notes.** Write your main points as short bullets and let the assistant turn them into full sentences. Your bullets hold the facts, so the AI does not need to guess them.

**2. Replying to a long thread.** You can ask the assistant to list the open questions in a thread and draft a reply that answers each one. Before you paste a thread, remove names, email addresses, signatures and anything confidential, and check your employer's AI policy. [REGION] If your organisation provides an approved AI tool inside your email system, it may be the better choice for this job. [VERSION]

**3. Changing tone.** The same message can be more formal, friendlier, shorter or more direct. Useful follow-up requests include:

```text
Make this more formal for a senior manager.
Make this friendlier but keep it short.
Make this more direct. The deadline must be clear in the first sentence.
```

A useful habit is to ask for **two versions in different tones** and then combine the best parts yourself. This gives you choices instead of one answer you must accept or reject.

After the AI drafts, you **edit**. Check it with the 4-step checklist from L04: facts and numbers, names and dates, tone for the reader, and confidential content. Then make it sound like you. AI drafts often use phrases that you would never say, such as very formal openings or too many polite words. Your reader knows how you write, so change those phrases.

**Analogy:** Using AI for email is like dictating rough notes to a skilled assistant who types them up neatly. The typed letter looks professional, but you still read it, correct it and sign it. If it does not sound like you, you change it before it goes out.

## Worked Example
Carlos is a hypothetical purchasing assistant in São Paulo, Brazil. A supplier has not delivered an order of office chairs that was due last week. He needs a reminder that is polite but firm.

He writes his points (with the supplier's name removed):

```text
Write a reminder email to a supplier. Points:
- Order of 40 office chairs was due last Monday, not delivered
- We need them before our new team starts on the 1st of next month
- Please confirm a delivery date by Thursday
Give me two versions: one very polite, one polite but firm. About 100 words each.
```

The **very polite** version is kind but weak: the Thursday deadline is in the last line and easy to miss. The **firm** version puts the deadline first but sounds cold: "Failure to respond will be escalated."

Carlos combines them. He keeps the friendly opening from the first version and the clear deadline from the second. He removes "Failure to respond will be escalated", because he has worked with this supplier for years and would never write that. He adds the supplier's name and the order number himself, inside his company email system.

Then he checks the facts: 40 chairs, due last Monday, confirmation by Thursday. All correct. The final email is clear, firm and sounds like Carlos.

## Common Mistake
Many people send the AI draft with only small changes. The result is an email that is correct but does not sound like the sender. Colleagues and customers notice, and it can make the message feel cold or strange. Another mistake is pasting a full email thread, with everyone's names and contact details, into a free tool. The correction is to remove personal details before you paste, and always to edit the final version in your own words.

## Key Takeaways
1. Give the AI your key points as bullets, so the facts come from you and not from its guesses.
2. Ask for two versions in different tones, then combine the best parts and edit them so they sound like you.
3. Remove names and personal details before pasting any email or thread, follow your employer's AI policy, and check the final email before you send it.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Write 3 bullet points for a real email you need to send, ask for two versions in different tones, then combine the best parts and edit the result in your own words.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier); your usual email program for the final version.
**Steps:**
1. Choose a real email you need to send this week, such as a reminder, a request or an update.
2. Write 3 bullet points with the key facts. Replace names with roles, such as "the supplier" or "my manager", and remove any confidential details.
3. Ask a free AI assistant for two versions in different tones, for example "friendly" and "formal", of about 100 words each.
4. Mark the best sentence or phrase in each version.
5. Combine the best parts into one email and rewrite any phrase you would never say.
6. Check the result with the 4-step checklist from L04, then add the real names in your own email program.
**What good looks like:** Your 3 bullet points, the two AI versions, and a final email that keeps all your facts, uses your own words and passes all 4 checklist steps. No names or confidential details were pasted into the AI tool.
**Time:** about 20 minutes
