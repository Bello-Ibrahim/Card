# L06 Writing and Editing Documents

Course: AI-06 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O3 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
If you ask an AI assistant to "write our new safety procedure", it will give you three pages in seconds. They will look professional. They will also be full of steps that your workplace does not use. There is a better way to work.

## Explanation
AI assistants can help with two kinds of document work.

**Drafting:** first versions of reports, procedures, briefings, meeting agendas and job descriptions.

**Editing:** making your own text clearer, shorter, better organised or easier for non-experts to read. For editing, the facts come from your text, so the risk of invented content is lower. You must still check that the meaning has not changed.

For drafting, the key idea is to **build the document step by step** instead of asking for everything at once:

1. **Outline first.** Ask for a list of headings and short notes on what each section should cover.
2. **Improve the outline yourself.** Remove sections that do not apply, add what is missing, and put the steps in the order your team really follows. This is where your knowledge matters most.
3. **Draft one section at a time.** Give the assistant the facts for that section and ask it to write it.
4. **Edit each section.** Check facts, remove anything invented, and adjust the words for your readers.

Working this way keeps you in control. Each step is small enough to check properly.

Useful editing prompts include:

```text
Rewrite this paragraph in plain English for new staff with no technical background. Keep all the facts.
Make this section 30% shorter without removing any steps.
List any sentences in this text that are unclear or could be read in two ways.
```

As always, do not paste confidential documents into free tools. Follow your employer's AI policy, which may name an approved tool for internal documents. [REGION]

**Analogy:** Building a document with AI is like working with a builder from a plan. You would not say "build me a house" in one sentence and accept whatever appears. You agree the plan first, then build one room at a time, and inspect each room before moving on.

## Worked Example
Aigerim is a hypothetical office coordinator at an engineering company in Almaty, Kazakhstan. Her manager asks her to write a one-page procedure for new staff on how to request office equipment.

She starts with an outline:

```text
Create an outline for a one-page procedure for new office staff: how to request equipment such as a laptop, monitor or headset. Headings and one line per section only.
```

The AI suggests seven sections, including "Budget approval by the finance director" and "Vendor selection". Aigerim knows these do not apply: new staff choose from a fixed list and their team leader approves. She removes both, adds "Where to collect your equipment", and reorders the rest to match the real process. Her outline now has five sections.

She asks the AI to draft only the section "How to submit a request", giving it the facts: use the online form, choose from the standard list, and your team leader approves within two working days.

The draft adds "You will receive an email confirmation within one hour." Aigerim is not sure this is true, so she asks the IT team. It is not, so she deletes it. She then asks the AI to rewrite the section in shorter sentences for new staff, and checks that no step was lost.

## Common Mistake
The most common mistake is asking for the whole document in one prompt and then only lightly editing it. The long result hides invented steps, wrong details and sections that do not apply, and it is tiring to check. The correction is to work outline, then sections, then edit, and to check each part while it is still small.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use AI both for first drafts and for editing your own text to make it clearer, shorter or simpler.
2. Build documents step by step: outline, improve the outline yourself, draft one section, then edit.
3. Check every section for invented details and changed meaning, and keep confidential documents out of free tools.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Ask a free AI assistant for an outline of a one-page procedure you know well, improve the outline yourself, then have it draft one section and edit that section for accuracy.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier); a word processor such as Microsoft Word, Google Docs or LibreOffice Writer.
**Steps:**
1. Choose a simple procedure you know well, such as booking a meeting room or reporting an IT problem. Use only information that is not confidential.
2. Ask the AI for an outline with headings and one line per section.
3. Edit the outline yourself: delete, add and reorder sections so they match how the task is really done. Note each change.
4. Give the AI the facts for one section and ask it to draft only that section.
5. Check the draft line by line. Mark anything invented, wrong or unclear, and correct it.
6. Ask the AI to simplify the corrected section for a new colleague, then check again that no step was lost.
**What good looks like:** An improved outline with at least 2 changes you made and why, and one finished section that is accurate, clear and contains no invented steps.
**Time:** about 25 minutes
