# L02 Writing Clear Prompts: Task, Context, Format, Examples

Course: AI-06 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O1, O3 · Video: 5 min (screen demo)

## Hook
Two people ask the same AI assistant for "a notice about the office move". One gets a vague, generic text that needs rewriting. The other gets a draft she can send after a few small edits. The tool is the same. The difference is the prompt.

## Explanation
A **prompt** is the instruction you type into an AI assistant. The assistant only knows what is in your prompt (and in the conversation so far). If you leave out important details, it fills the gaps with guesses.

A clear prompt has four parts:

1. **Task:** what you want the assistant to do. Use a clear verb: write, rewrite, summarise, list, compare.
2. **Context:** the background it needs. Who is the reader? What is the situation? What are the key facts, such as dates and places?
3. **Format:** what the answer should look like. For example: an email of about 120 words, five bullet points, a table with two columns, or a friendly and simple tone.
4. **Examples:** a sample of what you want, if you have one. An old notice that worked well, or one sentence in the style you like, helps a lot.

You do not always need all four parts. A quick question may only need a task. But for anything you will send to other people, adding context and format usually gives a much better first draft.

You can also **continue the conversation**. If the first answer is too long, you can type "Make it shorter and remove the second paragraph." You do not need to start again.

**Analogy:** Writing a prompt is like briefing a temporary colleague who has just arrived. If you say only "do the notice", they will guess. If you tell them what the notice is about, who will read it, how long it should be and show them last year's notice, they can do good work the first time.

Remember the safety rule from L01: the context you give must not include confidential or personal data. Use general descriptions, such as "our team of 40 staff", instead of names and private details.

## Worked Example
Amara is a hypothetical office manager in Lagos, Nigeria. Her company is moving to a new floor, and she must tell staff.

**The vague prompt:**

```text
Write a notice about the office move.
```

The answer is long, very general and invents details, such as a "new city location", that are not true.

**The improved prompt, using the four parts:**

```text
Task: Write a notice to staff about our office move.
Context: We move from the 3rd floor to the 7th floor of the same building on Friday. Staff must pack their desks by Thursday at 4 p.m. Boxes are at reception.
Format: An email of about 120 words, friendly and clear, with the key dates in bold.
Example: Start like this: "Good news: we are moving up!"
```

The second answer uses the correct facts, the right length and a friendly tone. Amara checks the dates, changes one sentence so it sounds like her, and sends it.

**Screen demo steps for the presenter:**

1. Open a free AI assistant in a web browser and start a new chat. [VERSION]
2. Type the vague prompt and press Enter. Pause on the answer and point out the invented or missing details.
3. Open a second new chat, so the first answer does not influence the second.
4. Type the improved prompt, with each of the four parts on its own line. Press Enter.
5. Place the two answers side by side (two browser windows) and compare length, facts and tone.
6. In the second chat, type a follow-up: "Make it 20 words shorter." Show the new answer.

## Common Mistake
Many beginners write very short prompts and then decide that "AI is not useful" when the answer is generic. The assistant cannot read your mind or see your calendar. The correction: before you press Enter, ask yourself, "Would a new colleague understand this request?" If not, add context and format. The opposite mistake also happens: people paste in whole documents with private details as "context". Give only the facts that the task needs.

## Key Takeaways
1. A clear prompt has four parts: task, context, format and, if you have one, an example.
2. The more relevant context you give, the fewer guesses the assistant makes, but never include confidential or personal data.
3. You can improve an answer by continuing the conversation, for example "make it shorter" or "use a more formal tone".

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Rewrite one vague request using the four parts, and compare the two answers from a free AI assistant.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) in a web browser; a notes app.
**Steps:**
1. Open a free AI assistant and start a new chat. [VERSION]
2. Type this vague prompt: "Write a notice about the new parking rules." Save the answer.
3. Invent realistic details for your version (for example, the start date, which car park, who is affected). Do not use real names.
4. Open a new chat. Write a prompt with four labelled lines: Task, Context, Format and Example.
5. Save the second answer and compare the two. Note three differences in facts, length or tone.
6. Send one follow-up message to improve the second answer, such as "Use shorter sentences."
**What good looks like:** Two saved answers and a short note with three specific differences, for example "the second version had the correct start date and was half the length". The improved prompt includes all four parts and no personal data.
**Time:** about 20 minutes
