# L10 Map Your Tasks: Where AI Helps and Where It Does Not

Course: AI-06 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O5 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
"Use AI for everything" is not a plan. Some of your tasks will become faster with AI. Others will become slower, riskier or simply worse. The skill is knowing which is which, and that starts with looking closely at your own work.

## Explanation
Good tasks for AI usually have three features:

- **Repeated:** you do them every week or every month, so a good method pays back many times.
- **Text-based:** they involve writing, reading, rewording, summarising or organising words.
- **Low-risk:** a mistake would be easy to notice and fix before it causes harm.

Tasks that involve personal data, legal or financial decisions, sensitive conversations or judgement about people are poor candidates for AI, or need strict human control. Your employer's AI policy may also say which tasks and data are allowed. [REGION]

Most work tasks are not "all AI" or "no AI". They are made of **steps**. The key method in this lesson is to **break a task into steps and mark each step** with one of three labels:

- **AI can draft:** the AI can produce a first version, which you then check and edit.
- **AI can check:** you do the work, and the AI helps review it, for example for clarity, length or missing points.
- **Human only:** the step needs your judgement, your relationships, private data or a final decision.

Then **time the task as you do it now**. This gives you a "before" measurement for the capstone, so later you can compare honestly. Do not rely on general claims about how much time AI saves. Your own tasks, your own speed and your own checking time are what matter.

**Analogy:** Mapping a task is like reading a recipe before you cook with a helper. You decide which steps the helper can do, such as chopping and washing, which steps they can check, such as tasting the salt, and which steps you must do yourself, such as deciding when the dish is ready to serve.

## Worked Example
Fatima is a hypothetical school administrator in Casablanca, Morocco. Every week she writes a newsletter for parents. She maps it into six steps:

| Step | Label | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Collect news from teachers | Human only | Needs her relationships with staff |
| 2. Choose which items to include | Human only | Needs judgement about what parents need |
| 3. Write short paragraphs from teachers' notes | AI can draft | Repeated, text-based, low-risk after removing student names |
| 4. Make the language simple and friendly | AI can draft | Rewording is a strength of AI |
| 5. Check dates, times and event details | AI can check, then human | AI can list all dates for her to verify; she confirms each one |
| 6. Approve and send | Human only | Final responsibility is hers |

Three of her six steps (3, 4 and 5) are good candidates for AI support. Step 5 still needs her to confirm every date herself.

She also notes a safety rule for step 3: teachers' notes sometimes include student names, so she will remove them before pasting anything into a free tool, following the school's policy. [REGION]

Finally, she times the newsletter the usual way this week and records the result. This is her "before" time.

## Common Mistake
Many people choose their most difficult or most important task for AI, hoping for the biggest benefit. These tasks often need the most judgement and carry the most risk. Another mistake is labelling a whole task "AI" without breaking it into steps, which hides the steps where a human must decide. The correction: start with repeated, text-based, low-risk tasks, map every step, and keep human judgement where it belongs.

## Key Takeaways
1. Good tasks for AI are repeated, text-based and low-risk.
2. Break each task into steps and label each step "AI can draft", "AI can check" or "human only".
3. Time each task as you do it now, so you can measure your own results later instead of trusting general claims.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 1: choose 3 weekly tasks from your own job, map each task into steps, mark where AI can help, and time how long each task takes you now.
**Tools:** A spreadsheet or table in a word processor; a timer (on your phone or computer).
**Steps:**
1. List 5 to 8 tasks you do every week or every month.
2. Choose 3 that are repeated, text-based and low-risk. Avoid tasks built around personal data or sensitive decisions.
3. For each task, write every step in order, usually 4 to 8 steps.
4. Label each step "AI can draft", "AI can check" or "human only", with a one-line reason.
5. Note any safety rule for each task, such as "remove customer names before pasting".
6. Do each task the usual way this week and time it from start to finish. Record the minutes.
7. Save your table. You will use it in L11 and L12.
**What good looks like:** A table for each of 3 tasks with all steps labelled and explained, at least one "human only" step per task, a safety note for each task and a real "before" time in minutes.
**Time:** about 30 minutes, plus the time to do and time your 3 tasks during the week
