# L02 Writing a Short-Form Script | Presenter Script

Course: AI-21 · Video: 5 min · Words: 703

## Hook
Most viewers decide in the first few seconds whether to keep watching. If your first line is, hello, and welcome to our company, many of them are already gone.

## Explain
In the last lesson, you met the tools and the workflow. Now we start your project with stage two, the script. A good short script earns attention quickly, says one thing clearly, and tells the viewer what to do next.

A sixty to ninety second script has three parts. First, the hook, in the first three to five seconds. It is a question, a problem or a surprising picture. For example, still waiting three days for a bank transfer?

Second, one clear message in the middle. One idea, supported by two or three points. Not five features. One idea. Third, the call to action, in the last five to ten seconds. One thing the viewer should do, like download the app today.

Next, write for the ear. Viewers hear your script only once. So use short sentences, common words, and one idea per sentence. Avoid lists of numbers. One number per sentence is enough.

And write for the screen at the same time. Every line happens while the viewer is looking at something. A useful habit is a two-column script. On the left, what the viewer sees. On the right, what they hear. If a line has nothing good to show, it may not belong in the video.

How long is sixty seconds of script? Do not guess from the word count alone. A calm voice is slower, and pauses for visuals take time. Read it aloud with a timer. Your timer is the final judge.

A free chatbot can help you write a first draft quickly. But the draft is where your work starts, not where it ends. Chatbots often write too many words, use general phrases, and repeat ideas. Always edit by hand, and never paste confidential plans or personal data into a chatbot.

Think of a short script as a small suitcase. You cannot bring everything. So you choose the few things you really need, and you put the most important one where you can reach it first.

## Demonstrate
Let's see an example. Chukwuemeka is a marketer at a mobile payment app in Lagos, Nigeria. The app is made up for this lesson. He needs a sixty-second explainer for market traders who use cash today.

He asks a free chatbot for a draft. You can see his prompt on screen. It names the audience, the structure with a hook, one message and a call to action, a word limit, and a friendly, clear tone.

The draft has a hundred and ninety words and five features. Payments, savings, loans, airtime and bill payment. So he edits by hand. He keeps one message. Get paid instantly, without cash. He removes loans and savings. They can have their own videos.

He replaces a phrase like seamless financial experience with money arrives in seconds. Then he adds a visual note to each line. A trader counts coins while a customer waits. The voice asks, still counting change while customers walk away?

He reads it aloud with a timer. Seventy-one seconds. Too long. He cuts one example and a repeated sentence. Second reading, fifty-nine seconds, and a hundred and forty-one words.

The most common mistake is trying to say everything, and then speaking fast to make it fit. The result feels rushed, and nobody remembers anything. If your script is too long, do not speak faster. Cut ideas until one clear message remains.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, a short script has a hook in the first seconds, one clear message, and one call to action. Second, a timed read-aloud is the real test of length. Third, write for the ear and the screen at the same time, and always edit a chatbot draft by hand.
