# L02 Writing a Short-Form Script

Course: AI-21 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O2 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Most viewers decide in the first few seconds whether to keep watching. If your first line is "Hello, and welcome to our company", many of them are already gone. A good short script earns attention quickly, says one thing clearly, and tells the viewer what to do next.

## Explanation
A 60 to 90 second script has three parts:

1. **Hook (first 3 to 5 seconds):** a question, a problem or a surprising picture. "Still waiting three days for a bank transfer?"
2. **One clear message (the middle):** one idea, supported by two or three points. Not five features. One idea.
3. **Call to action (last 5 to 10 seconds):** one thing the viewer should do. "Download the app today."

**How long is 60 seconds of script?** A typical narration pace is about 140 words per minute [VERIFY]. So:

- 60 seconds is roughly 130 to 150 words.
- 90 seconds is roughly 195 to 225 words.

Treat these as ranges, not rules. A calm voice is slower, and pauses for visuals take time. Your timer is the final judge.

**Write for the ear.** Viewers hear the script once, so use short sentences, common words and one idea per sentence. Avoid lists of numbers; one number per sentence is enough.

**Write for the screen.** Every line of narration happens while the viewer is looking at something. A useful habit is a two-column script: on the left, what the viewer sees; on the right, what they hear. If a line has nothing good to show, it may not belong in the video.

**Using a chatbot.** A free chatbot can help you produce a first draft quickly. But the draft is where your work starts, not where it ends. Chatbots often write too many words, use general phrases, and repeat the same idea. Always edit by hand and read aloud. Do not paste confidential business plans, customer data or personal information into the chatbot.

**Analogy:** A short script is like a well-packed small suitcase. You cannot bring everything, so you choose the few items you really need, and you put the most important one where you can reach it first.

## Worked Example
Chukwuemeka is a marketer at a hypothetical mobile payment app in Lagos, Nigeria. He needs a 60-second explainer for new users.

He asks a free chatbot:

```text
Write a 60-second voice-over script for a mobile payment app in Nigeria.
Audience: market traders aged 25-45 who use cash today.
Structure: hook in the first 5 seconds, one message, one call to action.
Maximum 150 words. Short sentences. Friendly, clear tone.
```

The draft has 190 words and five features: payments, savings, loans, airtime and bill payment. Chukwuemeka edits by hand:

- He keeps one message: "Get paid instantly, without cash."
- He removes loans and savings. They can have their own videos.
- He replaces "seamless financial experience" with "money arrives in seconds".
- He adds a visual note to each line.

His two-column opening:

| Viewer sees | Viewer hears |
|---|---|
| A trader counts coins while a customer waits | "Still counting change while customers walk away?" |
| A phone screen shows "Payment received" | "Now your customers can pay you in seconds." |

He reads it aloud with a timer: 71 seconds. He cuts one example and a repeated sentence. Second reading: 59 seconds, 141 words.

## Common Mistake
The most common mistake is trying to say everything. Learners fit in every feature, every benefit and a company history, then speak fast to make it fit. The result feels rushed and nobody remembers anything. If your script is too long, do not speak faster. Cut ideas until one clear message remains.

## Key Takeaways
1. A short script has a hook in the first seconds, one clear message and one call to action.
2. At about 140 words per minute, 60 seconds is roughly 130 to 150 words, but a timed read-aloud is the real test.
3. Write for the ear and the screen at the same time, and always edit a chatbot draft by hand.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Write a 60-second script for a real or fictional product, then read it aloud with a timer and cut it to length.
**Tools:** Any free chatbot (optional); a document or notes app; a phone timer.
**Steps:**
1. Choose a product. You will use this project for the whole course, so pick something you can show in 8 to 12 shots. A fictional product is fine.
2. Write one sentence: who the video is for and the one message you want them to remember.
3. Write a first draft yourself, or ask a chatbot using the structure in the worked example. Do not include confidential or personal data.
4. Edit it into a two-column script: "Viewer sees" and "Viewer hears".
5. Check the hook: does something happen in the first 5 seconds?
6. Read the script aloud at a natural pace with a timer. Note the time and the word count.
7. Cut until it reads in 55 to 65 seconds. Save both versions.
**What good looks like:** A two-column script of roughly 130 to 150 words with a clear hook, one message and one call to action, which reads aloud in about 60 seconds. The "before" and "after" versions show what you cut.
**Time:** about 30 minutes
