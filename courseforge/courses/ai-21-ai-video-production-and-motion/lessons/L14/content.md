# L14 Capstone: Finishing and Presenting Your Video

Course: AI-21 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O5, O6, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
You have a script, a shot list, clips, an avatar, an edit, sound, two exports and a pre-publish checklist. The last step is not about adding more. It is about checking carefully, fixing what matters, and explaining how you made it.

## Explanation
**The capstone brief.** Produce a 60 to 90 second AI-assisted brand or explainer video from script to final export, with a half-page production note listing your tools, key prompts and pre-publish checks. The rubric on the course page shows how it is assessed.

**Templates.** If you want to rebuild or check your plan, use these templates.

Script template (60 to 90 seconds, about 130 to 225 words at a typical pace of about 140 words per minute [VERIFY]):

| Part | Time | Viewer sees | Viewer hears |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:00–0:05 | | |
| Message, point 1 | | | |
| Message, point 2 | | | |
| Message, point 3 | | | |
| Call to action | last 5–10 s | | |

Shot-list template:

| # | Time | Dur. | Shot type | Camera movement | Viewer sees | Narration | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Status is keep, trim, regenerate or replace (L10).

**The final quality pass.** Watch the whole video four times, each time for one thing:

1. **Artifacts:** hands, faces, melting objects, flicker, text, physics (L10).
2. **Continuity:** characters, clothes, colours and settings across shots (L08).
3. **Audio:** every word clear over music, on phone speakers (L12).
4. **Captions:** every line correct, inside the safe area (L11, L12).

Fix the most visible problems first. Then do the final export of both versions.

**The production note.** Half a page, with four parts: the tools you used and what each did; 2 or 3 key prompts, shown exactly; the main problems and how you solved them; and your pre-publish checks from L13.

**Analogy:** The production note is like the notes an engineer leaves with a finished machine. Someone else, or you in six months, can see what was used, how it was built and what was checked.

## Worked Example
Lan is a content creator in Da Nang, Vietnam. Her capstone is a 72-second explainer for a hypothetical bicycle-rental app.

In her final pass she finds: a wheel that bends for half a second in shot 7 (she trims it); the rider's helmet changing from white to grey between shots 4 and 5 (she replaces shot 5 with a close-up of the phone screen); music too loud under the call to action (she lowers it); and the caption "Da Nang" split badly over two lines (she edits it).

Her production note, in short:

- **Tools:** Flow for 4 text-to-video shots; Kling for 3 image-to-video shots from keyframes; HeyGen stock avatar for the 15-second intro; CapCut for edit, captions and export.
- **Key prompt:**

```text
A young woman in a white helmet rides a blue rental bicycle along a seaside road. Medium shot. Slow tracking shot beside her. Bright morning sun, soft shadows. Clean modern commercial style. 6 seconds.
```

- **Problems solved:** helmet drift fixed by replacing a shot; hands artifact fixed with "hands on handlebars, steady ride".
- **Checks:** AI disclosure line in description and end card; stock avatar only; no real people; music licence saved; commercial-use terms checked for each tool on her plan.

## Common Mistake
Many learners spend the last hours generating new clips to make the video "better", then run out of time for the quality pass and the production note. A slightly simpler video that is checked, clear and well documented is stronger than an ambitious one with visible artifacts and no notes. Stop generating early.

## Key Takeaways
1. The capstone is a 60 to 90 second video from script to final export, plus a half-page production note.
2. Do the final quality pass in four separate viewings: artifacts, continuity, audio and captions.
3. The production note lists tools, key prompts, problems solved and pre-publish checks, so others can understand how the video was made.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 4: export your final 60 to 90 second video and write a half-page production note with your tools, key prompts and pre-publish checks.
**Tools:** Your edited project in CapCut or your fallback editor [VERSION] [REGION]; your shot list, logs and checklist; a document for the note.
**Steps:**
1. Update your shot list using the template, with a status for every shot.
2. Watch the video four times: artifacts, continuity, audio, captions. Write down every problem.
3. Fix the most visible problems by trimming, regenerating within your limit, or replacing.
4. Check the final length is 60 to 90 seconds.
5. Export the final 16:9 and 9:16 versions [VERSION].
6. Write the production note: tools, 2 or 3 key prompts, problems solved and pre-publish checks.
7. Submit the video files, the note, your shot list and your pre-publish checklist.
**What good looks like:** A 60 to 90 second video with no obvious artifacts, consistent characters, clear audio and correct captions, in two formats, plus a half-page note that someone else could follow.
**Time:** about 45 minutes
