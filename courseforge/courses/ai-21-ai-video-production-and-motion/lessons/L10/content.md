# L10 Diagnosing Artifacts: Regenerate, Edit or Replace?

Course: AI-21 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O5 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
You now have a folder full of clips. Some are excellent, some are strange, and some are almost right. The skill in this lesson is not generating more clips. It is deciding, quickly and with reasons, what to do with the clips you have.

## Explanation
An **artifact** is a visual error caused by the generation process. The common ones are:

- **Warped hands and faces:** extra fingers, fingers that merge, eyes that move differently.
- **Melting or morphing objects:** a cup changes shape, a bicycle wheel bends.
- **Flicker:** light, colours or textures jump from frame to frame.
- **Unreadable text:** signs and labels with letters that do not form words.
- **Wrong physics:** water flowing upwards, people walking without moving forward, objects passing through each other.

**How to review.** Watch each clip at full size, at least twice. Then step through the problem areas slowly or pause at several points. Many artifacts only appear for a moment, but viewers notice them on a large screen.

**The decision guide.** For each clip, choose one of four actions:

| Action | When to choose it |
|---|---|
| **Keep** | No visible problems at normal speed. |
| **Trim** | The problem is only at the start or end, and the good part is long enough for the shot. |
| **Regenerate** | The problem runs through the clip, and a prompt change (less motion, simpler action, a keyframe) could fix it. |
| **Replace** | Several attempts failed, or the shot needs readable text. Use a simple graphic, a still image with a slow zoom, a licensed stock clip or your own footage. |

Also consider **crop**: reframing a clip in the editor can remove a problem at the edge of the picture, as long as the quality stays good enough.

Set a limit, such as 3 regenerations per shot. After that, replacing is usually faster and cheaper than trying again.

**Analogy:** Reviewing clips is like a doctor at a clinic deciding how to treat each patient. Some need nothing, some need a small treatment, some need a second test, and some need to see a specialist. The doctor does not give every patient the same treatment, and does not keep repeating a test that already failed three times.

## Worked Example
Priya is producing a hypothetical 70-second explainer about rooftop solar panels for a small installer in Pune, India. She reviews 6 clips:

| Shot | Problem | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wide shot of a city at sunrise; none | Keep | Clean at normal speed. |
| 2 | Technician's hand has six fingers for about 1 second in the middle | Regenerate | Problem is in the middle; she will change the prompt to "hands out of frame" and focus on the panel. |
| 3 | Panels flicker in the last 2 seconds | Trim | The first 4 seconds are clean and enough for the shot. |
| 4 | An electricity meter shows random symbols | Replace | Readable numbers need a simple animated graphic made in the editor. |
| 5 | A family on a roof; a child's arm passes through a railing | Regenerate, then replace | After 3 failed attempts she uses a still keyframe with a slow zoom. |
| 6 | A bird near the edge of the frame melts | Crop | A slight crop removes it without losing the main subject. |

She now knows exactly which shots need more work and which are ready for the edit.

## Common Mistake
Many learners either keep every clip because they spent credits on it, or regenerate every problem shot many times hoping for perfection. Both waste time. Use the decision guide, set a regeneration limit, and remember that a simple still or graphic is often the best choice for a difficult shot.

## Key Takeaways
1. Common artifacts are warped hands and faces, melting objects, flicker, unreadable text and wrong physics.
2. For each clip, decide to keep, trim, regenerate or replace, and write a reason.
3. Set a regeneration limit, and replace shots that need readable text or keep failing.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Review your generated clips with the checklist and mark each one keep, trim, regenerate or replace, with a reason.
**Tools:** Your clips from L06 to L09; a video player that can pause and step through frames; a table.
**Steps:**
1. Put all your clips in one folder, named by shot number.
2. Watch each clip at full size twice.
3. Check each clip for the five artifact types in this lesson, and pause at problem moments.
4. In a table, write the shot number, the problem, the decision and the reason.
5. For each "regenerate" decision, write the one prompt change you will try.
6. For each "replace" decision, write what you will use instead.
7. Keep the table for your capstone production note.
**What good looks like:** Every clip has a decision and a specific reason that names where the problem is and why the chosen action fits, with planned prompt changes for regenerations.
**Time:** about 25 minutes
