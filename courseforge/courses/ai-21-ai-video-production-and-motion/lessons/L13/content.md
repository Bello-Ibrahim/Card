# L13 Publishing Responsibly: Disclosure, Consent and Rights

Course: AI-21 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O6 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Your video looks finished. Before you press "publish", ask four questions. Would a viewer be misled? Did every real person agree? Do you have the right to use every piece? Does the platform allow it? A few minutes of checking can prevent a problem that is very hard to fix after publication.

## Explanation
This lesson gives **principles** for a pre-publish review. It is not legal advice. Rules for AI content differ by country and by platform, and they change often [VERIFY] [REGION] [VERSION]. Always check the current rules that apply to you.

**1. Disclosure.** Some platforms and some laws require creators to label AI-generated or AI-altered content, especially realistic content [VERIFY] [REGION] [VERSION]. Many platforms offer a label option when you upload [VERSION]. As a principle: if a viewer could think an AI-generated scene or person is real, label it. A short line in the description or on screen, such as "This video includes AI-generated scenes and an AI presenter", is a good habit even where it is not required. Do not remove watermarks from AI tools.

**2. Consent.** Every real person whose face or voice appears needs to have given clear consent for this use. This includes your own footage of staff or customers. Custom avatars or voice clones of real people need their clear, recorded consent [VERIFY] [REGION]. Stock avatars and fictional characters avoid this problem.

**3. Rights and tool terms.** Check that:

- Your music licence allows this use (L12) [VERIFY].
- Each AI tool's terms allow commercial use of what you generated on your plan. Free plans sometimes have different terms from paid plans [VERIFY].
- Images, logos and footage you uploaded are yours or licensed.

**4. Misleading content.** Avoid realistic scenes that could deceive: a fake customer testimonial, a product doing something it cannot do, a real place or event shown falsely, or a real person saying words they never said. Even with a label, these can harm viewers and your brand.

**Keep records.** Save your prompts, tool names, licences and consent forms in the project folder.

**Analogy:** A pre-publish review is like the safety check a pilot does before take-off. The pilot has flown many times, but still goes through the list every time, because a missed item is much harder to fix in the air.

## Worked Example
Here are three hypothetical cases.

**Case 1: Anna, a marketer in Kraków, Poland,** makes a video for a dental clinic. She generates a realistic "patient" saying the treatment was painless. **Problem:** it looks like a real testimonial but no patient said it. **Fix:** she removes it and uses a stock avatar clearly presented as the clinic's presenter, with an AI disclosure line.

**Case 2: Samir, a freelancer in Amman, Jordan,** uses a popular song in a hotel video, because "it is only 20 seconds". **Problem:** short use does not by itself make it allowed [VERIFY]. **Fix:** he replaces it with a royalty-free track whose licence allows commercial use, and saves the licence.

**Case 3: Mei Lin, a content creator in Penang, Malaysia,** films her colleague and wants to turn the footage into an avatar. **Problem:** the colleague agreed to be filmed, not to have an avatar made. **Fix:** she uses a stock avatar. If the colleague later wants an avatar, Mei Lin will get clear, recorded consent for that specific use and check local rules [VERIFY] [REGION].

## Common Mistake
Many learners think disclosure is only needed when a law requires it, or that a label solves every problem. A label does not replace consent, and it does not make a misleading scene acceptable. Use all four checks together: disclosure, consent, rights and misleading content.

## Key Takeaways
1. Label AI-generated or altered content where a viewer could think it is real, and check current platform and legal rules because they differ and change.
2. Every real person's face or voice needs their clear consent, and custom avatars or voice clones need clear, recorded consent.
3. Check music licences and tool terms for commercial use, avoid misleading realistic scenes, and keep records.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 3: complete a pre-publish checklist for your video, covering disclosure, consent, music licence and tool terms.
**Tools:** Your exported video from L12; the terms pages of each tool you used [VERIFY]; the upload guidance of your target platform [VERSION]; a checklist document.
**Steps:**
1. List every AI-generated or AI-altered part of your video, shot by shot.
2. Decide where and how you will disclose AI use, and check your platform's current labelling option [VERSION].
3. List every real person who appears or is heard, and confirm their consent. If you used only stock avatars and fictional characters, write this.
4. Check your music licence against your planned use and platforms [VERIFY].
5. Open the terms of each AI tool and note whether commercial use is allowed on your plan [VERIFY].
6. Watch the video once more, asking: could any scene mislead a viewer? Fix or remove it.
7. Save the checklist, licences and notes in your project folder.
**What good looks like:** A completed checklist in which every item has an answer and evidence, such as a licence screenshot or a note from the tool's terms, and any problem has a clear fix.
**Time:** about 30 minutes
