# L12 Sound, Music and Export Settings

Course: AI-21 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O4 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Viewers often forgive a small visual problem. They rarely forgive a voice they cannot hear. Sound is half of your video, and the export is the version people actually watch. This is capstone step 2.

## Explanation
**Three layers of sound.** Most short videos have three layers:

1. **Voice:** the narration or avatar. It is the most important layer.
2. **Music:** it sets the mood and the pace.
3. **Sound effects:** small sounds, such as a door, waves or a coffee pour, that make generated clips feel real.

**Balancing levels.** The voice must always be clear. A practical method:

- Set the voice first, so that it is loud and clear without distortion.
- Bring the music in much lower than the voice. Many editors show levels in decibels (dB); a common starting point is to set the music well below the voice and adjust by ear [VERIFY].
- Use a feature that lowers music automatically under speech, if your editor has one [VERSION].
- Listen on phone speakers and on headphones. A mix that sounds good on headphones can sound unclear on a phone.

**Choosing music.** Only use music whose licence allows your use. "Royalty-free" does not always mean "free for any use". Check: can you use it commercially? On which platforms? Do you need to credit the creator? Editors, stock libraries and AI music tools each have their own terms, and they change [VERIFY]. Save a copy or screenshot of the licence with your project. Never use a popular song just because you own a copy of it.

**Export settings.** Plan two versions:

| Version | Aspect ratio | Common use |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal | 16:9 | Websites and presentations |
| Vertical | 9:16 | Short-form social apps |

Common export settings are 1080p resolution, a frame rate that matches your clips, and the MP4 format [VERSION]. Platform requirements change, so check each platform's current guidance [VERSION].

**Safe area.** On vertical social apps, buttons and text cover parts of the screen, especially the bottom and the right side. Keep captions and important details inside the central **safe area**. Many editors can show safe-area guides [VERSION].

**Analogy:** Mixing sound is like arranging a dinner conversation in a restaurant. The music is the background noise of the room. If it becomes too loud, nobody at the table can hear the person speaking, however interesting they are.

## Worked Example
Lucas runs a hypothetical surf school in Florianópolis, Brazil. His 80-second video is edited. Now he works on sound and export:

1. He sets the voice so it is clear and steady, and removes a short hum with a noise-reduction feature [VERSION].
2. He chooses a calm acoustic track from a royalty-free library. He reads the licence: commercial use is allowed, and credit is optional. He saves a copy of the licence in the project folder [VERIFY].
3. He places the music under the whole video, well below the voice, and raises it slightly only in the 4-second opening with no narration.
4. He adds two sound effects: waves on the beach shot and a board hitting the water.
5. He listens on his phone: the music hides the word "beginners". He lowers the music further.
6. He exports a 16:9 version at 1080p.
7. He duplicates the project, changes it to 9:16, and reframes each clip so the surfer stays in the centre.
8. He moves the captions up, into the safe area, and exports the vertical version.

## Common Mistake
Many learners make the music loud because it sounds exciting while they edit on headphones. On a phone, the voice then disappears. Others simply change the aspect ratio for the vertical version without reframing, so subjects are cut off and captions sit under app buttons. Always reframe each shot and check captions in the safe area.

## Key Takeaways
1. The voice comes first; music and sound effects sit clearly below it, and you should test the mix on phone speakers.
2. Use only music whose licence allows your use, and save a copy of the licence.
3. Export a 16:9 and a 9:16 version, reframe each shot for vertical, and keep captions inside the safe area.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 2: add music and sound, balance the levels, and export one horizontal and one vertical version.
**Tools:** CapCut or your fallback editor [VERSION] [REGION]; a royalty-free music source whose licence you have checked [VERIFY]; headphones and a phone.
**Steps:**
1. Open your rough cut from L11 and make the voice clear and steady.
2. Choose a music track and read its licence. Save a copy or screenshot of it.
3. Add the music well below the voice. Add 1 to 3 sound effects where they help.
4. Listen on phone speakers and on headphones, and adjust until every word is clear.
5. Export the main version at 16:9, 1080p, MP4 [VERSION].
6. Duplicate the project, change it to 9:16, and reframe every shot.
7. Move captions into the safe area and export the vertical version.
**What good looks like:** Two exports in which every word is clear over the music, the music licence is saved, vertical shots are reframed with subjects in view, and captions sit inside the safe area.
**Time:** about 35 minutes
