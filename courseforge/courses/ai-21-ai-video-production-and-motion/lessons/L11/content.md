# L11 Editing in CapCut: Assembly, Pacing and Captions

Course: AI-21 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O4, O7 · Video: 5 min (screen demo)

## Hook
Your clips, avatar segment and script are ready. On their own, they are pieces. In the editor, they become a video. Today you build your rough cut, which is capstone step 1.

## Explanation
CapCut is a video editor with a free option and AI-assisted features, such as automatic captions [VERSION]. It is available as a desktop app, a web version and a mobile app, but features, free options and availability differ by version and by country [VERSION] [REGION]. If CapCut is not available to you, use a free editor such as DaVinci Resolve, Shotcut or Clipchamp as a fallback [VERSION] [REGION]. The editing steps in this lesson are the same in any timeline editor.

**The rough cut.** A rough cut is the first full version of the video: every shot in order, with the narration, at about the right length. It does not need music, colour work or perfect timing yet.

**Build from the voice.** Put the narration or avatar segment on the timeline first. The voice sets the timing. Then place each clip where its words are spoken, using your shot list.

**Trim for pace.** Most generated clips have slow starts or ends. Trim each clip so it starts when something is already happening. A shot that stays after its words have finished feels slow.

**Transitions: use few.** A simple cut is right for most shots. Use a short cross-fade only where time or place changes. Many different transition effects make a video look less professional, not more.

**Captions.** Many viewers watch without sound, so captions matter. Automatic captions save time, but they make mistakes, especially with names, brands, numbers and accents. Always read every caption line and correct it.

**Analogy:** A rough cut is like the first fitting of a tailored suit. All the pieces are sewn together loosely so you can see the shape. You adjust the length and fit before you add the final buttons and details.

## Worked Example
Kwame is making a 75-second explainer for a hypothetical cocoa farmers' cooperative in Kumasi, Ghana. He has a voice-over recording, a HeyGen introduction and 9 clips. The presenter follows these on-screen steps in the CapCut desktop app [VERSION]:

1. Open CapCut and create a **new project**. Set the project to 16:9 [VERSION].
2. Click **Import** and add the voice-over, the avatar segment and all clips, named by shot number.
3. Drag the avatar segment to the start of the main track, and the voice-over to the audio track after it.
4. Drag shot 2 to the video track above the voice-over, where its words begin. Repeat for each shot, following the shot list.
5. Select each clip and drag its edges to trim. Kwame cuts the first second of shot 4, where the camera had not started moving.
6. Play the timeline from the start. Shot 6 stays on screen after its line ends, so he trims 2 seconds.
7. Add one short cross-fade between the farm scenes and the city market, where the place changes [VERSION].
8. Open **Text**, then **Auto captions**, choose the language and generate [VERSION].
9. Read every caption line. "Kumasi" was written as "Kumasee" and "cocoa" as "cocoon". He corrects both.
10. Set a clear caption style: plain font, high contrast, placed in the lower third.
11. Play the whole rough cut and note the length: 78 seconds. He saves the project.

## Common Mistake
Many learners place clips first, then try to fit the voice under them. The result has pictures that do not match the words, and many small gaps. Put the voice down first, then place pictures against it. Also, do not trust automatic captions; one wrong brand name in a caption looks careless to viewers.

## Key Takeaways
1. A rough cut is the first full version of the video: every shot in order, with narration, at about the right length.
2. Build from the voice, trim each clip for pace, and use simple cuts with very few transitions.
3. Generate automatic captions to save time, then read and correct every line, and have a fallback editor if CapCut is not available.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 1: assemble a rough cut of your 60 to 90 second video in CapCut, with captions.
**Tools:** CapCut (free option) [VERSION] [REGION]; fallback: DaVinci Resolve, Shotcut or Clipchamp [VERSION] [REGION]; your clips, avatar segment, voice-over and shot list.
**Steps:**
1. Record your narration, or use your avatar segment and a stock voice for the rest. Put all files in one folder.
2. Create a new project in the aspect ratio of your main version [VERSION].
3. Import all files and place the voice on the timeline first.
4. Place each clip against its words, following your shot list.
5. Trim every clip so it starts with action and ends with its line.
6. Use cuts, and at most two short cross-fades.
7. Generate automatic captions [VERSION], then read and correct every line.
8. Play the whole video, check that it is 60 to 90 seconds, and save the project.
**What good looks like:** A 60 to 90 second rough cut where every shot matches its narration, clips are trimmed so nothing drags, transitions are rare, and captions are fully corrected.
**Time:** about 40 minutes
