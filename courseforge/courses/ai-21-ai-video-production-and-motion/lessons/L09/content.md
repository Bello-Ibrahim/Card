# L09 Avatar Presenters in HeyGen

Course: AI-21 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O4, O6 · Video: 5 min (screen demo)

## Hook
Not everyone wants to appear on camera, and not every project has a budget for a presenter. An avatar tool can turn a typed script into a person speaking to the camera. It is powerful, and for the same reason it comes with clear responsibilities.

## Explanation
HeyGen creates avatar videos: you choose a digital presenter and a voice, type a script, and the tool produces a video of the presenter speaking your words with matching lip movements [VERSION].

In this course you use **stock avatars and stock voices only**. These are presenters and voices that the tool provides for its users under its terms [VERIFY]. You do not create a custom avatar or clone a voice in this course.

**Consent is a principle, not a detail.** Some tools let users make an avatar or voice from a real person's video or recording. If that is ever done, it must be the person themself, with their clear and recorded consent for that specific use. Never make an avatar or voice of someone else, such as a colleague, a celebrity or a family member, without it. Laws about using a person's face and voice differ by country [VERIFY] [REGION], so this course does not give legal advice. Treat clear, recorded consent as the minimum.

**Free-tier limits.** HeyGen's free plan has limits on video length, number of videos, available avatars and export quality, and may add a watermark [VERSION]. Check the current limits before you start.

**Checking quality.** Avatar videos often have small problems:

- **Lip-sync:** mouth movements slightly out of time with the words.
- **Pronunciation:** names, brands and numbers said wrongly.
- **Pacing:** no pauses, so the delivery feels flat.

You can often fix pronunciation by spelling a word as it sounds, and pacing by adding punctuation or pause features [VERSION].

Do not paste confidential or personal data into the script.

**Analogy:** A stock avatar is like hiring a presenter through an agency. The agency has already agreed with the presenter how their image can be used. Making an avatar from someone's face without asking is like putting a person in your advertisement without telling them.

## Worked Example
Mariam runs a hypothetical online Arabic-language school in Cairo, Egypt. She needs a 25-second introduction from a presenter. The presenter follows these on-screen steps [VERSION]:

1. Sign in to HeyGen and check the free-plan limits shown in the account area [VERSION].
2. Click **Create video** and choose a landscape (16:9) format [VERSION].
3. Open the **avatars** list and filter to **stock** or public avatars [VERSION]. Choose a presenter whose style fits a friendly language school.
4. Paste the script into the **script** box:

```text
Welcome to Nile Words. Learning Arabic can feel hard at first. That is why our lessons are short, practical and fun. In the next minute, you will see how our students go from their first word to their first conversation. Let's begin.
```

5. Choose a **stock voice** in the right language and accent, and play the preview [VERSION].
6. Mariam hears "Nile" pronounced wrongly. She changes it to "Nyle Words" in the script only for pronunciation.
7. She adds a full stop after "Let's begin" and a short pause before it [VERSION].
8. She previews again, then clicks **Submit** or **Generate** and notes the time used from her allowance [VERSION].
9. She watches the result 3 times: once for lip-sync, once for pronunciation, once for pacing.
10. She downloads it and checks the resolution and watermark [VERSION]. She names the file "S01_avatar_intro.mp4".

## Common Mistake
Many learners think that if a tool allows something, it must be acceptable. A tool may technically let you upload a photo or recording of another person. That does not mean you have that person's consent, and it does not answer the legal question in your country. Use stock avatars and voices, and treat consent as your responsibility, not the tool's.

## Key Takeaways
1. HeyGen turns a script into a presenter video with a stock avatar and a stock voice, within free-plan limits that change often.
2. A custom avatar or voice of a real person needs that person's clear, recorded consent, and laws differ by country.
3. Check every avatar segment three times: lip-sync, pronunciation and pacing.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Create a 20 to 30 second HeyGen segment with a stock avatar that introduces your video, then check lip-sync and pronunciation.
**Tools:** HeyGen free tier [VERSION]; your script from L02; a checklist.
**Steps:**
1. Write a 45 to 70 word introduction for your video, based on your L02 script.
2. Sign in to HeyGen and check your free-plan limits [VERSION].
3. Create a new video in the aspect ratio of your final video [VERSION].
4. Choose a stock avatar and a stock voice. Do not upload any real person's photo, video or voice.
5. Paste the script and preview the voice. Fix any mispronounced word by spelling it as it sounds.
6. Generate the video and download it [VERSION].
7. Watch it 3 times and write one note each for lip-sync, pronunciation and pacing.
**What good looks like:** A 20 to 30 second segment with a stock avatar and stock voice, correct pronunciation of names, lip-sync that looks natural at normal speed, and a three-line quality note.
**Time:** about 30 minutes
