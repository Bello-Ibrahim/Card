# L09 Avatar Presenters in HeyGen | Presenter Script

Course: AI-21 · Video: 5 min · Words: 682

## Hook
Not everyone wants to appear on camera. An avatar tool can turn a typed script into a person speaking to the camera. It is powerful, and for the same reason, it comes with clear responsibilities.

## Explain
In the last lesson, you learned to keep your clips consistent. Today, you add a presenter to your video.

HeyGen creates avatar videos. You choose a digital presenter and a voice, and type a script. The tool produces a video of the presenter speaking your words, with matching lip movements.

In this course, you use stock avatars and stock voices only. These are presenters and voices the tool provides for its users. Check the tool's terms for how you may use them. You do not create a custom avatar or clone a voice in this course.

Consent is a principle, not a detail. If an avatar or voice is ever made from a real person, it must be that person, with their clear and recorded consent for that specific use. Never make one of a colleague, a celebrity or a family member without it.

Laws about a person's face and voice differ by country, and this course does not give legal advice. Treat clear, recorded consent as the minimum.

The free plan has limits on video length, the number of videos, avatars and export quality, and it may add a watermark. Check the current limits first. And do not paste confidential or personal data into your script.

Avatar videos often have small problems. Lip-sync, where the mouth is slightly out of time. Pronunciation, where names or numbers sound wrong. And pacing, where there are no pauses. You can often fix pronunciation by spelling a word as it sounds, and pacing with punctuation.

A stock avatar is like hiring a presenter through an agency. The agency has already agreed with the presenter how their image can be used. Using someone's face without asking is like putting a person in your advert without telling them.

## Demonstrate
Let's try it. Mariam runs an online Arabic-language school in Cairo, Egypt. The school is made up for this lesson. She needs a twenty-five second introduction from a presenter. Button names in your version may be different.

She creates a new video in landscape format. Then she opens the avatar list and filters it to stock avatars only. She chooses a presenter whose style fits a friendly language school.

She pastes her script. It welcomes viewers to Nile Words, says that learning Arabic can feel hard, and promises short, practical and fun lessons. Then she chooses a stock voice and plays the preview.

She hears the name Nile pronounced wrongly. So she spells it as it sounds, only in the script, not on screen. She also adds a short pause before the last line, let's begin.

She generates the video, and notes the time it used from her allowance. Then she watches the result three times. Once for lip-sync, once for pronunciation, and once for pacing.

Finally, she downloads it, checks the resolution and any watermark, and names the file by shot number.

A common mistake is thinking that if a tool allows something, it must be acceptable. A tool may let you upload a photo or recording of another person. That does not give you their consent, and it does not answer the legal question in your country. Consent is your responsibility, not the tool's.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, HeyGen turns a script into a presenter video with a stock avatar and a stock voice, within free-plan limits that change often. Second, an avatar or voice of a real person needs that person's clear, recorded consent. Third, check every segment three times, for lip-sync, pronunciation and pacing.

## CTA
Now it is your turn. Write a short introduction for your video, and create a twenty to thirty second segment with a stock avatar and a stock voice. Do not upload any real person's photo or voice. Then write one note each on lip-sync, pronunciation and pacing. It takes about thirty minutes. Next lesson: Diagnosing Artifacts, Regenerate, Edit or Replace?
