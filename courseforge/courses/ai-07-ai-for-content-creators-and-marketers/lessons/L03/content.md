# L03 Audience Research and Personas with AI

Course: AI-07 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O3 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Ask an AI assistant to describe your ideal customer, and in ten seconds you get a name, an age, a job, three hobbies and a favourite app. It looks like research. It is not. It is a well-written guess.

## Explanation
A **persona** is a short description of a typical member of your audience: who they are, what they need, what problems they have and where they spend time online. Personas help you decide what to say and where to say it.

AI assistants are useful for drafting personas quickly. You can ask: "Suggest two audience personas for a small bicycle repair shop in a large city. For each, list their main questions, problems and favourite channels." In seconds you have a structure to work with.

But the AI does not know your customers. It combines general patterns from its training text. Some details will be reasonable, some will be wrong for your market, and some will be **stereotypes**: simple, often unfair ideas about a group, such as assuming all older customers avoid social media.

So treat every AI persona as a **starting hypothesis**, not a result. A hypothesis is a guess that you test. Test it against real sources:

- **Customer comments and messages:** what do people actually ask you?
- **Reviews:** what do they praise or complain about?
- **Short surveys:** three or four questions sent to existing customers.
- **Your own analytics:** the age range, locations and channels your platforms report. [VERSION]
- **Conversations:** staff who talk to customers every day often know the most.

When you check each detail, sort it into one of three groups: **confirmed by real data**, **plausible, needs checking**, or **probably a stereotype**. Then rewrite the persona using what you confirmed.

If you give AI real comments or survey answers to summarise, remove names, contact details and anything personal first. Only share data your organisation allows you to use this way.

**Analogy:** An AI persona is like a sketch a police artist draws from a short description. It helps you start looking, but you would never arrest someone based only on the sketch. You compare it with real evidence and correct it.

## Worked Example
Karim runs marketing for a sample bicycle repair shop in Casablanca, Morocco. He asks an AI assistant for two personas.

The first persona is "Youssef, 28, a delivery rider who needs fast repairs, uses Instagram and wants low prices." Karim checks his sources:

- **Fast repairs:** confirmed. Many messages ask "Can you fix it today?"
- **Delivery rider:** plausible, needs checking. The shop has some riders as customers, but he has no numbers.
- **Uses Instagram:** partly wrong. His analytics and customer messages show that most repair requests come through a messaging app, not Instagram.
- **Wants low prices:** probably a stereotype. Reviews from riders mention speed and reliability much more than price.

The second persona, "a retired man who rides on weekends and does not use smartphones", is mostly a stereotype. Several weekend riders in their sixties send photos of their bikes by phone before booking.

Karim rewrites the first persona: "Delivery riders and daily commuters who lose income when their bike is broken. Main need: same-day repair and a clear price before work starts. Main channel: messaging app. To check: what share of customers are delivery riders." He adds a question about this to a short customer survey.

## Common Mistake
The common mistake is to copy the AI persona into a strategy document without testing it, because it looks detailed and professional. Detail is not evidence. A fake name and exact age make a guess feel real. Always mark what is confirmed, what needs checking and what is a stereotype, and keep the "needs checking" list visible so the team knows what is still a guess.

## Key Takeaways
1. AI can draft personas quickly, but they are starting hypotheses built from general patterns, not research about your customers.
2. Test each persona detail against real sources such as comments, reviews, surveys and your own analytics.
3. Label details as confirmed, needing checks or probable stereotypes, then rewrite the persona from the evidence.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Ask a free AI assistant to create 2 personas for your brand. Mark each detail as "confirmed by real data", "plausible, needs checking" or "probably a stereotype", and rewrite one persona.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) [VERSION]; your comments, reviews or analytics if you have them; a notes app.
**Steps:**
1. Write a two-sentence description of your brand and product. Do not include confidential information.
2. Ask the assistant for 2 personas, each with needs, problems, questions and favourite channels.
3. Copy the personas into a document and mark every detail with one of the three labels.
4. For each "confirmed" detail, write the source, such as "20 comments ask about delivery time".
5. If you have no real data, use a sample brand and mark details as "needs checking", then list where you would find the evidence.
6. Rewrite one persona using only confirmed and plausible details, and add a "to check" list.
**What good looks like:** Every detail has a label. Confirmed details name a real source. At least one stereotype is spotted and removed. The rewritten persona is shorter and more specific than the AI version.
**Time:** about 20 minutes
