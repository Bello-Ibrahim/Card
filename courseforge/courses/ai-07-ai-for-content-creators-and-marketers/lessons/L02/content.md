# L02 Teaching AI Your Brand Voice

Course: AI-07 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O4, O6 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Ask an AI assistant to "write an Instagram post about our new coffee" and you will get something like: "Discover the rich, bold flavour of our new blend! Perfect for every moment." It is not wrong. It just sounds like everyone, and your brand is not everyone.

## Explanation
A chat assistant writes in a safe, average style unless you tell it otherwise. It has read a huge amount of text, so without guidance it produces the most common kind of marketing language. To get text that sounds like your brand, you need to give it your **brand voice** as context.

A **brand voice guide** is a short document, one page is enough, that describes how your brand speaks. It has four parts:

- **Personality:** three to five adjectives, each with a short explanation. For example, "warm, not childish" or "expert, not formal".
- **Words to use:** words and phrases your brand likes, such as the way you name your products or greet customers.
- **Words to avoid:** words that do not fit, such as overused marketing words, slang your audience would not use, or claims you cannot prove.
- **Example posts:** two or three real posts that you think sound exactly right. Examples teach style better than descriptions.

You can paste the guide at the start of a conversation, or save it in the tool's project or custom instruction feature if your plan has one. [VERSION] Then add your request after it: "Using the voice guide above, write a caption for…"

Only include information you are allowed to share. Do not paste confidential plans, customer data or private contracts into an AI tool.

**Analogy:** A voice guide is like the notes you give a new team member on their first day. A talented new writer still needs to know how the company speaks to customers. Without those notes, they write in their own general style. With them, their work starts to sound like the team.

## Worked Example
Valentina manages marketing for a sample coffee brand from a family farm in the hills near Medellín, Colombia. The brand sells whole beans online.

First she asks, with no guide: "Write an Instagram caption for our new washed-process coffee."

The result: "Elevate your mornings with our exquisite new blend! Rich, smooth and bursting with flavour. Order now and taste the difference! #coffee #coffeelover"

Then she writes a short voice guide:

- **Personality:** warm like a family table; proud of the farm without boasting; clear about how coffee is made.
- **Use:** "our farm", "the harvest", "the Suárez family", tasting notes in plain words.
- **Avoid:** "elevate", "exquisite", "bursting with flavour", health claims.
- **Examples:** two past captions that followers liked, both mentioning a person on the farm.

She pastes the guide and repeats the request. The new caption: "This harvest, Don Álvaro tried something new: washing the beans before drying them. The result is a cleaner cup with notes of red apple and brown sugar. Small batch, from our farm to your kitchen."

Valentina lists three differences: the second caption names a person, uses plain tasting notes and removes the empty words. She still edits it. She checks with the farm that the tasting notes are accurate, because the AI invented "red apple" from general coffee knowledge, and she changes one phrase to match how Don Álvaro describes the process himself.

## Common Mistake
Many learners write a voice guide that is only a list of adjectives, such as "friendly, professional, fun". Almost every brand could use those words, so the AI has nothing specific to follow. Add clear "use" and "avoid" lists and, most important, real example posts. The second mistake is trusting the improved text completely. A better style does not mean correct facts. You still review for accuracy and voice before publishing.

## Key Takeaways
1. Without context, AI writes in an average marketing style that could belong to any brand.
2. A one-page voice guide with personality, words to use, words to avoid and example posts helps AI match your brand.
3. Text that sounds right can still contain invented details, so always review it for facts and voice.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Write a one-page voice guide for a real or sample brand. Ask Claude or ChatGPT for the same post with and without the guide, and list 3 differences.
**Tools:** A document or notes app; Claude or ChatGPT (free tier). [VERSION]
**Steps:**
1. Choose a real brand you work with or invent a sample brand. If you use a real brand, share only public information.
2. Write the four parts of the guide: personality (3 to 5 adjectives with explanations), words to use, words to avoid, and 2 or 3 example posts.
3. Open a new chat and ask for one social post about a product, with no guide.
4. Open another new chat, paste the guide, and make exactly the same request.
5. Compare the two posts and list 3 specific differences.
6. Mark any detail in the second post that you would need to check before publishing.
**What good looks like:** A guide with specific words and real examples, not just adjectives; two posts side by side; three differences that point to exact words or sentences; and at least one detail marked "check".
**Time:** about 20 minutes
