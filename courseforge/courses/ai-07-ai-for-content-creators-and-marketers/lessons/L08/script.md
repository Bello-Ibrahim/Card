# L08 Designing Visuals with Canva and Free Image Generators | Presenter Script

Course: AI-07 · Video: 5 min · Words: 682

## Hook
People often decide in one second whether to stop at your post, and the visual is usually what they see first. Today, you will watch three branded visuals being built on screen, one with an AI image, and a record of where every image came from.

## Explain
Last time, we wrote long-form content. Now we design the pictures that go with it. We use two kinds of tool. Canva gives you templates and a brand kit, where you save your colours, fonts and logo. It also has AI features that can suggest designs or remove backgrounds. A free image generator, such as Google Gemini, creates original images from a written prompt.

A good image prompt has four parts. The subject, what is in the image. The style, such as a realistic photo in soft natural light. The colours, your brand colours in words. And the composition, such as a top view, with empty space on the left for text.

Some safety rules. Do not generate real people, real logos or copyrighted characters. Do not ask for the style of a named living artist. Do not upload photos of people without their consent. And record the tool and prompt for every AI image, so you can check licences and labels later.

Why does a brand kit matter? It is like a school uniform. Each student is different, but the uniform shows at once which school they belong to. Consistent colours and fonts do the same for your visuals.

## Demonstrate
Let's build it. Fatou designs visuals for a sample textile shop in Dakar, Senegal, that sells hand-dyed fabric. She opens Canva and goes to the brand kit. She adds two brand colours by their codes, one font for headings and one for body text.

She searches templates for Instagram post, and picks a simple layout with space for an image and a short headline. Then she applies her brand kit, and the template changes to her colours and fonts.

Now the image. In Google Gemini, she types her prompt: folded hand-dyed cotton fabric in deep indigo and teal patterns, on a light wooden shelf, soft morning light, realistic photo, with empty space at the top. Notice that the prompt covers all four parts.

She looks closely at the results. She rejects one where the patterns melt into each other, and one with an extra object. She chooses the best image, downloads it, and places it in her Canva template.

She adds the headline, new indigo collection, in the brand heading font. Then she zooms out, to check that the text is easy to read on a phone screen. She downloads the design.

Last, she opens her asset log, a simple spreadsheet. She writes the visual's name, the tool, the exact prompt, the date, the plan, and whether she has checked the licence.

She makes two more visuals in the same style: a quote card with a customer review, and a tip card on washing dyed fabric. The AI image for the tip card shows a label with letters that are not real words. So she removes the label, and adds real text in Canva.

The common mistake is to accept the first image without looking closely. AI images often contain errors, such as letters that are not real words, odd hands, or objects joined together. If an image shows your product, it must look like your real product, or customers may feel misled. Zoom in and check every detail.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, Canva's brand kit and templates keep your colours, fonts and layout consistent. Second, a good image prompt describes subject, style, colours and composition. Third, avoid real people, logos, copyrighted characters and named artists' styles, and log the tool, prompt and licence check for every AI image.
