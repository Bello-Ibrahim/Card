# L08 Designing Visuals with Canva and Free Image Generators

Course: AI-07 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O4, O2 · Video: 5 min (screen demo)

## Hook
People often decide in a second whether to stop at your post, and the visual is usually what they see first. Today you will build three branded visuals on screen, one of them with an AI-generated image, and keep a record that shows where every image came from.

## Explanation
There are two ways to use AI for visuals in this course.

**Canva** gives you templates and a brand setup. You can save your brand colours, fonts and logo, then apply them to any template. Canva also includes AI features that can create images from text, suggest designs or remove backgrounds. Feature names, limits and which plan includes them change often, so check the current version. [VERSION]

**Free image generators** create original images from a written prompt. Several free options exist, inside design tools and as separate websites and chat assistants. Their free limits and licence terms differ and change, so this course refers to them in general terms. Choose one, and check its current terms before using its images for business. [VERSION]

A good image prompt has four parts:

- **Subject:** what is in the image. "A bar of yellow soap on a wooden table beside fresh lemongrass."
- **Style:** "soft natural light, realistic photo" or "flat illustration".
- **Colours:** your brand colours in words, such as "deep blue and bright teal accents".
- **Composition:** "top view, empty space on the left for text".

Keep your brand consistent: use the same colours, fonts and layout style across visuals, so people recognise your posts without reading the name.

Some safety rules. Do not generate images of real people, real logos or copyrighted characters. Do not ask for images "in the style of" a named living artist. Do not upload photos of people without their consent. And record the tool and prompt for every AI image, so you can check licences and disclosure later (L05).

**Analogy:** A brand kit is like a school uniform. Each student is different, but the uniform shows at once which school they belong to. Your visuals can show many different things, but the same colours and fonts show that they all belong to your brand.

## Worked Example
Fatou designs visuals for a sample textile shop in Dakar, Senegal, that sells hand-dyed fabric. This is the on-screen sequence. Menu names may differ in the current version. [VERSION]

1. Open Canva and go to the brand kit area. Add two brand colours by their colour codes and choose one font for headings and one for body text. [VERSION]
2. Search templates for "Instagram post" and choose a simple layout with space for an image and a short headline.
3. Apply the brand kit so the template's colours and fonts change to the brand's.
4. Open the AI image feature. [VERSION] Type: "Folded hand-dyed cotton fabric in deep indigo and teal patterns, on a light wooden shelf, soft morning light, realistic photo, empty space at the top."
5. Look at the options it creates. Reject any with strange details, such as patterns that melt into each other or extra objects. Choose one and place it in the template.
6. Add the headline "New indigo collection" in the brand heading font. Check that the text is easy to read on a phone screen by zooming out.
7. Download the design.
8. Open the asset log, a simple spreadsheet, and write: visual name, tool, exact prompt, date, plan, and "licence checked: yes/no".

Fatou makes two more visuals: a quote card with a customer review she has permission to use, and a "how to wash dyed fabric" tip card. Both use the same template style. The AI image for the tip card shows a label with unreadable letters, so she removes the label area and adds real text in Canva.

## Common Mistake
Many beginners accept the first generated image without looking closely. AI images often contain errors: text that is not real words, odd hands, objects joined together or details that do not match the product. If the image shows your product, it must look like your real product, or customers may feel misled. Zoom in, check every detail and, if the image could be mistaken for a real photo of your product, use a real photo instead or follow your disclosure rule.

## Key Takeaways
1. Canva's brand kit and templates keep colours, fonts and layout consistent across your visuals.
2. A good image prompt describes subject, style, colours and composition.
3. Avoid real people, logos, copyrighted characters and named artists' styles, and log the tool, prompt and licence check for every AI image.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Create 3 branded visuals for your brand in Canva, including at least one AI-generated image. Record the tool and prompt used for each image and check the licence terms.
**Tools:** Canva (free plan) [VERSION]; one free image generator of your choice, or Canva's AI image feature [VERSION]; a spreadsheet for your asset log.
**Steps:**
1. Set up your brand colours and fonts in Canva, using the brand kit if your plan has one, or by noting the colour codes and fonts to use manually. [VERSION]
2. Choose one template style for all 3 visuals.
3. Write an image prompt with subject, style, colours and composition, and generate at least 3 options.
4. Choose the best image, check it closely for errors and place it in your design.
5. Make 2 more visuals with the same style, using your own photos, AI images or text only.
6. In your asset log, record for each visual: tool, prompt (if any), date, plan and whether you checked the licence.
**What good looks like:** 3 visuals that clearly share colours, fonts and layout; at least one AI image with no visible errors; no real people, logos or copyrighted characters; and a complete asset log row for each visual.
**Time:** about 35 minutes
