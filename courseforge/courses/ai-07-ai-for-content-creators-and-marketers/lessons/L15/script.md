# L15 Final Review and Campaign Presentation | Presenter Script

Course: AI-07 · Video: 5 min · Words: 677

## Hook
Your campaign is almost ready. Before you show it to a client or a manager, one question matters most. If they check any single post, will it be accurate, on-brand and safe to publish?

## Explain
Last time, you used your results to improve the plan. Now we finish it. The final stage has two parts: a quality review of the whole campaign, and a clear presentation. Let's start with the review, at three levels.

First, every asset. Apply the five-point checklist: accurate, on-brand, original, inclusive and legally safe. Second, the voice. Read several pieces in a row. Do they sound like the same brand? Look for words from your avoid list, and patterns that repeat across the month.

Third, your disclosure rule. Open your asset log and check every AI-generated item. Does each one follow your rule? Is every licence checked? Then look at the whole campaign. Does every week move the audience toward the goal, and are the key dates correct?

Then the presentation. Clients and managers are busy. So present a short document in seven sections: goal and audience, strategy, the thirty-day calendar, sample assets from each channel, the asset log, your review notes, and a measurement plan.

In the strategy section, add one sentence to explain each choice: your channels, pillars, weekly themes and posting rhythm. In the measurement plan, say which measures you will track, and when you will review them. And be honest about how AI was used. Clients and managers increasingly want to know, and a clear asset log shows care and control.

The final review is like the checks a pilot makes before take-off. The plane may have flown well yesterday, but the pilot still follows the list every time. Small problems are easy to fix on the ground, and costly in the air.

## Demonstrate
Let's watch a final review. Rafael prepares a campaign for a sample co-working space in Manila, in the Philippines. The goal is forty bookings for a free trial day in thirty days, across a social feed, a professional network and email.

At the asset level, he finds a carousel that says the space is open twenty-four hours. It is actually open from seven in the morning to ten at night, so he corrects it. At the voice level, he notices that the word community appears in almost every caption, so he uses it less.

At the disclosure level, his log shows one realistic AI image of an office, with no label. His rule says realistic AI scenes must be labelled, and this image could make people think it is the real space. So he replaces it with a real photo.

His document has seven short sections. Under review notes, he lists two open questions for his manager. Can we use the photo of the member event? And do we have written consent from the people in it? This makes the decisions clear, instead of hiding them.

Take your time with the review. A common mistake is to check only the first week in detail. Problems often appear later in the month, when work was faster, and dates or offers had changed. Another is leaving out the asset log and review notes. Those show a client that the campaign is safe to publish.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, review the whole campaign at three levels: every asset, the brand voice across the month, and the disclosure rule, using your asset log. Second, present it as a short, seven-section document. Third, be open about how AI was used, and list open questions, so decision-makers can see that the campaign is under human control.

## CTA

It takes about an hour. Before you submit, read the capstone rubric and tick every item on the submission checklist. Then submit your campaign. You planned it, you shaped it, and it sounds like you.
