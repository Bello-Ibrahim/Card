# L15 Final Review and Campaign Presentation

Course: AI-07 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O6, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Your campaign is almost ready. Before you show it to a client or a manager, one question matters most: if they check any single post, will it be accurate, on-brand and safe to publish?

## Explanation
The final stage has two parts: a **quality review** of the whole campaign, and a **clear presentation**.

**1. Final quality review.** Review the campaign at three levels.

- **Every asset:** apply the 5-point checklist from L10: accurate, on-brand, original, inclusive, legally safe.
- **The voice guide:** read several pieces in a row. Do they sound like the same brand? Look for words from your "avoid" list and for repeated patterns across the month.
- **Your disclosure rule:** open your asset log and check every AI-generated item. Does each one follow your rule from L05? Is every licence checked? [VERSION]

Also check the campaign as a whole: does every week move the audience toward the goal, and are the key dates correct?

**2. Presentation.** A client or manager is usually busy. Present the campaign as a short document in this order:

1. **Goal and audience:** one measurable goal and a short persona.
2. **Strategy:** channels, pillars, weekly themes and posting rhythm, with one sentence explaining each choice.
3. **Calendar:** the 30-day table.
4. **Sample assets:** at least one finished example from each channel.
5. **Asset log:** tools, prompts, licence checks and disclosure decisions.
6. **Review notes:** what you checked, what you changed and what still needs a decision.
7. **Measurement plan:** which measures you will track and when you will review them.

Be honest about how AI was used. Clients and managers increasingly want to know, and a clear asset log shows care and control.

**Analogy:** The final review is like the checks a pilot makes before take-off. The plane may have flown well yesterday, but the pilot still follows the list every time, because small problems are easy to fix on the ground and costly in the air.

## Worked Example
Rafael prepares a campaign for a sample co-working space in Manila, the Philippines. The goal is 40 bookings for a free trial day in 30 days, across a social feed, a professional network and email.

He runs his final review. On the asset level, he finds that one carousel says the space is "open 24 hours". It is open from 7 a.m. to 10 p.m., so he corrects it. On the voice level, he notices that "community" appears in almost every caption and reduces it. On the disclosure level, his log shows one realistic AI image of an office interior with no disclosure label. His rule says realistic AI scenes must be labelled, and the image could make people think it is the real space. He replaces it with a real photo.

His presentation document is seven short sections. Under review notes he writes: "Two open questions for the manager: Can we use the photo of the member event? Do we have written consent from the people in it?" This makes the decisions clear instead of hiding them.

## Common Mistake
The common mistake is to review only the first week in detail and assume the rest is fine. Problems often appear later in the month, when work was faster and dates or offers had changed. Another mistake is to present only the attractive assets and leave out the asset log and review notes. Those are what show a client that the campaign is safe to publish.

## Key Takeaways
1. Review the whole campaign at three levels: every asset with the 5-point checklist, the brand voice across the month, and the disclosure rule using the asset log.
2. Present the campaign as a short document: goal, strategy, calendar, sample assets, asset log, review notes and measurement plan.
3. Be open about how AI was used and list open questions, so decision-makers can see the campaign is under human control.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 3: review and finish your 30-day campaign, then present it as a short document with the goal, calendar, sample assets, asset log and your review notes.
**Tools:** Your L12 calendar, L13 assets and asset log, and L14 updates; a document editor or Canva for the presentation [VERSION]; Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) to help check structure and spelling [VERSION].
**Steps:**
1. Check every finished asset with the 5-point checklist and record the result.
2. Read at least 10 captions in a row against your voice guide and fix repeated patterns.
3. Check every AI item in your asset log against your disclosure rule and licence notes.
4. Make sure you have at least one finished sample asset for each channel.
5. Build your document in the 7-section order above.
6. Add review notes: what you changed and any open questions.
7. Read the capstone rubric and tick every item on the submission checklist.
**What good looks like:** A clear document of a few pages that a manager can understand in 10 minutes, with a complete calendar, sample assets from every channel, a full asset log and honest review notes.
**Time:** about 60 minutes
