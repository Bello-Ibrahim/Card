# L13 Producing Campaign Assets at Speed

Course: AI-07 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O4, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Making one post at a time means opening the same tools, finding the same files and writing the same prompts every day. Making a whole week of content in one focused session can be much faster, if you prepare well.

## Explanation
**Batch production** means creating many similar pieces of content in one working session, instead of one piece each day. It saves time because you prepare once and stay focused on one type of task.

Prepare three things before you start:

- **Your voice guide** (L02), ready to paste.
- **Templates:** your Canva templates for feed posts, quotes and tips (L08), and your CapCut project or template for short videos (L09). [VERSION]
- **Saved prompts:** prompts that worked well before, stored in a document with blanks to fill in. For example: "Using the voice guide above, write a [platform] caption of about [length] words about [topic]. Hook: [type]. CTA: [action]. Give 3 variations."

Then work in **blocks**, by task type, not by day:

1. **Text block:** write all captions and email text for the week.
2. **Visual block:** make all graphics in Canva using your templates.
3. **Video block:** film and edit the week's short video.
4. **Review block:** check everything with the 5-point checklist (L10).

Keep an **asset log**. This is a simple spreadsheet with one row per asset. It records which tools produced which parts, so you can check licences and apply your disclosure rule (L05). Suggested columns:

| Asset ID | Calendar day | Channel | Asset type | Tools used | Prompt or source | AI-generated parts | Licence checked | Disclosure needed | Review status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Speed must not remove the review. Batch production makes it easier to review, because you can compare all pieces side by side and spot repeated phrases or inconsistent visuals.

**Analogy:** Batch production is like preparing lunches for the whole week on Sunday. You wash all the vegetables at once, cook the rice once and pack the boxes in a row. It is faster than cooking every day, but you still check that each box is fresh and correct.

## Worked Example
Noor runs content for a sample handmade jewellery brand in Cairo, Egypt. Her campaign calendar for days 1 to 7 has 5 feed posts, 1 short video and 1 email, across three channels.

On Saturday morning she works in blocks:

- **Text block (50 minutes):** she uses her saved caption prompt and voice guide to draft all 5 captions and the email, asking for 3 variations each. She chooses, combines and adds a personal sentence to each.
- **Visual block (60 minutes):** she opens her three Canva templates and makes 5 graphics. Three use her own product photos; two use AI-generated background textures. She logs the image tool and prompt for each.
- **Video block (60 minutes):** she films a 30-second video of her hands shaping a silver ring, then edits it in CapCut with auto-captions, checking each word.
- **Review block (30 minutes):** she places all 7 assets side by side. She notices that three captions start with the same question pattern and rewrites two. The email mentions "free delivery", which ends before day 7, so she corrects it.

Her asset log now has 7 rows. For the two AI textures, "Disclosure needed" says "no", because under her disclosure rule, abstract backgrounds that do not show a realistic scene do not need a label. She notes the rule in the log so a reviewer can see her reasoning.

## Common Mistake
The common mistake is to rush the review because the batch felt productive. Mistakes in batch work repeat: one wrong price or one repeated phrase can appear in every piece. Another mistake is not keeping the asset log while working, and trying to remember later which image came from which tool. Fill in the log as you create each asset.

## Key Takeaways
1. Batch production creates a week of content in one session by working in blocks: text, visuals, video, then review.
2. Prepare your voice guide, templates and saved prompts before you start.
3. Keep an asset log as you work, recording tools, prompts, licence checks and disclosure decisions for every asset.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 2: produce the content for the first 7 days of your campaign using Claude or ChatGPT, Canva and CapCut, and keep an asset log with the tool used for each item.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier), Canva (free plan), CapCut (free version where available) [VERSION] [REGION]; your L12 calendar; a spreadsheet for your asset log.
**Steps:**
1. List every asset needed for days 1 to 7 from your calendar.
2. Prepare your voice guide, templates and 2 or 3 saved prompts.
3. Create the asset log with the columns above.
4. Work in blocks: text, visuals, video. Fill in a log row as you finish each asset.
5. In the review block, check all assets with the 5-point checklist, side by side.
6. Update the "Asset status" column in your calendar.
**What good looks like:** All assets for days 1 to 7 are finished and reviewed, they share a consistent look and voice, and every asset has a complete row in the asset log, including licence and disclosure decisions.
**Time:** about 60 minutes
