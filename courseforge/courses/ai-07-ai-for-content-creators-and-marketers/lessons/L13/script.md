# L13 Producing Campaign Assets at Speed | Presenter Script

Course: AI-07 · Video: 5 min · Words: 691

## Hook
Making one post at a time means opening the same tools, finding the same files and writing the same prompts, every single day. Making a whole week of content in one focused session can be much faster, if you prepare well.

## Explain
You now have a thirty-day calendar. In this lesson, you produce the first week of it. The method is batch production. That means creating many similar pieces in one working session, instead of one piece each day. It saves time, because you prepare once and stay focused on one type of task.

Prepare three things before you start. Your voice guide, ready to paste. Your templates, in Canva for posts, quotes and tips, and in CapCut for short videos. And saved prompts, prompts that worked well before, stored in a document with blanks to fill in, such as the platform, the length and the topic.

Then work in blocks, by type of task, not by day. First a text block, for all captions and emails. Then a visual block, for all the graphics. Then a video block, to film and edit the week's short video. And last, a review block, where you check everything with the five-point checklist.

As you work, keep an asset log, a spreadsheet with one row per asset. It records which tools produced which parts, which prompt you used, whether you checked the licence, and whether the asset needs a disclosure label under your rule.

Batch production is like preparing lunches for the whole week on Sunday. You wash all the vegetables at once, cook the rice once, and pack the boxes in a row. It is faster than cooking every day. But you still check that each box is fresh and correct.

## Demonstrate
Let's follow Noor. She runs content for a sample handmade jewellery brand in Cairo, Egypt. Days one to seven of her calendar have five feed posts, one short video and one email, across three channels.

On Saturday morning, she works in blocks. In the text block, she uses her saved prompt and voice guide to draft all five captions and the email, with three variations each. She chooses, combines, and adds a personal sentence to each one.

In the visual block, she makes five graphics with her three Canva templates. Three use her own product photos, and two use AI-generated background textures. She logs the image tool and prompt for each one. In the video block, she films her hands shaping a silver ring, and edits it in CapCut with auto-captions, checking each word.

In the review block, she places all seven assets side by side. She notices that three captions start with the same question, so she rewrites two. And the email mentions free delivery, but that offer ends before day seven. So she corrects it.

Her asset log now has seven rows. For the two AI textures, disclosure needed says no, because under her rule, abstract backgrounds that do not show a realistic scene need no label. She writes the rule in the log, so a reviewer can see her reasoning.

The common mistake is to rush the review, because the batch felt productive. Speed must not remove the review. In fact, batching makes review easier, because you can compare every piece side by side. Mistakes in batch work repeat. One wrong price can appear in every piece. And fill in the log as you create each asset, not later from memory.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, batch production creates a week of content in one session, by working in blocks: text, visuals, video, then review. Second, prepare your voice guide, templates and saved prompts before you start. Third, keep an asset log as you work, recording tools, prompts, licence checks and disclosure decisions for every asset.
