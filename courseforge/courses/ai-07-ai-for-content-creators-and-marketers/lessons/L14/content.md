# L14 Measuring Results and Improving with AI

Course: AI-07 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O5, O6 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
After a week of posting, you have numbers: views, likes, clicks. An AI assistant can read them and tell you exactly what to do next, in a very confident voice. Should you follow its advice?

## Explanation
First, choose a few **measures** that match your campaign goal. Four common ones:

- **Reach:** how many different people saw the content.
- **Engagement:** how many people reacted, commented, shared or saved it.
- **Clicks:** how many people followed a link.
- **Sign-ups or sales:** how many completed the action in your goal.

If your goal is sign-ups, reach and likes are helpful signs, but sign-ups are what matter most. A post with fewer likes that brings more sign-ups is doing the more important job. Platforms name and count these measures in different ways, so check the definitions in each tool. [VERSION]

AI assistants can summarise a small results table quickly and suggest changes. But be careful: **a small amount of data can mislead**. With one week of posts, one result can be unusual because of the day, the weather, a holiday or luck. AI may still describe a "clear trend". Check its reasoning: How many posts is this based on? Could something else explain it? Is the suggestion connected to the goal?

Do not paste personal data, such as names or email addresses of people who signed up, into an AI tool. Use totals only.

**Analogy:** Reading one week of results is like judging a restaurant after one meal. The meal tells you something, but one bad evening might be the chef's day off. You look for patterns over several visits before you change the whole menu.

## Worked Example
Ewa manages a sample campaign for a vegan bakery in Kraków, Poland. The goal is 120 sign-ups for a baking class in 30 days. Here is her hypothetical week 1 data. Learners without live results can use this table.

| Day | Channel | Format | Reach | Engagements | Link clicks | Sign-ups |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Social feed | Photo post | 1,200 | 85 | 12 | 2 |
| 2 | Short video | 30-second video | 3,400 | 210 | 20 | 1 |
| 3 | Social feed | Carousel (recipe tips) | 950 | 140 | 31 | 6 |
| 4 | Email | Newsletter | 600 | 45 | 38 | 9 |
| 5 | Social feed | Quote card | 700 | 30 | 4 | 0 |
| 6 | Short video | 30-second video | 2,100 | 95 | 9 | 1 |
| 7 | Social feed | Behind-the-scenes photo | 1,500 | 160 | 15 | 3 |

Total sign-ups after week 1: 22.

She asks an AI assistant: "Here is week 1 of a campaign with the goal of 120 class sign-ups. Summarise the results and suggest 3 improvements. Explain your reasoning and say how confident you are."

The AI suggests:

1. **"Post more short videos, because they have the highest reach."** Ewa **changes** this idea. The videos reach many people, but they brought only 2 sign-ups. She keeps videos for awareness but adds a clear sign-up call to action at the end.
2. **"Send the email twice a week, because it brings the most sign-ups."** Ewa **accepts** a smaller version: she adds one short reminder email in week 3, not two emails every week, because too many emails may make people unsubscribe.
3. **"Stop quote cards completely, because they perform worst."** Ewa **rejects** this for now. It is one post on one day. She will try one more quote card with a stronger call to action before deciding.

She updates her calendar for weeks 2 to 4 with these changes and notes the reason for each.

## Common Mistake
The common mistake is to follow the AI's suggestions because they are clear and confident, without checking the amount of data or the link to the goal. Another mistake is to judge content only by reach or likes. Always ask: "Which content moves people toward the goal?" and "Is this result based on enough posts to trust?"

## Key Takeaways
1. Choose a few measures that match your campaign goal, and give the most weight to the one closest to it.
2. AI can summarise results and suggest changes, but small amounts of data can mislead, so check its reasoning.
3. Accept, change or reject each suggestion with a reason, then update your calendar.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Give an AI assistant the sample results table (or your own) and ask for 3 improvement ideas. Accept, change or reject each idea with a reason, and update your calendar.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) [VERSION]; the sample table above or your own totals; your L12 calendar.
**Steps:**
1. Copy the sample table, or build your own with totals only and no personal data.
2. Write your campaign goal above the table.
3. Ask the AI for a summary and 3 improvement ideas, with reasoning and a confidence level.
4. For each idea, write "accept", "change" or "reject" and one or two sentences explaining why, including how much data it is based on.
5. Update at least 2 rows in your calendar based on your decisions, and note the reason in the Notes column.
**What good looks like:** Three clear decisions, each with a reason linked to the goal and to the amount of data, at least one idea changed or rejected, and a calendar that shows the updates.
**Time:** about 25 minutes
