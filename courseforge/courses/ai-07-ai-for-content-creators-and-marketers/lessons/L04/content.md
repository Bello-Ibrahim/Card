# L04 Generating and Filtering Content Ideas

Course: AI-07 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O3 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Getting 40 content ideas from AI takes less than a minute. Choosing the 8 that are worth making takes real thinking. This lesson is about both halves, and the second half is the one that matters.

## Explanation
AI assistants are very good at producing many ideas quickly. The quality of the ideas depends on the structure you give them. A vague prompt like "Give me content ideas for my gym" returns general ideas that any gym could post. A **structured prompt** gives the AI a framework. Three useful frameworks are:

- **Content pillars:** the three to five main themes your brand talks about, such as "training tips", "community stories" and "healthy routines". Ask for ideas under each pillar.
- **Formats:** the types of content you can produce, such as short video, carousel, poll, behind-the-scenes photo or newsletter. Ask for ideas that match each format.
- **Customer questions:** real questions people ask you. Ask the AI to turn each question into a post idea.

Add your voice guide and persona from L02 and L03 so that the ideas fit your brand and audience.

Then **filter**. Score every idea from 1 to 3 on three criteria:

1. **Fits the brand:** Does it match your pillars and voice?
2. **Useful to the audience:** Does it answer a real need or question from your persona?
3. **Possible to produce:** Can you make it with your time, budget, skills and permissions?

Add the scores and keep the highest. When scores are equal, choose the idea that is most different from the rest, so your shortlist has variety.

**Analogy:** AI ideas are like a large catch from a fishing net. The net brings up many fish at once, but also weeds, stones and fish that are too small. Filtering is the sorting table where you keep only what you can use.

## Worked Example
Priya is the marketer for a sample fitness studio in Pune, India. The studio offers group classes for working adults. Her pillars are "short workouts", "member stories" and "rest and recovery".

She writes a structured prompt: "Using the voice guide below, suggest 40 content ideas for a fitness studio for busy working adults in Pune. Group them under our 3 pillars. For each idea, suggest a format: short video, carousel, poll or story. Also include 10 ideas based on these real member questions: 'Can I join if I have never exercised?', 'What should I eat before an evening class?', 'How do I avoid knee pain?'"

She gets 40 ideas in a table. She scores them. Some examples:

| Idea | Brand | Audience | Possible | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10-minute desk stretch video | 3 | 3 | 3 | 9 |
| Member story: first class at 45 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 8 |
| "Celebrity workout secrets" | 1 | 1 | 1 | 3 |
| Meal plan for weight loss | 2 | 3 | 1 | 6 |

The celebrity idea is off-brand and uses real people she has no permission to feature. The meal plan scores low on "possible" because the studio has no nutrition expert, and giving diet advice without one could be unsafe. The knee pain idea becomes "When to see a doctor about knee pain", with a trainer giving general tips and a clear note to get medical advice. Priya ends with a shortlist of 8 ideas across all three pillars and four formats.

## Common Mistake
The most common mistake is to skip the filter and post the AI's first ideas in order. The first ideas are often the most general. Another mistake is to judge ideas only by "Do I like it?". Personal taste matters, but scoring against three clear criteria makes your choices easier to explain to a manager or client, and it catches ideas that sound good but cannot be produced.

## Key Takeaways
1. Structured prompts using content pillars, formats and real customer questions give more useful ideas than vague prompts.
2. Filter every idea on three criteria: fits the brand, useful to the audience, possible to produce.
3. A short, varied, scored list is more valuable than a long list of unfiltered ideas.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Generate 30 content ideas for your brand with Claude or ChatGPT, then score each on 3 criteria and keep your top 8.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) [VERSION]; a spreadsheet or table in a document; your voice guide from L02 and persona from L03.
**Steps:**
1. Write down 3 content pillars and 3 formats you can produce.
2. List 3 real or likely customer questions.
3. Write a structured prompt with your voice guide, persona, pillars, formats and questions, and ask for 30 ideas in a table.
4. Copy the table into a spreadsheet and add three score columns (1 to 3) and a total column.
5. Score every idea honestly, then sort by total.
6. Keep your top 8, making sure they cover at least 2 pillars and 2 formats.
7. Write one sentence for why you rejected the lowest-scoring idea.
**What good looks like:** 30 ideas with all three scores filled in, a top 8 with variety across pillars and formats, and a clear reason for at least one rejection.
**Time:** about 25 minutes
