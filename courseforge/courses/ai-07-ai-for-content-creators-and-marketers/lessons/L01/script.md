# L01 Where AI Fits in Content Work | Presenter Script

Course: AI-07 · Video: 5 min · Words: 736

## Hook
It is Monday morning. Your list says three posts, one newsletter, two graphics and a short video, all by Friday. AI tools promise to help with all of it. But which tool helps with which job? And which jobs should stay with you?

## Explain
Hi, and welcome to AI for Content Creators and Marketers. In this first lesson, we look at where AI fits in the work you already do, and where it does not.

Most content work follows the same six steps, for a large company or for your own small channel. Research, ideas, creation, editing, publishing, and finally measuring, where you look at results and decide what to change.

AI tools for content work fall into four main types. The first type is chat assistants, such as Claude or ChatGPT. They work with text. They are good at drafting, summarising, suggesting ideas and rewriting in a new tone. But they are weaker at facts. They can state wrong information in a very confident way.

The second type is design tools, such as Canva. They give you templates and layouts, and many now have AI features that suggest designs or remove backgrounds. They help you make consistent, branded graphics quickly.

The third type is image generators. They create new images from a written description, called a prompt. They are good for original illustrations and backgrounds, but they can struggle with text inside images, with hands and with exact details.

The fourth type is video editors, such as CapCut. They help you cut clips, add music and create automatic captions. They save time on the editing jobs you repeat every week.

No single tool covers the whole workflow. And none of them knows your brand, your customers or your goals unless you tell it. AI can speed up the middle of the work a lot. But the first decision, what to say, and the last decision, whether it is ready to publish, stay with you.

Here is a simple way to picture it. Think of AI as a kitchen assistant. The assistant can chop vegetables and wash dishes very quickly. But the chef still chooses the recipe and tastes the dish before it goes to the customer. If the chef stops tasting, a mistake reaches the table. In content work, you are the chef.

## Demonstrate
Let's look at an example. Yuki runs the social media channels for a small bakery in Osaka, Japan. She maps her weekly workflow, step by step.

For research, a chat assistant can group customer comments into themes. For ideas, it can suggest twenty post ideas from those themes. For creation, it can draft captions, and a design tool can help with graphics. For editing, it can suggest shorter versions. Scheduling features in her social apps can help with publishing. And for measuring, a chat assistant can summarise a small table of results.

But Yuki decides that one step stays fully human: the photos of the bread. Her customers follow the bakery because the photos are real. An AI loaf might look attractive, but it is not the bread they can buy. She also decides that she will read and approve every caption before it is posted.

Many beginners think AI is most useful for creation. They ask it to write a post, and they publish the result. In practice, AI often helps most with the slow, repeated parts around creation: grouping research, listing ideas, making variations and summarising results.

The opposite mistake is using AI for the step where your own knowledge matters most, such as your opinion, or your real product photos. So decide which steps need you before you choose a tool.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, content work usually follows six steps: research, ideas, creation, editing, publishing and measuring. Second, the four main AI tool types are chat assistants, design tools, image generators and video editors, and each is good at different jobs. Third, AI can speed up many steps, but you decide what to say and whether it is ready to publish.
