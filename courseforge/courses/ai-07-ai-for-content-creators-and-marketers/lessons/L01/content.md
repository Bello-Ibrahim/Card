# L01 Where AI Fits in Content Work

Course: AI-07 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O1 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
You open your laptop on Monday with a list: three posts, one newsletter, two graphics and a short video, all by Friday. AI tools promise to help with all of it. But which tool helps with which job, and which jobs should stay with you?

## Explanation
Most content work follows the same six steps, whether you post for a large company or for your own small channel:

1. **Research:** learning about your audience, your market and what people are asking.
2. **Ideas:** deciding what to make, and why.
3. **Creation:** writing text, designing visuals and filming video.
4. **Editing:** improving, shortening and checking the work.
5. **Publishing:** posting at the right time, on the right channel, in the right format.
6. **Measuring:** looking at results and deciding what to change.

AI tools for content work fall into four main types. Each type is good at different things.

- **Chat assistants**, such as Claude or ChatGPT, work with text. They are good at drafting, summarising, suggesting ideas, rewriting for a new tone and organising information into tables or outlines. They are weaker at facts: they can state wrong information in a confident way.
- **Design tools**, such as Canva, give you templates and layouts. Many now include AI features that suggest designs, remove backgrounds or create images from a text description. They are good at producing consistent, branded graphics quickly. [VERSION]
- **Image generators** create new images from a written description, called a prompt. They are good for original illustrations and backgrounds. They can struggle with text inside images, hands and exact details, and their licence terms vary. [VERSION]
- **Video editors**, such as CapCut, help you cut clips, add music and create automatic captions. They are good at saving time on repeated editing tasks. [VERSION]

No single tool covers the whole workflow, and none of them knows your brand, your customers or your goals unless you tell it. AI can speed up the middle of the workflow a lot. The first and last decisions, what to say and whether it is ready to publish, stay with you.

**Analogy:** Think of AI as a kitchen assistant. The assistant can chop vegetables, measure ingredients and wash dishes very quickly. But the chef still chooses the recipe, adjusts the seasoning and tastes the dish before it goes to the customer. If the chef stops tasting, a mistake reaches the table. In content work, you are the chef.

## Worked Example
Yuki runs the social media channels for a small, hypothetical bakery in Osaka, Japan. She maps her weekly workflow and writes which type of AI tool could help at each step.

| Step | What Yuki does now | AI tool type that could help |
|---|---|---|
| Research | Reads customer comments and reviews | Chat assistant: group comments into themes |
| Ideas | Thinks of posts in the shower | Chat assistant: suggest 20 ideas from the themes |
| Creation | Writes captions, takes photos, makes graphics | Chat assistant for caption drafts; design tool for graphics |
| Editing | Fixes typos, shortens captions | Chat assistant: suggest shorter versions |
| Publishing | Posts by hand every morning | Scheduling features in her social media apps |
| Measuring | Looks at likes once a month | Chat assistant: summarise a small results table |

Yuki decides that one step stays fully human: the photos of the bread. Her customers follow the bakery because the photos are real and show the actual products. An AI-generated loaf of bread might look attractive, but it would not be the bread they can buy. She also decides that she will always read and approve every caption before it is posted.

## Common Mistake
Many beginners think AI is most useful for creation, so they ask it to "write a post" and publish the result. In practice, AI often helps most with the slow, repeated parts around creation: grouping research, listing ideas, making variations and summarising results. The other common mistake is the opposite: using AI for the step where your personal knowledge matters most, such as your opinion or your real product photos. Decide which steps need you before you choose a tool.

## Key Takeaways
1. Content work usually follows six steps: research, ideas, creation, editing, publishing and measuring.
2. The four main AI tool types are chat assistants, design tools, image generators and video editors, and each is good at different jobs.
3. AI can speed up many steps, but you decide what to say and whether it is ready to publish.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Map your current content workflow in 6 steps and write which type of AI tool could help at each step, and which step you want to keep fully human.
**Tools:** Pen and paper, a notes app or a spreadsheet. Optional: Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) to suggest ideas. [VERSION]
**Steps:**
1. Draw a table with three columns: "Step", "What I do now", "AI tool type that could help".
2. Fill in the six steps with how you work today, for a real brand or a sample one.
3. For each step, write one tool type (chat assistant, design tool, image generator or video editor) or "none".
4. Circle the one step you want to keep fully human, and write one sentence explaining why.
5. Optional: describe your workflow to a chat assistant and ask where it thinks AI could help. Compare its answer with yours. Do not include confidential client information.
**What good looks like:** All six steps are filled in with a realistic tool type or "none", and the fully human step has a clear reason linked to your audience or brand, such as "customers trust our real photos".
**Time:** about 15 minutes
