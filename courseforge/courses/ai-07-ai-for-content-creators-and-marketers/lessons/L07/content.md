# L07 Long-Form Content: Blogs and Newsletters

Course: AI-07 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O4, O6 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
An AI assistant can write a 600-word blog post in less than a minute. It will be grammatically correct, well organised and, very often, forgettable. It may also contain a statistic that does not exist. Long-form content needs a different way of working.

## Explanation
Long-form content, such as blog posts and newsletters, builds trust over time. Readers come back because the writing is useful and because they can hear a real person or brand behind it. If you ask AI to "write a blog post about X", you usually get general text that repeats what many other articles say.

Instead, build the piece **step by step**, with you in control:

1. **Your outline first.** Write the title, the main point and three or four section headings yourself. This decides what the piece is about.
2. **Key points.** Under each heading, add your notes: examples, opinions, customer stories and facts you know are true.
3. **AI draft.** Give the assistant your outline, notes and voice guide, and ask it to draft one section at a time.
4. **Human editing.** Rewrite the opening, add detail only you know, cut repeated sentences and check that the piece says something clear.

The most important part of human editing is **originality**. Add your own experience, examples and opinions. These make the piece useful and different, and they are things the AI cannot know.

Then **check every factual claim**. AI assistants can invent statistics, quotes, studies and links that look real but are not. This is sometimes called "hallucination". Highlight every number, name, date, quote and source in the draft. For each one, find a source you can link to. If you cannot find one, remove the claim or rewrite it as your own opinion.

**Analogy:** Writing with AI is like building a house with a fast construction team. You give them the plan, and they put up the walls quickly. But you still decide where the windows go, you add the furniture that makes it a home, and you inspect every wall before anyone moves in.

## Worked Example
Anh writes a monthly newsletter for a sample small travel agency in Hanoi, Vietnam. This month's topic is "Travelling in the rainy season".

She writes her outline:
- Title: "Why we love the rainy season (and how to plan for it)"
- Section 1: What the rainy season is really like in northern Vietnam
- Section 2: Three trips that are better in the rain
- Section 3: What to pack
- Section 4: How we change plans when the weather turns

Under each heading she adds notes, such as a story about a group who saw a waterfall at its fullest, and her team's rule of always keeping one free afternoon in every rainy-season trip.

She asks the AI to draft each section. The draft is clear, but section 1 includes: "According to tourism data, visitor numbers fall by 40% in the rainy season." Anh highlights it. She cannot find any source for this number, so she deletes it and writes instead: "Many of our clients avoid these months, so popular places are often quieter."

She also highlights a claim about average rainfall. She checks it against a national weather service and links the source. [VERIFY] Finally, she rewrites the opening with her own memory of her first rainy-season trip as a guide.

## Common Mistake
The most serious mistake is to trust numbers and quotes because they look precise. A made-up statistic with a decimal point looks more believable, not less. A second mistake is to let the AI choose the structure. If the outline is not yours, the piece will probably not say anything new. Start with your outline, and check every claim.

## Key Takeaways
1. Build long-form content step by step: your outline, your key points, an AI draft, then human editing.
2. Add your own experience, examples and opinions to make the piece original and useful.
3. Highlight every factual claim and check it against a source you can link to, because AI can invent statistics, quotes and sources.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Draft a 600-word blog post or newsletter with AI help, starting from your own outline. Highlight every factual claim and check each one against a source you can link to.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) [VERSION]; a document editor with highlighting; a web browser for checking sources.
**Steps:**
1. Choose a topic your audience cares about.
2. Write your own title, main point and 3 or 4 section headings.
3. Add your notes under each heading, including at least one personal example or opinion.
4. Paste your outline, notes and voice guide into the assistant and ask it to draft one section at a time.
5. Edit the draft: rewrite the opening, cut repeated sentences and add details only you know.
6. Highlight every number, name, date, quote and source. Next to each, add a link to a source, or remove the claim.
7. Do not paste confidential client or customer information into the tool.
**What good looks like:** A post of about 600 words that follows your outline, includes at least one personal example, and has every factual claim either linked to a real source or removed.
**Time:** about 35 minutes
