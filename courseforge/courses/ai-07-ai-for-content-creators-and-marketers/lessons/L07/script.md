# L07 Long-Form Content: Blogs and Newsletters | Presenter Script

Course: AI-07 · Video: 5 min · Words: 680

## Hook
An AI assistant can write a six-hundred-word blog post in less than a minute. It will be correct and well organised, and very often forgettable. It may also contain a statistic that does not exist. Long-form content needs a different way of working.

## Explain
In the last lesson, we wrote short social posts. Now we go longer. Blog posts and newsletters build trust over time. Readers come back because the writing is useful, and because they can hear a real person behind it. If you simply ask AI to write a blog post about a topic, you usually get general text that repeats other articles.

So build the piece step by step, with you in control. First, write your own outline: the title, the main point and three or four headings. Second, add your key points under each heading: examples, opinions, customer stories and facts you know are true.

Third, give the assistant your outline, notes and voice guide, and ask it to draft one section at a time. Fourth, edit it yourself. Rewrite the opening, cut repeated sentences, and add the experience, examples and opinions that only you have. That is what makes the piece original.

Then check every factual claim. AI assistants can invent statistics, quotes, studies and links that look real but are not. This is sometimes called hallucination. Highlight every number, name, date, quote and source. Find a source you can link to for each one. If you cannot, remove the claim, or rewrite it as your own opinion.

Writing with AI is like building a house with a fast construction team. You give them the plan, and they put up the walls quickly. But you decide where the windows go, you add the furniture that makes it a home, and you inspect every wall before anyone moves in.

## Demonstrate
Let's watch this in action. Anh writes a monthly newsletter for a sample small travel agency in Hanoi, Vietnam. This month, the topic is travelling in the rainy season.

First she writes her outline. The title is, why we love the rainy season, and how to plan for it. The four sections cover what the season is really like, three trips that are better in the rain, what to pack, and how the team changes plans when the weather turns.

Under each heading she adds notes. One is a story about a group who saw a waterfall at its fullest. Another is her team's rule: always keep one free afternoon in every rainy-season trip.

She asks the AI to draft each section. The draft is clear. But section one says that, according to tourism data, visitor numbers fall by forty percent in the rainy season. Anh cannot find any source for this number. So she deletes it, and writes: many of our clients avoid these months, so popular places are often quieter.

She also highlights a claim about average rainfall. She checks it against a national weather service, and links the source. Finally, she rewrites the opening with her own memory of her first rainy-season trip as a guide.

The most serious mistake is to trust numbers because they look precise. A made-up statistic with a decimal point looks more believable, not less. And if the outline is not yours, the piece will probably say nothing new.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, build long-form content step by step: your outline, your key points, an AI draft, then human editing. Second, add your own experience, examples and opinions to make it original and useful. Third, highlight every factual claim and check it against a source you can link to, because AI can invent statistics, quotes and sources.
