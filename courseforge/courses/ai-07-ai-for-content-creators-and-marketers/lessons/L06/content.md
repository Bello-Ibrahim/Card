# L06 Writing Social Posts and Captions

Course: AI-07 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O4 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
A caption that works well on one platform can fail on another. A long story works in one place, a single sharp line works in another, and a professional network expects something different again. So why do so many people ask AI for "a post" and use the same text everywhere?

## Explanation
A good social post has four parts, and each one changes by platform:

- **Length:** some platforms reward short text; others allow and even reward longer stories. Check the current character limits and how much text shows before "more". [VERSION]
- **Hook:** the first line, which decides whether people stop scrolling. It can be a question, a surprising detail or a clear benefit.
- **Call to action (CTA):** what you want people to do next, such as "comment your favourite scent" or "book through the link in our profile".
- **Hashtags:** a few relevant tags help people find the post on some platforms; on others they matter less. Avoid long lists of unrelated tags. [VERSION]

When you prompt for a post, include all of this: the platform, the length, the type of hook, the CTA, the hashtag rule and your voice guide. For example: "Using the voice guide above, write an Instagram caption of about 80 words for our new lemongrass soap. Start with a sensory hook. End with a CTA to order via the link in our profile. Add 3 relevant hashtags."

Then ask for **several variations**, such as three versions in different styles: a story, a list and a question. The first answer is rarely the best. Comparing options helps you see what works, and you can combine the best lines.

Finally, make the post yours. Edit each post so that at least one sentence is fully your own: a real detail, a personal opinion or something only your brand could say. This keeps the human voice and adds information the AI does not have.

**Analogy:** Asking for variations is like a photographer taking many shots of the same scene. Nobody expects the first photo to be the best one. You take several, compare them and choose, and sometimes you crop the best parts together.

## Worked Example
Wanjiru runs a sample handmade-soap brand in Nairobi, Kenya. She is launching a lemongrass and honey soap. Her voice guide says: "Warm, local, honest. Use: 'handmade in Nairobi', ingredient names. Avoid: 'miracle', 'cures', 'chemical-free'."

She asks for three Instagram captions in three styles.

**Story style:** "Every Saturday, the lemongrass arrives from a small farm outside the city. By Monday, it is soap. Our new lemongrass and honey bar is handmade in Nairobi, in small batches. Order through the link in our profile."

**List style:** "3 things to know about our new bar: 1. Fresh lemongrass. 2. Local honey. 3. Handmade in Nairobi, in small batches. Order through the link in our profile."

**Question style:** "What does your morning smell like? Ours smells like lemongrass and honey. Our new bar is handmade in Nairobi. Tell us your favourite scent in the comments."

Wanjiru checks the facts. The lemongrass does come from a farm outside the city, but deliveries are on Fridays, not Saturdays, so she corrects it. She chooses the story style for Instagram and the list style for a short post on another platform. Then she adds her own sentence: "My grandmother made lemongrass tea every morning, and this bar is for her." One version from the AI said the soap "calms the skin". That is a health claim she cannot prove, so she removes it.

## Common Mistake
The common mistake is to accept the first answer and post the same text on every platform. The result often sounds generic and ignores how each platform works. Another mistake is to let the AI add claims such as "natural", "healing" or "best in the city" without evidence. Always ask for variations, adapt to each platform and remove any claim you cannot support.

## Key Takeaways
1. Good posts are shaped for each platform: length, hook, call to action and hashtags.
2. Ask for several variations and choose or combine, instead of accepting the first answer.
3. Add at least one sentence that is fully your own, and remove any claim you cannot prove.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Write 5 social posts for one product or service using Claude or ChatGPT and your voice guide. Edit each post so that at least one sentence is fully your own.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) [VERSION]; your voice guide from L02; a document to collect your drafts.
**Steps:**
1. Choose one product or service and one or two platforms.
2. Paste your voice guide and write a prompt with the platform, length, hook type, CTA and hashtag rule.
3. Ask for 3 variations in different styles, then ask for more until you have enough material for 5 posts.
4. Choose or combine the best lines into 5 final posts.
5. In each post, write at least one sentence yourself and highlight it.
6. Check every fact and remove any claim you cannot support.
**What good looks like:** 5 posts that fit their platforms, each with a clear hook and CTA, one highlighted sentence in your own words per post, and no unproven claims.
**Time:** about 25 minutes

## Review Flags
- [VERSION] Platform character limits, visible text before "more", and current hashtag practices change often and must be checked before scripting.
