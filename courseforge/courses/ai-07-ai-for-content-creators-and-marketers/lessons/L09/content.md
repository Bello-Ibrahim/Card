# L09 Short Video with CapCut

Course: AI-07 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O4 · Video: 5 min (screen demo)

## Hook
Many people watch short videos on a phone, often with the sound off, and decide in the first two seconds whether to keep watching. That means two things matter more than expensive equipment: a strong opening and clear captions.

## Explanation
A short video of about 30 seconds needs a simple plan:

- **Hook (0 to 2 seconds):** an image or line that makes people stop. Show the result first, ask a question or show movement.
- **Three scenes:** each one shows one idea, for example problem, product in use, result.
- **Call to action:** what the viewer should do next, shown on screen and said aloud.

You can ask Claude or ChatGPT to draft this plan as a **script and shot list**: a table with the time, what the camera shows, what is said and the on-screen text. Give it your voice guide, the product and the platform. Then adjust the plan so every shot is something you can actually film.

**CapCut** is a video editor for phone and computer. It has templates, simple effects and **auto-captions**, which turn speech into on-screen text. Auto-captions save time, but they make mistakes, especially with names, product words, accents and noisy places, so check them word by word. Captions matter because many viewers watch without sound, and captions make video accessible to people who are deaf or hard of hearing.

**Analogy:** A short video is like a shop window seen from a moving bus. You have a few seconds, so the most interesting thing must be at the front, clearly lit and easy to understand at a glance.

## Worked Example
Mateus runs social media for a sample plant shop in São Paulo, Brazil. He asks an AI assistant: "Using the voice guide below, write a 30-second video script and shot list for a vertical video showing how to repot a small plant. Include a hook for the first 2 seconds, 3 scenes and a call to action."

The AI returns a table. Mateus changes one shot, because the shop has no outdoor garden to film in, and uses the shop counter instead. He films five short clips on his phone, vertically, near a window for good light.

This is the on-screen sequence. Menu names may differ in the current version. [VERSION]

1. Open CapCut and start a new project. Import the five clips in order.
2. Trim the first clip so the video opens on the most striking moment: roots coming out of a too-small pot. Keep it under 2 seconds.
3. Trim each scene to match the script timings. Delete pauses.
4. Open the captions feature and choose auto-captions for the spoken language. [VERSION]
5. Play the video and read every caption. Mateus finds two errors: the plant name is spelled wrong, and "soil" is written as "soul". He corrects both by hand.
6. Choose a caption style with high contrast, such as white text with a dark outline, and move the captions away from the bottom edge, where app buttons may cover them.
7. Add the call to action as text on the last scene: "Visit us for free repotting advice."
8. Add quiet background music from the editor's library, and check its licence for commercial use. [VERSION]
9. Export in vertical format and watch the file once on a phone with the sound off.

## Common Mistake
The most common mistake is to trust auto-captions and publish without reading them. One wrong word can change the meaning or embarrass the brand, especially with product and place names. Another mistake is starting with a slow introduction, such as a logo or "Hi everyone, today I want to…". Put the most interesting moment first and the logo at the end.

## Key Takeaways
1. Plan a 30-second video as a hook, three scenes and a call to action, and use AI to draft the script and shot list.
2. Captions and a strong first 2 seconds matter on mobile, because many people watch without sound and scroll quickly.
3. Auto-captions save time but make mistakes, so check them word by word before publishing.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Ask Claude or ChatGPT for a 30-second script and shot list, film it on your phone, and edit it in CapCut with captions. Check that the captions are correct word by word.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) [VERSION]; a phone camera; CapCut (free version where available in your country) [VERSION] [REGION].
**Steps:**
1. Ask the assistant for a script and shot list in a table, including your voice guide, product and platform.
2. Change any shot you cannot film, and make sure the hook fits in the first 2 seconds.
3. Film each shot vertically, in good light. Do not film people who have not agreed to appear.
4. In CapCut, import and trim the clips so the video is about 30 seconds.
5. Add auto-captions, then read and correct every word.
6. Add the call to action as on-screen text, and check the licence of any music you add.
7. Export and watch the video on a phone with the sound off.
**What good looks like:** A video of about 30 seconds with a clear hook in the first 2 seconds, three scenes, a call to action, and captions with no errors that are easy to read on a phone.
**Time:** about 40 minutes
