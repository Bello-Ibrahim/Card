# L09 Short Video with CapCut | Presenter Script

Course: AI-07 · Video: 5 min · Words: 689

## Hook
Many people watch short videos on a phone, often with the sound off. They decide in the first two seconds whether to keep watching. So two things matter more than expensive equipment: a strong opening, and clear captions.

## Explain
In the last lesson, we designed branded visuals. Now we make them move. A short video of about thirty seconds needs a simple plan. First, a hook in the first two seconds. Show the result first, ask a question or show movement. Then three scenes, each with one idea, such as problem, product in use, and result. And finally, a call to action, shown on screen and said aloud.

Ask Claude or ChatGPT to draft this plan as a script and shot list. That is a table with the time, what the camera shows, what is said, and the on-screen text. Give it your voice guide, the product and the platform. Then change any shot you cannot really film.

CapCut is a video editor for phone and computer. It has templates, simple effects and auto-captions, which turn speech into on-screen text. Auto-captions save time, but they make mistakes, especially with names, product words, accents and noisy places. Captions also make video accessible to people who are deaf or hard of hearing.

A short video is like a shop window seen from a moving bus. You have a few seconds. So the most interesting thing must be at the front, clearly lit, and easy to understand at a glance.

## Demonstrate
Let's make one. Mateus runs social media for a sample plant shop in São Paulo, Brazil. He asks an AI assistant for a thirty-second script and shot list for a vertical video on how to repot a small plant, with a hook, three scenes and a call to action.

One shot needs an outdoor garden, and the shop has none. So Mateus changes it to the shop counter. He films five short clips on his phone, vertically, near a window for good light. Then he opens CapCut, starts a new project and imports the clips in order.

He trims the first clip so the video opens on the most striking moment: roots coming out of a pot that is too small. It lasts less than two seconds. Then he trims each scene to match the script, and deletes the pauses.

Now the captions. He opens the captions feature and chooses auto-captions for the spoken language. Then he plays the video, and reads every caption. He finds two errors. The plant name is spelled wrong, and soil is written as soul. He corrects both by hand.

He chooses a high-contrast caption style, white text with a dark outline, and moves the captions up from the bottom edge, where app buttons may cover them. On the last scene he adds the call to action: visit us for free repotting advice.

He adds quiet background music from the editor's library, and checks its licence for commercial use. Finally, he exports the video in vertical format, and watches it once on a phone with the sound off.

The most common mistake is to trust auto-captions and publish without reading them. One wrong word can change the meaning. Another mistake is a slow start, such as a logo or a long hello. Put the best moment first, and the logo at the end.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, plan a thirty-second video as a hook, three scenes and a call to action, and use AI to draft the script and shot list. Second, captions and a strong first two seconds matter on mobile, because many people watch without sound and scroll quickly. Third, auto-captions save time but make mistakes, so check them word by word.

- [REGION] CapCut availability differs by country. Confirm availability in the target markets (en, fr, pt, ar) before recording; if it is not available in a market, name a free alternative editor on the lesson page.

- The five practice clips must be filmed by the team: a small plant being repotted on a shop counter, vertical, near a window, with no people's faces unless they have agreed to appear. Mateus and the plant shop in São Paulo are fictional; no real shop name or logo in shot.
