# L12 Planning a 30-Day Campaign Calendar

Course: AI-07 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O3, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Posting "when you have time" produces random content and random results. A campaign is different: one goal, one audience, a clear plan for 30 days. This lesson starts your capstone.

## Explanation
A campaign plan answers six questions, in this order. You decide the first four; AI can help with the rest.

1. **Goal:** one main result, stated so you can measure it. For example: "Get 150 sign-ups for our free workshop by 30 June."
2. **Audience:** the persona you confirmed in L03.
3. **Channels:** 2 to 4 channels where that audience really spends time. For this capstone, choose at least 3. Fewer channels done well are better than many done badly.
4. **Key dates:** launches, events, holidays and deadlines that the campaign must include.
5. **Content pillars:** 3 themes, from L04, that support the goal.
6. **Posting rhythm:** how often you post on each channel, based on what you can really produce.

Once you have decided these, give them to an AI assistant and ask it to draft a 30-day calendar in a table. Use a clear structure so the calendar is easy to use and check. A useful template has these columns:

| Day | Date | Week theme | Channel | Format | Pillar | Topic / hook | Call to action | Asset status | Owner | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Day and date:** day 1 to 30, with real dates.
- **Week theme:** each week has a focus, for example: week 1 "awareness", week 2 "education", week 3 "proof and stories", week 4 "final push".
- **Channel and format:** where it goes and in what form.
- **Pillar:** which content pillar it belongs to, so you can check the balance.
- **Topic / hook:** the idea in one line.
- **Call to action:** what you want people to do.
- **Asset status:** idea, drafted, reviewed, scheduled, published.
- **Owner:** who is responsible.
- **Notes:** repurposing links ("from L07 blog"), key dates, disclosure needed.

Then review the AI draft. Check that the key dates are in the right places, that the rhythm is realistic, that every week moves the audience toward the goal and that the pillars are balanced.

**Analogy:** A campaign calendar is like a train timetable. The destination is fixed (your goal), the stations are your key dates, and each train leaves at a known time. Without the timetable, trains might still run, but nobody could plan a journey.

## Worked Example
Amara plans a campaign for a sample eco-friendly cleaning products brand in Accra, Ghana. She writes:

- **Goal:** 200 people sign up for a refill-station launch list in 30 days.
- **Audience:** young families in the city who want to reduce plastic waste but worry about cost.
- **Channels:** an image-led social feed, a short-video platform and email.
- **Key dates:** refill station opens on day 22; a community clean-up event on day 15.
- **Pillars:** "how to refill", "real savings", "our community".
- **Rhythm:** 4 feed posts, 2 short videos and 1 email per week.

She asks the AI: "Create a 30-day content calendar as a table with these columns… Use these decisions… Week 4 must build toward the opening on day 22 and follow up after it." The AI returns a full table. Amara reviews it and finds two problems: the clean-up event appears on day 16, and week 2 has no "real savings" content, which is the persona's main worry. She fixes both. She also reduces the videos in week 3 to one, because she knows she has less time that week.

## Common Mistake
The common mistake is to let the AI choose the goal, channels and dates. It will produce a calendar that looks complete but is not connected to your real business, audience or capacity. The second mistake is planning more content than you can make. A realistic calendar that you complete is much better than an ambitious one you abandon in week 2.

## Key Takeaways
1. You decide the goal, audience, channels and key dates; AI helps fill in the structure.
2. A clear calendar template includes day, date, week theme, channel, format, pillar, topic, call to action, status, owner and notes.
3. Review the AI draft for correct dates, balanced pillars and a posting rhythm you can really produce.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 1: write your campaign goal and audience, then build a 30-day content calendar with AI help for a real or sample brand, covering at least 3 channels.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) [VERSION]; a spreadsheet (any free spreadsheet app); your persona, pillars and voice guide from earlier lessons.
**Steps:**
1. Write your goal as one measurable sentence with a number and a date.
2. Write your audience in two or three sentences from your L03 persona.
3. Choose at least 3 channels, your key dates, 3 pillars and a realistic posting rhythm.
4. Give these decisions and the template columns to the AI and ask for a 30-day calendar.
5. Copy the table into a spreadsheet.
6. Review it: check dates, pillar balance, weekly themes and workload, and fix any problems.
7. Write 3 sentences on what you changed from the AI draft and why. Keep the calendar for L13.
**What good looks like:** A measurable goal, a clear audience, and a 30-row calendar across at least 3 channels with every column filled in, key dates in the right places and a workload you can manage.
**Time:** about 40 minutes
