# L11 Repurposing One Idea Across Channels

Course: AI-07 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O5 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
You spent a whole day making one excellent tutorial video. It gets posted once, and a week later nobody sees it. What if that one day of work could fill a week of content on four different channels?

## Explanation
**Repurposing** means turning one core piece of content into several new pieces for different channels. The core piece is usually the one that took the most work: a blog post, a video, a webinar or a detailed guide.

Repurposing is not copying. Each channel has a different audience, format and length:

- **Short-video platforms:** vertical, fast, visual. One idea per video, a hook in the first seconds, captions.
- **Image-led social feeds:** a strong visual, a carousel of slides, or a short caption with a clear call to action.
- **Professional networks:** a clear lesson or insight, often in text, written for colleagues and decision-makers.
- **Email newsletters:** people who chose to hear from you. They expect more depth, a personal tone and a useful link.
- **Blogs and websites:** long, searchable, with headings and details people can return to.

When you paste the same text everywhere, it usually performs poorly. A caption written for a short video makes no sense in an email, and a long blog paragraph is not read on a phone feed. Each audience also needs a different reason to care.

AI is very useful for repurposing. Give it the core content, for example a transcript or article, and ask for a specific new format: "Turn this tutorial transcript into a 5-slide carousel for a design-learning audience. One tip per slide, maximum 20 words per slide." Then edit each piece for the channel and check that nothing new and untrue was added.

A simple method is to ask one question for every new piece: **"What changes for this audience?"** Write the answer in one sentence. If the answer is "nothing", you are copying, not repurposing.

**Analogy:** Repurposing is like cooking one large pot of rice. From the same pot you can make fried rice, a rice salad and rice pudding. The base ingredient is the same, but each dish is prepared differently for a different meal and different people.

## Worked Example
Lucas is a content creator in Lisbon, Portugal, who teaches beginners how to edit photos on a phone. He recorded one 12-minute tutorial: "Five edits that fix most phone photos." He uses AI to help turn it into 7 pieces.

He first asks the AI to create a timestamped summary of his transcript. Then he requests each format separately:

1. **Three short vertical videos**, one edit per video, cut from the tutorial. Change: one idea only, hook in the first 2 seconds, for people scrolling quickly.
2. **A carousel of five slides**, one edit per slide, with before-and-after images. Change: visual and saveable, for people who want a quick reference.
3. **A professional network post** about why small businesses should fix their product photos before paying for ads. Change: business angle, for owners, not hobby photographers.
4. **A newsletter section** with a personal story about his worst photo and a link to the full tutorial. Change: personal and deeper, for subscribers who already trust him.
5. **A blog post** with headings for each edit and screenshots. Change: searchable, for people looking for answers.

The AI draft of the professional post claims that "better photos double sales". Lucas has no evidence for this, so he replaces it with his own experience of a client whose customers asked fewer questions after the photos improved.

## Common Mistake
The most common mistake is to change only the length and nothing else. A shorter version of the same text is still written for the wrong audience. The second mistake is repurposing without checking: when AI adapts content, it may add examples, numbers or promises that were not in the original. Review every repurposed piece as if it were new.

## Key Takeaways
1. Repurposing turns one core piece of content into several new pieces for different channels.
2. Channels differ in audience, format and length, so copying the same text everywhere usually performs poorly.
3. For each new piece, write what changes for that audience, and check that the AI did not add untrue details.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Choose one piece of your content and use AI to repurpose it into at least 4 formats for different channels. For each format, write one sentence explaining the change you made for that audience.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free tier) [VERSION]; Canva and CapCut if you create visuals or video [VERSION]; one existing piece of your content, such as your L07 blog post or L09 video.
**Steps:**
1. Choose your core piece and copy its text or transcript. Remove any private or confidential details.
2. List at least 4 target formats and channels.
3. Ask the AI for one format at a time, giving the channel, the audience and any length limits.
4. Edit each piece in your voice and check it for new claims that were not in the original.
5. Under each piece, write one sentence: "For this audience, I changed…"
**What good looks like:** At least 4 pieces that each look native to their channel, each with a clear sentence about what changed for its audience, and no new unsupported claims.
**Time:** about 30 minutes
