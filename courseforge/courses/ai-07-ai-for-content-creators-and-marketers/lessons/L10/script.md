# L10 Keeping a Human Voice: Editing and Reviewing AI Content | Presenter Script

Course: AI-07 · Video: 5 min · Words: 688

## Hook
In today's fast-paced world, it is more important than ever to. If you have read that sentence many times, you have already learned to spot unedited AI text. And so have your customers.

## Explain
Over the last four lessons, you created posts, articles, visuals and video with AI. Now we make sure they still sound like you, and are safe to publish. Unedited AI text often has signs that readers notice, even if they cannot name them.

The first sign is generic openings, such as, unlock the power of. The second is repeated patterns, like the same sentence shape again and again, or every paragraph ending with a question. The third is claims without evidence, like proven, or loved by thousands. And the fourth is no specific details, nothing that could only come from your brand, your people or your place.

When readers notice these signs, trust can fall, because the content feels like it was made without care. So use a five-point review checklist on every piece. Is it accurate, with every fact checked? Is it on-brand, matching your voice guide? Is it original, with something only you could know, and no copying or imitating of another creator or brand?

Is it inclusive, with respectful language, no stereotypes, and captions or alt text so that different people can use it? And is it legally safe, with licences checked, permissions in place, disclosure that follows your rule, and no misleading claims? If a piece fails one point, fix it, or do not publish it.

Think of it this way. AI gives you clay. Clay has potential, but a lump of clay is not a finished pot. Your editing shapes it into something people recognise as yours. And your review is the firing that makes it strong enough to use.

## Demonstrate
Let's review a real draft. Hira manages content for a sample independent bookshop in Lahore, Pakistan. The shop is holding a poetry evening.

The AI draft says: in today's busy world, it is more important than ever to slow down. Join us for an unforgettable evening of poetry. Loved by thousands of readers, our events are the best in the city. Do not miss out.

Hira applies the checklist. Accurate fails, because loved by thousands has no evidence. On-brand fails, because the shop's voice is quiet, literary and welcoming, and the exclamation marks do not fit. Original fails, because nothing is specific to this evening.

Inclusive needs improving, because the post does not say whether the venue is step-free, and people often ask. And legally safe fails, because best in the city is a claim she cannot support.

Her rewrite says: on Thursday evening, three local poets will read new work in Urdu and English, between the shelves where many of them first found poetry. Free tea is served. The shop is step-free. Reserve a free seat through the link in our profile.

Hira explains her changes. She removed the generic opening and the claims she could not prove. She added the real details of the evening, and the access information. And she matched the calm voice of the shop.

A common mistake is to edit only grammar and spelling. AI text usually has few errors of that kind. The real problems are generic language, unsupported claims and missing human detail. And asking a tool to make it sound more human is not enough. The real fix is your own specific knowledge.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, unedited AI text often shows generic openings, repeated patterns, claims without evidence and no specific details. Second, review every piece with the five-point checklist: accurate, on-brand, original, inclusive and legally safe. Third, the strongest edit adds specific human knowledge that only you or your brand have.
