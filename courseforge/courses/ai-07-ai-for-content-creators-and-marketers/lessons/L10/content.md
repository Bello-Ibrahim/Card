# L10 Keeping a Human Voice: Editing and Reviewing AI Content

Course: AI-07 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O6 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
"In today's fast-paced world, it's more important than ever to…" If you have read that sentence many times, you have already learned to spot unedited AI text. So have your customers.

## Explanation
Unedited AI text often has signs that readers notice, even if they cannot name them:

- **Generic openings:** "In today's world…", "Are you looking for…?", "Unlock the power of…".
- **Repeated phrases and patterns:** the same sentence shape again and again, lists of three adjectives, or every paragraph ending with a question.
- **Claims without evidence:** "the best", "proven", "loved by thousands", with no source.
- **No specific details:** nothing that could only come from your brand, your people or your place.

When readers notice these signs, trust can fall. The content feels like it was made without care.

Use a **5-point review checklist** on every piece before you publish:

1. **Accurate:** Every fact, number, name and quote is checked against a source.
2. **On-brand:** It matches your voice guide: words to use, words to avoid, personality.
3. **Original:** It includes something specific to you: an example, opinion or detail the AI could not know. It does not copy or imitate another creator or brand.
4. **Inclusive:** It uses respectful language, avoids stereotypes, and works for different people, for example captions on video and alt text on images.
5. **Legally safe:** Licences are checked, permissions are in place for people and quotes, disclosure follows your rule, and there are no misleading claims.

If a piece fails one point, fix it or do not publish it.

**Analogy:** AI gives you clay. Clay has potential, but a lump of clay is not a finished pot. Your editing shapes it into something people recognise as yours, and your review is the firing that makes it strong enough to use.

## Worked Example
Hira manages content for a sample independent bookshop in Lahore, Pakistan. She reviews an AI-assisted post for a poetry evening:

"In today's busy world, it's more important than ever to slow down. Join us for an unforgettable evening of poetry! Loved by thousands of readers, our events are the best in the city. Don't miss out!"

She applies the checklist:

- **Accurate:** "Loved by thousands" has no evidence. Fail.
- **On-brand:** the bookshop's voice guide says "quiet, literary, welcoming". The exclamation marks and "don't miss out" do not fit. Fail.
- **Original:** nothing specific about this evening. Fail.
- **Inclusive:** no problem found, but the post does not say whether the venue is step-free, and people often ask. Improve.
- **Legally safe:** no licence issues, but "the best in the city" is an unsupported claim. Fail.

Her rewrite: "On Thursday evening, three local poets will read new work in Urdu and English, between the shelves where many of them first found poetry. Free tea is served. The shop is step-free. Reserve a free seat through the link in our profile."

She explains the change: she removed the generic opening and claims, added the real details of the evening and the access information, and matched the calm voice of the shop.

## Common Mistake
Many people edit only for grammar and spelling, because AI text usually has few errors of that kind. But correct grammar is not the problem. The problems are generic language, unsupported claims and missing human detail. A second mistake is to use an AI tool to "make it sound more human" and stop there. The result often has new generic phrases. The real fix is adding your own specific knowledge.

## Key Takeaways
1. Unedited AI text often shows generic openings, repeated patterns, claims without evidence and no specific details.
2. Review every piece with the 5-point checklist: accurate, on-brand, original, inclusive and legally safe.
3. The strongest edit adds specific human knowledge that only you or your brand have.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Take 3 pieces of AI-assisted content you made this week and review them with the 5-point checklist. Rewrite the weakest piece and explain what you changed.
**Tools:** Your posts, blog or newsletter and visuals from L06 to L09; your voice guide; a document or spreadsheet.
**Steps:**
1. Choose 3 pieces, ideally of different types, such as a post, a newsletter section and a video caption.
2. Make a table with the 5 checklist points as columns and the 3 pieces as rows.
3. Mark each point "pass", "improve" or "fail", with a short note.
4. Choose the piece with the most "fail" or "improve" marks.
5. Rewrite it, adding at least one specific detail only you know.
6. Write 3 to 5 sentences explaining what you changed and why.
**What good looks like:** A complete checklist table with notes, a rewrite that fixes every "fail", and an explanation that links each change to a checklist point.
**Time:** about 30 minutes
