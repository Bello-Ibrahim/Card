# L05 Is It Defensible? Moats for AI Startups

Course: AI-28 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O2 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Imagine you spend three months building an AI product that writes perfect job advertisements. It works. Customers like it. Then a competitor copies it in a weekend, and the model supplier you depend on adds the same feature to its own chat app for free. What do you have left?

## Explanation
A **moat** is anything that makes your business hard to copy or replace. The word comes from the water around a castle that kept attackers out. A business is **defensible** when it has a strong moat.

Many AI products are **wrappers**: a simple screen and a set of instructions around a public model. A wrapper can be a good way to start and learn. But on its own, it is easy to copy, because anyone can use the same model and write similar instructions. There is also a second risk: the model supplier may add the same feature to its own product. We need to be honest about this. The model is not your moat, because your competitors can buy it too.

Stronger moats for AI startups include:

- **Proprietary data**: data that only you have and that improves your product, such as thousands of corrected examples from real customers. You must have the right to use it (see L11).
- **Workflow integration**: your product sits inside the customer's daily work and connects to their other tools. Replacing it would mean retraining staff and changing processes.
- **Distribution**: a reliable way to reach customers that others do not have, such as partnerships with an industry association or a trusted local sales network.
- **Domain expertise**: deep knowledge of an industry's rules, language and edge cases, which is built into the product and hard for outsiders to learn.
- **Trust**: a reputation for accuracy, safety and good support, which matters most in areas such as health, finance and law.
- **Network effects**: the product becomes more valuable as more people use it, for example a marketplace where more buyers attract more sellers.

Most startups do not begin with a moat. They begin with a wrapper and a plan to build a moat over time. The important question is: "What will be hard to copy in one year, and what am I doing now to build it?"

**Analogy:** Anyone can buy the same oven. A new bakery cannot win just because it owns a good oven. But a bakery with loyal customers, a secret recipe improved over years, and a contract to supply the local hospital is very hard to copy. The AI model is the oven. Your data, workflow, distribution and trust are the recipe, the customers and the contract.

## Worked Example
Rafael is a hypothetical founder in Goiás, Brazil. His product reads invoices and bank statements for small farms and prepares monthly accounts for their accountants. It uses a public model. He completes a **moat scorecard**, scoring each moat from 0 (none) to 3 (strong), today and his target in one year.

| Moat | Today | In one year | Plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proprietary data | 0 | 2 | Keep (with permission) corrected examples of rural invoices, which general tools handle poorly |
| Workflow integration | 1 | 3 | Export directly into the accounting software that local accountants use |
| Distribution | 1 | 2 | Partnership with a farmers' cooperative that recommends him to members |
| Domain expertise | 2 | 3 | His family runs a farm; he adds rules for local rural tax categories [REGION] |
| Trust | 1 | 2 | Accountants review every output; he publishes his accuracy on test cases |
| Network effects | 0 | 0 | Not relevant yet |

Today, the total is 5 out of 18. A competitor could copy his screens and instructions quickly. The model supplier could also add invoice reading to its own app. But it would be much harder to copy the corrected rural invoice examples, the cooperative partnership and the connection to local accounting software. Rafael chooses one action for the next month: sign a pilot agreement with the cooperative.

## Common Mistake
Founders often say "our prompts are our secret" or "we use the best model" as their moat. Instructions can be copied or rewritten, and competitors can buy the same model, sometimes on the same day. Another mistake is to claim "we have data" when the data belongs to customers and the startup has no clear permission to use it for improving the product. A data moat is only real if you have the legal right to use the data.

## Key Takeaways
1. A product that only wraps a public model is easy to copy, and the model supplier may add the same feature.
2. Real moats come from proprietary data, workflow integration, distribution, domain expertise, trust and sometimes network effects.
3. Most startups start without a moat; what matters is a clear plan to build one within a year.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Complete a moat scorecard for your idea and write one action that would make it harder to copy within a year.
**Tools:** A free spreadsheet or paper. Optional: Claude (free plan) as a critic.
**Steps:**
1. Copy the six moats from the table above into a sheet.
2. Score each from 0 to 3 for today, and write the score you want in one year.
3. For every moat with a target of 2 or more, write one sentence about how you will build it.
4. Optional: describe your idea to Claude without confidential details and ask: "How could a competitor or the model supplier copy this in a month?" Add any risks you missed.
5. Choose one action you can start in the next 30 days to strengthen your strongest potential moat.
**What good looks like:** An honest scorecard where "today" scores are low for a new idea, targets are realistic, and the chosen action is specific, such as "sign a pilot with one association" instead of "build a brand".
**Time:** about 25 minutes

## Review Flags
- [REGION] Rural tax categories and accounting rules differ by country; the Brazil example is illustrative only and does not describe real tax rules.
