# L13 Finishing Your MVP Demo

Course: AI-28 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O4, O7 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
You have two minutes. Someone important is watching your screen: a potential customer, a partner, an investor. You could show every button and every setting. Or you could show one person solving one real problem. Which one will they remember?

## Explanation
This lesson starts your **capstone**: a validated AI startup concept, an MVP demo and a 10-slide pitch deck. You will build it in three steps:

- **Step 1 (this lesson):** a 2-minute recorded demo of your MVP and a one-page validation summary.
- **Step 2 (L14):** a 10-slide pitch deck in Canva.
- **Step 3 (L15):** pitch practice, improvements and final submission.

The capstone rubric scores five areas: validation evidence, the MVP and its quality checks, unit economics and business model, risk and readiness, and the final deck and demo. Read the rubric before you start, so you know what "excellent" looks like.

**A good demo** shows one user completing the core job from start to finish. It is not a tour of features. Use this structure:

1. **The problem (15 seconds):** who has the problem and what it costs them, in one or two sentences.
2. **The user (10 seconds):** "Meet a typical user." Use a made-up persona, not a real customer's data.
3. **The core task (60 to 70 seconds):** the user enters realistic input and the product does its job. Talk through what is happening in plain language.
4. **The result (15 seconds):** show the output and what the user can now do with it.
5. **Quality and safety (15 seconds):** briefly show how you check the AI, for example your test pass rate or the human approval step.
6. **Close (5 seconds):** one sentence on what comes next.

Record your screen with a free screen recorder, such as the one built into many operating systems [VERSION]. Use fake data only. Close other tabs and hide notifications, so that no personal or confidential information appears.

**Your one-page validation summary** should include: your problem statement, the number of interviews and the top three patterns with evidence, your demand test and whether it met the threshold, your test-set pass rate, and one thing you learned that changed your product.

**Analogy:** A film trailer does not show the whole film. It shows the main character, the main problem and one exciting moment, so that people want to see more. Your demo is a trailer for your product.

## Worked Example
Yasmin is a hypothetical founder in Cairo, Egypt. Her MVP helps small online clothing shops write product descriptions in Arabic and English from a photo and a few notes. Her 2-minute demo plan:

| Time | What is on screen | What Yasmin says |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | A slide with the problem | "Small online shops spend hours writing product descriptions in two languages. In my interviews, 7 of 9 owners said this delays new stock." |
| 0:15–0:25 | Persona card | "This is a made-up shop owner, Nadia, who sells handmade scarves." |
| 0:25–1:30 | The form, then the AI step running, then the draft | "Nadia uploads a photo, adds three notes about material and size, and presses Create. The product drafts both descriptions using only her notes." |
| 1:30–1:45 | The output with an Edit button | "She edits one word and copies it to her shop." |
| 1:45–1:58 | A small table of test results | "I tested 15 cases. 13 passed. When the notes are missing the size, the tool asks for it instead of guessing." |
| 1:58–2:00 | Closing line | "Next, a pilot with 10 shops." |

On her first recording, Yasmin spent 40 seconds on the settings page. She removed it. The settings were not part of the core job.

## Common Mistake
The most common mistake is trying to show everything, which makes the demo long and confusing. The second is a live demo that fails because of a slow connection or an unexpected AI answer. Record your demo in advance, try it several times, and keep the recording. Never hide failures in your written summary, but choose a clean, realistic example for the demo itself.

## Key Takeaways
1. A demo shows one user completing the core job, from problem to result, in about two minutes.
2. Use a clear structure: problem, user, core task, result, quality check and close.
3. Record in advance with fake data, and pair the demo with a one-page summary of your validation evidence.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 1: record a 2-minute demo of your MVP and summarise your validation evidence on one page.
**Tools:** Your MVP from L07 and L08; a free screen recorder [VERSION]; a free document editor; the capstone rubric.
**Steps:**
1. Read the capstone rubric.
2. Write a demo plan using the six-part structure and a table like Yasmin's.
3. Prepare fake but realistic input data and a made-up persona.
4. Close other apps and hide notifications, then practise the flow twice.
5. Record the demo. Keep it under 2 minutes and 15 seconds.
6. Watch it once and remove anything that is not part of the core job.
7. Write your one-page validation summary with the five items listed above.
**What good looks like:** A clear recording of one user completing the core job, with no personal data on screen, and a one-page summary with real numbers from your interviews, demand test and test set.
**Time:** about 60 minutes
