# L04 Testing Demand Before You Build

Course: AI-28 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O3 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Interviews tell you that a problem is real. They do not tell you whether people will act: sign up, give their time or pay. You can find out in a few days, before you build anything.

## Explanation
A **demand test** is a small, cheap experiment that asks people to take a real action. Actions are stronger evidence than words. Three common tests are:

- **Landing page with a waitlist.** A **landing page** is a single web page that describes your product and asks visitors to do one thing, such as leave their email to join a **waitlist**. You can make one with a free no-code website builder or with Canva. If people will not even leave an email, they are unlikely to pay later.
- **Concierge test.** You deliver the service yourself, by hand, and the customer knows a person is doing it. You learn exactly what customers need and whether they value the result.
- **Wizard of Oz test.** The customer uses what looks like a product, but a person does the AI's job behind the scenes. You learn whether the experience works before you build the AI part. Be honest if customers ask, and never make promises about automation that you cannot keep.

Before you run any test, write a **success threshold**: the result that will count as a "yes". For example: "If at least 20 of 100 visitors join the waitlist, we continue." You set this number **before** the test so that you cannot change your mind after seeing the results. Choose a number that would honestly convince you.

A useful term is **conversion rate**: the share of people who take the action. If 100 people visit and 20 join, the conversion rate is 20 out of 100, or 20%.

Stronger actions give stronger evidence. From weakest to strongest: a like on social media, an email address, time spent on a call, a small deposit or pre-order, and a paid order.

**Analogy:** Before a restaurant opens, the chef might run a few "pop-up" evenings in a borrowed kitchen. She does not buy the building first. If people come, pay and return, she has evidence. If the tables are empty, she has lost a few evenings, not her savings.

## Worked Example
Hamza is a hypothetical founder in Lahore, Pakistan. He believes parents of secondary school students want affordable help with maths homework in the evenings. His long-term idea is an AI tutor on the phone that explains problems step by step in Urdu or English.

He does not build anything. Instead, he runs a **concierge test**:

1. He makes a one-page flyer and a simple landing page: "Send a photo of a maths problem before 9 p.m. and get a step-by-step explanation in 30 minutes."
2. He writes his success threshold: "In two weeks, at least 15 families send a problem, and at least 5 agree to pay a small weekly fee for a third week."
3. He shares the page in parent groups at two schools where he knows teachers.
4. When photos arrive, Hamza and a friend who is a maths teacher write the explanations by hand.

After two weeks, 22 families have sent problems and 7 have agreed to pay. The threshold is met. He also learned things he could not have guessed: most requests arrive between 8 and 9 p.m., parents prefer explanations as voice notes, and geometry problems with diagrams are the hardest. His future product will need to handle all three.

## Common Mistake
Founders often run a test and then decide what "success" means after they see the numbers. A weak result becomes "a promising start". To avoid this, write the threshold down and share it with someone before the test begins. Another mistake is sharing the landing page only with friends and family. Their kindness makes the results look better than they are. Share it with people in your real target group.

## Key Takeaways
1. Demand tests ask people for a real action, which is stronger evidence than an opinion.
2. Landing pages, concierge tests and Wizard of Oz tests let you learn before you build the AI part.
3. Set your success threshold before the test, and share it with your target group, not only with friends.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Build a one-page landing page with a no-code builder or Canva, set a success threshold, and share it with at least 10 people in your target group.
**Tools:** Canva (free plan) or a free no-code website builder of your choice [VERSION]; a free form or waitlist tool if your builder does not include one [VERSION]; optional Claude (free plan) to draft the page text.
**Steps:**
1. Write your success threshold, for example: "At least 3 of 10 people join the waitlist within 7 days."
2. Draft the page text: a headline that names the problem, two or three sentences on how you will solve it, and one clear button or form, such as "Join the waitlist".
3. Optional: ask Claude to make the headline shorter and clearer. Check that it does not promise anything you cannot deliver.
4. Build the page in Canva or your no-code builder, and connect a form that collects email addresses [VERSION].
5. Add one sentence explaining how you will use the email addresses, and collect only what you need.
6. Share the page with at least 10 people in your target group.
7. After 7 days, record the visitors (if your tool shows them), the sign-ups and the conversion rate, and compare the result with your threshold.
**What good looks like:** A live, one-page site with one clear action, a threshold written before sharing, at least 10 people from the target group reached, and a short note that states whether the threshold was met.
**Time:** about 45 minutes, plus a week of waiting
