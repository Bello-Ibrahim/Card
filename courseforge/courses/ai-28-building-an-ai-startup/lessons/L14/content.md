# L14 Building the 10-Slide Pitch Deck

Course: AI-28 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O7 · Video: 5 min (screen demo)

## Hook
A person reading your deck may give each slide only a few seconds. If a slide tries to say five things, they will remember none. If each slide says one thing clearly, they can follow your whole story in a few minutes.

## Explanation
A **pitch deck** is a short set of slides that explains your business. It is used when you present, and it is often sent before or after a meeting. The rule for every slide is: **one message per slide**. Write that message as the slide title, as a full sentence, and use the rest of the slide to support it.

**The 10-slide outline**

1. **Problem**: who has the problem and what it costs them. Use a quote or a number from your interviews.
2. **Solution**: what your product does, in one sentence, plus one image.
3. **Demo**: two or three screenshots from your MVP, or a link to your 2-minute demo.
4. **Customer evidence**: interviews, the demand-test result against your threshold, pilots or payments.
5. **Market**: who your first customers are and how many of them you can realistically reach. Build the number from the bottom up (for example, "shops in our city × share we can reach"), and say where each number comes from.
6. **Business model**: how you charge, your gross margin for a typical customer, and your plan for heavy users.
7. **Moat**: what will be hard to copy in one year: data, workflow, distribution or trust. Be honest that the model itself is not the moat.
8. **Competition**: what customers use today, including spreadsheets, manual work and general AI chat apps, and why they will choose you.
9. **Team**: why you are the right people to solve this problem.
10. **The ask**: what you want from this audience, such as a pilot, an introduction, a grant, or a specific investment, and what you will achieve with it.

Keep text short, use large fonts and simple visuals, and keep your risk checklist and unit economics sheet ready as backup for questions.

**Analogy:** A pitch deck is like a series of road signs on a motorway. Each sign gives one piece of information, large and clear, because drivers only see it for a moment. A sign with a paragraph of text would be useless.

## Worked Example
Olumide is a hypothetical founder in Ibadan, Nigeria. His MVP helps pharmacies check stock and expiry dates by reading photos of shelves and invoices. The presenter builds his deck in Canva, following these on-screen steps. Canva's menu names, templates and free-plan features change over time [VERSION].

1. **Open Canva** and sign in with a free account.
2. **Search for a presentation template** by typing "pitch deck" in the search bar, and choose a simple one with plenty of white space [VERSION].
3. **Set the number of slides to 10.** Delete extra slides, or duplicate slides until there are exactly 10.
4. **Apply your brand colours and font** once, using the template's style options, so every slide matches [VERSION].
5. **Write the slide titles first.** For slide 1, Olumide types: "Small pharmacies lose stock to expiry because they track dates on paper."
6. **Add the evidence.** On slide 4, he adds a simple text box: "11 interviews; waitlist 34 of 120 visitors (threshold 25); 4 pharmacies in a paid pilot."
7. **Add screenshots** from his MVP to slide 3 with the Uploads option, after checking that they contain no real customer data [VERSION].
8. **Make a simple chart** for slide 6 using a chart element, showing revenue and cost to serve for a typical customer [VERSION].
9. **Use Present mode** to read through the deck and check that each slide has one message.
10. **Download or share** the deck as a PDF or a view-only link [VERSION].

After the first draft, Olumide's slide 7 said "We use the latest AI". He changed it to: "Our moat: a growing set of corrected shelf photos from partner pharmacies, used with permission, and a distribution deal with a pharmacists' association."

## Common Mistake
Many founders fill slides with long sentences and read them aloud, which makes the audience read instead of listen. Others use a large, unexplained market number from the internet. A market number that you cannot explain damages trust. Use a small, realistic number you can defend, and show how you calculated it. Finally, do not skip the ask: every pitch should end with a clear request.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use ten slides: problem, solution, demo, customer evidence, market, business model, moat, competition, team and the ask.
2. Give every slide one message, written as a full-sentence title and supported by simple evidence.
3. Use your own validation evidence and unit economics, and keep backup detail ready for questions.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 2: build your 10-slide pitch deck in Canva using your validation evidence and unit economics.
**Tools:** Canva (free plan) [VERSION]; your validation summary from L13; your unit economics and business model sheets from L09 and L10; your moat scorecard from L05.
**Steps:**
1. Write the 10 slide titles as full sentences in a document before opening Canva.
2. Open Canva, choose a simple presentation template and set it to 10 slides.
3. Paste your titles into the slides.
4. Add evidence to each slide: quotes, numbers, screenshots or one simple chart. Remove any personal data.
5. Check slide 7: does it describe a moat beyond the model?
6. Check slide 10: is the ask specific?
7. Use Present mode to review, then share or download the deck.
**What good looks like:** Ten slides, each with one clear full-sentence title, evidence from your own work, a defensible market estimate, honest moat and competition slides, and a specific ask.
