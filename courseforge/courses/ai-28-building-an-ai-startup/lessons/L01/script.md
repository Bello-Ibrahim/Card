# L01 The AI Startup Landscape | Presenter Script

Course: AI-28 · Video: 5 min · Words: 731

## Hook
New AI companies appear all the time. Some build giant models that cost a fortune to train. Others build a simple app for one type of shop in one city. Both are called AI startups. So where could you realistically begin?

## Explain
Hi, and welcome to Building an AI Startup. You do not need any business training. We will explain every term as we go.

A startup is a young company that is still searching for a product that many customers want, and for a way to earn money from it that can grow. A founder is the person who starts the company.

Most startups move through five stages. First, an idea: a problem you want to solve. Second, validation: evidence that real people have this problem. Third, an MVP, or minimum viable product, the smallest product that tests your main idea with real users. Fourth, product market fit, when customers keep paying and tell others. Fifth, growth. This course covers the first three.

AI startups can be grouped into four layers. Think of floors in a building. On the ground floor is infrastructure: companies that build large AI models, the chips that run them, or the data centres that host them. This floor needs huge amounts of money and computing power.

Above that are tools for builders, which help other companies build with AI, for example by testing AI output or tracking costs. Then vertical AI, built for one industry, such as law, farming or dentistry. Vertical means going deep into one sector. At the top are applications, for general tasks like writing emails or editing photos.

Most applications and vertical products do not train their own model. They use a model from a model supplier, a company that offers a large model over the internet. The startup adds the rest: the screens, the instructions, the data, the connections to other tools, and customer support.

Here is a picture to remember. In a city's food business, a few large companies run flour mills. That is infrastructure. Others sell ovens. Those are tools. A bakery that makes bread only for hospitals is vertical. A café that sells to anyone is an application.

You do not need to own a flour mill to open a successful bakery. For a small team, vertical AI is often the best place to start, because it wins by knowing one industry better than anyone else.

## Demonstrate
Let's meet three made up founders. Amara lives in Ghana. Farmers near her ask about pests and planting in local languages, but most advice online is in English.

So she builds a phone service. Farmers send a voice note and get advice in their own language. This is vertical AI for farming. Her strength is not the model. It is her knowledge of local crops and languages, and her trust with farmer groups.

Diego lives in Colombia. Small clinics lose hours each week writing appointment reminders and summarising patient forms. He builds a tool for clinic receptionists. That is also vertical AI. Because it touches health information, he must take data protection seriously, which we cover in lesson eleven.

Mei lives in Malaysia. Logistics companies ask her to read delivery documents that arrive in many formats. Later, other software developers want the same function inside their own products. So she considers selling it as a tool for builders.

None of the three trains a large model. All three add knowledge, data and relationships to existing models. And that brings us to a common mistake.

Many new founders believe a real AI startup must build its own model. So they give up, or spend their first months and money on technology no customer asked for. Training a large model belongs to the infrastructure layer. For most founders, the valuable work is understanding a customer's problem deeply.

## Recap
Let's recap. First, the startup journey moves from idea to validation, MVP, product market fit and growth. Second, AI startups sit in four layers: infrastructure, tools for builders, vertical AI and applications. Third, a small team with little money usually has the best chance in vertical AI.
