# L01 Where AI Helps in Customer Support

Course: AI-26 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O1 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Think about the last busy day on your support team. How many of the questions were new, and how many had you already answered a hundred times before? AI is useful for the second group. This lesson shows where it helps, and where people must stay in charge.

## Explanation
AI in customer support is not one single tool. It is a group of uses, and each one helps with a different part of the work. There are five common uses.

**1. Chatbots and virtual assistants.** A chatbot answers customer questions in a chat window, on a website, in an app or in a messaging service. Modern chatbots use a language model, so customers can write in their own words and do not have to choose from a fixed menu.

**2. Ticket triage.** When a message arrives by email, form or chat, AI can read it and add labels: the topic (billing, delivery, technical problem), the urgency, the language and the customer's mood. The system then sends the ticket to the right team. You will practise this in L05.

**3. Agent assistance.** Here AI does not talk to the customer. It helps the agent. It can suggest a reply, summarise a long conversation, find the right help article or translate a message. The agent reads, edits and decides what to send.

**4. Knowledge bases.** A knowledge base is your collection of help articles, policies and guides. AI can help write and update articles, find gaps, and search the knowledge base to answer questions. A good knowledge base is the base for almost every other use in this list.

**5. Support analytics.** AI can read thousands of tickets, reviews and survey comments and group them by theme. For example, it can show that many complaints this month mention a new login page. This helps managers fix the cause of problems, not only answer them.

The goal of all five uses is the same: **faster and more consistent answers without losing the human touch**. AI is good at repeated, predictable work. People are better at judgement, empathy and unusual cases. A well-designed support team uses both.

**Analogy:** Think of a busy hospital reception. A sign on the wall answers "Where is the pharmacy?" A receptionist sorts arriving patients and sends them to the right department. A nurse prepares notes before the doctor arrives. The doctor makes the important decisions. AI in support can play the role of the sign, the receptionist and the helpful nurse, but the difficult decisions still belong to a person.

## Worked Example
Here are three hypothetical companies, each using AI in a different way.

**A telecom company in Morocco.** Youssef leads a support team that receives many messages in Arabic, French and English. Most are about bills and mobile data. The team adds AI triage: each message is labelled by topic and language, and sent to the right queue. Agents now start each ticket with the topic and language already known. Messages about a lost or stolen phone are always sent to a person at once, because they can involve fraud.

**An online shop in Vietnam.** Linh runs support for a shop that sells home appliances. Customers often ask "Where is my order?" and "How do I return an item?" Linh sets up a chatbot that answers these two questions from the shop's help articles. When a customer wants to complain about a damaged product, the chatbot offers to pass the conversation to a person.

**An airline in Mexico.** Mariana manages a contact centre for a small airline. Flight changes involve rules, fees and sometimes stressed travellers. She does not let AI answer customers directly. Instead, AI suggests a reply and a summary to each agent, who checks the fare rules and edits the message before sending it.

Notice that each company chose a different use based on its own questions and risks.

## Common Mistake
Many teams start with the question "How can we replace agents with a chatbot?" This usually leads to frustrated customers and a chatbot that tries to answer questions it should not. A better starting question is "Which of our questions are simple, frequent and low-risk?" Start there, keep people for the rest, and grow step by step.

## Key Takeaways
1. The five main uses of AI in support are chatbots, ticket triage, agent assistance, knowledge bases and support analytics.
2. AI works best on frequent, predictable, low-risk questions; people are needed for judgement, empathy and unusual cases.
3. Choose where to use AI by looking at your own questions and risks, not by starting with a tool.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** List the 10 questions your team receives most often and mark each one: AI can answer it, AI can help an agent, or a human must handle it.
**Tools:** A spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel or LibreOffice Calc) or pen and paper.
**Steps:**
1. Write down the 10 questions your team receives most often. If you do not work in support now, use a company you know as a customer, such as a phone provider or an online shop.
2. Write each question in your own general words. Do not copy real customer messages, names or account details.
3. Next to each question, write one label: "AI can answer", "AI can help an agent" or "Human must handle".
4. For each label, add one short reason, such as "same answer every time", "needs a policy check" or "customer is often upset".
5. Count how many questions you placed in each group. Keep this list; you will use it again in L06 and L09.
**What good looks like:** Ten general questions with no personal data, each with one label and a clear reason. Simple, frequent questions with a fixed answer are marked "AI can answer". Questions about money, complaints, safety or strong emotions are marked "AI can help an agent" or "Human must handle".
**Time:** about 20 minutes
