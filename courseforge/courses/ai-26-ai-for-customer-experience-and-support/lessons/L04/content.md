# L04 Brand Voice and Reply Drafting

Course: AI-26 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O3 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Two replies say the same thing: "Your refund will arrive in 5 working days." One makes the customer feel heard. The other makes them feel like a ticket number. The difference is tone. This lesson shows how to help AI draft replies that sound like your company, while a person stays responsible for every message.

## Explanation
**Brand voice** is the way your company sounds when it writes. Some companies are formal and careful. Others are friendly and relaxed. Customers notice when replies from the same company sound completely different from one agent to the next, or from one day to the next.

AI can draft replies quickly, but without guidance it tends to write in a general, often too long and too formal style. A short **tone guide** fixes much of this. A useful tone guide is about 5 lines long and answers these questions:

1. **Who are we?** For example: "A friendly local company that speaks to customers as neighbours."
2. **How formal are we?** For example: "Use first names. No slang. No exclamation marks in complaints."
3. **How do we show empathy?** For example: "Name the customer's problem in one sentence before giving the solution."
4. **How long are replies?** For example: "Under 120 words. One question per message."
5. **What do we never do?** For example: "Never promise dates or compensation that are not in the policy. Never blame the customer."

You give the tone guide to the AI assistant together with the customer message and the relevant policy or article. The AI then drafts a reply in your voice.

A tone guide also helps with **several languages**. Many AI assistants can draft in French, Portuguese, Arabic and other languages. Ask the assistant to follow the same tone guide in each language. But a person who reads the language well should check important replies, because politeness rules differ between languages and a direct translation can sound rude or strange.

The order of importance is clear: **accuracy first, then empathy and clarity, then speed.** A fast reply with a wrong promise creates a second, bigger problem. That is why an agent always reviews and edits a draft before it is sent. The agent checks facts against the policy, adjusts the tone for this customer, and removes anything the company cannot promise.

**Analogy:** A tone guide is like a recipe card in a restaurant chain. Different cooks in different cities can make a dish that tastes the same, because they follow the same card. But the cook still tastes the dish before it leaves the kitchen. The AI drafts from the recipe card; the agent tastes before serving.

## Worked Example
Ana Paula leads support for a hypothetical meal-kit subscription company in São Paulo, Brazil. Her team replies in Portuguese and English. Her tone guide says: warm and direct, first names, name the problem first, under 100 words, never promise credits that are not in the policy.

A customer writes (in English): "Third time this month my box arrived without the vegetables. I'm paying for nothing. Cancel everything."

Ana Paula gives the AI assistant the tone guide, the message and the policy on missing items (a credit for missing items, and cancellation available any time in the account settings). She removes the customer's name and order number first.

The first draft starts: "We sincerely apologise for any inconvenience this may have caused." It is too formal, it does not name the problem, and it offers "a full refund for the month", which the policy does not allow.

Ana Paula edits it: "Hi, three incomplete boxes in one month is not acceptable, and I understand why you want to cancel. I have added a credit for the missing vegetables. If you still want to cancel, you can do it in your account settings, and I can guide you through it. If you would like to stay, I will ask our warehouse team to check your next box personally." She notes her changes: removed the refund promise, named the problem, shortened the reply.

## Common Mistake
Many agents send AI drafts after a quick look because the text "sounds professional". A draft can sound perfect and still contain a promise the company cannot keep, a wrong date, or empathy that feels copied ("We understand your frustration" in every reply). The correction: read every draft as if you were the customer, check every fact, and change at least the first sentence so that it responds to this person's real problem.

## Key Takeaways
1. A short tone guide of about 5 lines helps AI draft replies that sound like your company, in several languages.
2. Accuracy comes first, then empathy and clarity, then speed.
3. An agent always reviews, checks and edits an AI draft before it is sent.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Write a 5-line tone guide, then use an AI assistant to draft replies to 3 upset customers. Edit each draft for accuracy and empathy and note what you changed.
**Tools:** Claude or ChatGPT (free plan) [VERSION]; a document or notes app.
**Steps:**
1. Write a 5-line tone guide for a made-up company or your own team, using the five questions in this lesson.
2. Write 3 short made-up messages from upset customers, for example a late delivery, a double charge and a rude staff member. Do not use real customer messages or names.
3. Write one or two lines of made-up policy for each case.
4. For each message, paste the tone guide, the policy and the message into the AI assistant, and ask for a reply draft.
5. Edit each draft. Check it against the policy and the tone guide.
6. Under each reply, list what you changed and why, for example "removed a promise not in the policy" or "named the problem first".
7. Optional: ask for one reply in a second language you read well, and check the tone.
**What good looks like:** A clear 5-line tone guide, three edited replies that are accurate, warm and within your length limit, and a short change list for each draft. No draft promises anything the policy does not allow.
**Time:** about 30 minutes
