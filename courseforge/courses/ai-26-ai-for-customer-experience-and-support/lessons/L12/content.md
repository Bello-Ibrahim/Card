# L12 Measuring Success and Keeping the Human Touch

Course: AI-26 · Module: M3 · Objectives: O5, O6 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Imagine your new assistant "handles" almost every conversation without a person. It looks like a great result. But what if many of those customers simply gave up and closed the chat? The numbers you choose decide what you see, so choose them carefully.

## Explanation
Four measures are useful for most support assistants:

1. **First response time.** How long a customer waits for the first useful reply. AI usually makes this faster, but a fast wrong answer is not a success.
2. **Resolution rate.** The share of conversations where the customer's problem was actually solved. Ask the customer ("Did this answer your question?") or check whether they contacted you again about the same issue soon afterwards.
3. **Hand-off rate.** The share of conversations passed to a person. A hand-off is not a failure. The question is whether the right conversations are handed off.
4. **Customer satisfaction.** A short rating or question at the end of the conversation, for both AI-only and handed-off conversations.

This course does not give target numbers for these measures. Good values depend on your industry, customers and channels. Compare your assistant with your own team's results before launch, and follow the trend over time.

Read the measures together, never one alone. The rate of conversations that never reach a person is sometimes called **containment**. A high containment rate is not always good. If customers leave without an answer, containment goes up while satisfaction and resolution go down. Look for warning signs: many conversations that end suddenly, customers who contact you again through email or phone, and low ratings after AI-only conversations.

Keep checking the other points from L11: **accuracy** (read a sample of conversations each week), **tone**, **fairness** (compare results across languages and customer groups) and **privacy**.

**Privacy and transparency** are principles, not optional extras:

- **Tell customers they are talking to AI**, clearly and at the start, and tell them how to reach a person. In some regions the law requires this, for example the transparency obligations in the EU AI Act [REGION] [VERIFY].
- **Collect only the personal data you need**, explain how it is used, and follow the data protection law of the countries where your customers live, such as the GDPR in the European Union [REGION] [VERIFY]. Ask your company's legal or data protection team before any real launch.
- **Do not put real customer data into free test tools.** Move to an approved company tool before a real launch.

**Analogy:** Measuring an assistant is like checking a patient's health. A doctor does not look only at temperature. A normal temperature with a very high heart rate still means something is wrong. In the same way, a high containment rate with low satisfaction means customers are not being helped.

## Worked Example
Sofie runs support for a hypothetical bicycle rental company in Ghent, Belgium, with customers who write in Dutch, French and English. She prepares a one-page launch note for a two-month pilot of her assistant:

- **Scope:** bike availability, prices, opening hours; everything else goes to a person.
- **Measures:** first response time, resolution rate (from a "Did this help?" button), hand-off rate, satisfaction rating, compared with the two months before launch.
- **Warning signs:** chats that end with no rating after one message; repeat contacts within two days; any complaint that the assistant was hard to leave.
- **Quality checks:** a team member reads 20 random conversations each week, including all three languages, and scores them with the L11 checklist.
- **Transparency and privacy:** the welcome message says it is an AI assistant; the data protection officer approves what data is collected before launch.
- **Review schedule:** weekly quality check, monthly review of measures, and a decision after two months to continue, change or stop.

After the first month, containment is high, but satisfaction for French conversations is lower. Sofie reads the conversations and finds that two French articles are missing. She adds them and watches the next month's results.

## Common Mistake
Many teams launch an assistant and then only report one number, usually the number of conversations it "handled". This rewards the assistant for keeping customers away from people. The correction: always report containment together with resolution and satisfaction, read real conversations regularly, and treat a well-timed hand-off as good service.

## Key Takeaways
1. Track first response time, resolution rate, hand-off rate and customer satisfaction together, compared with your own results before launch.
2. High containment is not always good; customers who give up also count as "contained".
3. Tell customers they are talking to AI, make a person easy to reach, collect only the data you need, and follow local data protection and disclosure rules.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Capstone step 4: finish your prototype and write a one-page launch note with the measures you will track and the review schedule.
**Tools:** Your assistant, hand-off flow and test sheet from L09 to L11; a document editor (Google Docs, Word or LibreOffice Writer).
**Steps:**
1. Make any final fixes from your L11 test results and check that the welcome message says it is AI.
2. Write a one-page launch note with these headings: Scope, Hand-off rules, Measures, Warning signs, Quality checks, Transparency and privacy, Review schedule.
3. Under Measures, list at least the four measures from this lesson and how you will collect each one. Do not invent target numbers.
4. Under Transparency and privacy, write how customers are told they are talking to AI and which personal data the assistant collects and why.
5. Set a review schedule, including who reads sample conversations and how often.
6. Collect your capstone deliverables using the rubric checklist.
**What good looks like:** A finished prototype and a clear one-page note that a manager could approve. Measures are read together, warning signs are named, and privacy and transparency are covered without claims about specific laws you have not checked.
**Time:** about 45 minutes
