# L08 Nigeria's NDPA: Structure and Key Duties

Course: AI-30 · Module: M2 · Objectives: O1, O3 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Many AI governance courses stop at the EU. But if your organisation processes personal data of people in Nigeria, the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) has its own structure, its own regulator and its own duties. How does it apply when a Nigerian fintech uses AI to decide who gets credit?

## Explanation
This lesson is educational and is not legal advice. Nigerian data protection rules are developing, and implementing guidance may add detail to the Act. Check every point below against current official sources.

The NDPA has a structure that GDPR professionals will recognise, with some important local features.

**1. A national regulator.** The Act establishes [Nigeria's national data protection regulator] with powers to issue regulations and guidance, investigate complaints and impose sanctions [VERIFY] [REGION]. Its implementing directives or guidance can add practical duties, so they must be read alongside the Act [VERIFY] [REGION].

**2. Controllers and processors.** Duties fall on **data controllers** and **data processors**, with definitions in [the interpretation section] [VERIFY] [REGION].

**3. Principles.** Similar to the GDPR: lawful, fair and transparent processing; purpose limitation; minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; security; and accountability [VERIFY] [REGION].

**4. Lawful bases.** A list comparable to the GDPR's, with extra conditions for sensitive personal data [VERIFY] [REGION].

**5. Rights.** Data subjects have rights such as information, access, correction, erasure, objection and protections about automated decisions (see L09) [VERIFY] [REGION].

**6. Security and breaches.** Controllers must apply appropriate technical and organisational measures and handle personal data breaches, including notification in some cases (see L14) [VERIFY] [REGION].

**7. Impact assessments.** Processing likely to create a high risk to people requires a data protection impact assessment (see L10) [VERIFY] [REGION].

**8. Cross-border transfers.** Transfers of personal data out of Nigeria are allowed only on permitted grounds, such as an adequate level of protection in the receiving country or other recognised safeguards [VERIFY] [REGION]. AI projects often send data abroad, for example to cloud hosting or model providers, so this duty matters.

**9. Extra duties for some organisations.** The Act gives the regulator power to classify some controllers and processors as being of greater importance, which may bring extra duties, such as registration, appointing a data protection officer, or periodic filings or audits [VERIFY] [REGION]. Classification depends on [the relevant criteria and thresholds] [VERIFY] [REGION].

**10. Enforcement.** Sanctions are set out in [the enforcement sections]; do not quote penalty figures without checking [VERIFY] [REGION].

**Analogy:** If you have driven in one country, you can drive in another. The steering wheel, pedals and main road signs are familiar. But the speed limits, the licence rules and the police who enforce them are local. The NDPA is familiar in shape, but its local rules must be learned on their own terms.

## Worked Example
Hauwa Bello is head of compliance at a hypothetical fintech in Abuja. It plans to use an AI model to score applicants for small business loans, using bank statements, mobile phone usage data and information from applicants.

Hauwa lists the duties that are likely to apply:

1. **Lawful basis and transparency:** choose and record a basis for training and for scoring, and explain the AI scoring in the privacy notice in plain words.
2. **Automated decisions:** if loans are refused without human involvement, apply the NDPA protections, including a way to ask for human review [VERIFY] [REGION].
3. **Impact assessment:** credit scoring with new data types is likely high risk, so a DPIA should be completed before launch [VERIFY] [REGION].
4. **Cross-border transfer:** the model is hosted by a cloud provider outside Nigeria, so a valid transfer ground must be confirmed and recorded [VERIFY] [REGION].
5. **Classification check:** the fintech must check whether it falls into a category with registration, DPO or filing duties [VERIFY] [REGION].

She adds one more point: the mobile usage data may reveal sensitive facts, so she asks the data team to justify each field.

## Common Mistake
Many international teams assume "if we comply with the GDPR, we comply with the NDPA". The principles overlap, but local duties, such as registration, filings, transfer grounds and the regulator's guidance, can differ. Keep a separate NDPA column in your compliance map.

## Key Takeaways
1. The NDPA sets up a national regulator and places duties on data controllers and data processors, in a structure similar to the GDPR.
2. Key duties cover principles, lawful bases, rights, security, impact assessments and cross-border transfers.
3. Some organisations may carry extra duties, such as registration or a DPO, depending on their classification; check current sources, as this lesson is educational, not legal advice.
