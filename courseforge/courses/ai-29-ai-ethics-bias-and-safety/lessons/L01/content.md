# L01 Why AI Ethics Matters to Everyone

Course: AI-29 · Module: M1 · Objectives: O1 · Video: 5 min

## Hook
Imagine you apply for a job and never get an interview. No person read your application. A computer system sorted it into the "no" pile, and nobody can tell you why. Would you want to know how that system was built and checked?

## Explanation
AI systems now help decide who gets a job interview, a loan, an insurance price or a medical appointment. They also write emails, answer customer questions and suggest what we read. When an AI system makes a mistake, the mistake does not stay inside the computer. It reaches real people, and it can repeat thousands of times before anyone notices.

AI ethics is the practice of asking: is this system fair, honest, safe and under human control? You do not need to be a programmer to ask these questions. People who **build** AI, people who **buy** AI for their organisation and people who simply **use** AI tools every day all make choices that affect others.

This course uses eight key terms. Learn them now, because every later lesson builds on them.

- **Bias:** a system gives results that are systematically better or worse for some groups of people. Example: a voice assistant that understands some accents much less well than others.
- **Fairness:** the goal that a system treats people and groups in a just way. Example: a loan tool that approves equally qualified applicants at similar rates, whatever their gender.
- **Hallucination:** an AI tool produces confident information that is false or invented. Example: a chatbot gives a book title and author that do not exist.
- **Misinformation:** false or misleading information that spreads, whether or not anyone meant to deceive. Example: an AI-generated "news" picture shared as if it were real.
- **Privacy:** people's control over their personal information. Example: not pasting a customer's name and phone number into a public chatbot.
- **Safety:** avoiding physical, financial, emotional or social harm. Example: a health chatbot that refuses to give a dangerous medicine dose and tells the user to see a doctor.
- **Transparency:** people can find out that AI is being used and understand, in simple terms, how it reaches its results. Example: a website that says "This reply was written by an AI assistant."
- **Accountability:** a named person or team is responsible for the system's results and for fixing problems. Example: a manager who signs off every AI-assisted hiring decision.

**Analogy:** An AI system is like a new colleague who works very fast but was trained by someone else. The colleague can be very helpful. But you did not see their training, so you do not know which habits they learned, which mistakes they repeat or which customers they misunderstand. Before you trust their work, you check it, and you learn what they were taught.

## Worked Example
Linh Tran manages customer service for a hypothetical hotel group in Vietnam. Her company buys an AI tool that reads guest reviews and flags "urgent complaints" for the team to handle first.

After two months, Linh notices something. Reviews written in formal English are flagged quickly. Reviews written in Vietnamese, or in short and simple English, are often missed, even when they describe serious problems such as a broken door lock.

Linh uses the eight terms to describe what is happening:
- It is a **bias** problem: the tool works worse for some guests, based on their language.
- It is a **fairness** problem: those guests wait longer for help.
- It is a **safety** problem: a broken lock is a real risk.
- It raises **transparency** questions: the vendor never explained which languages the tool was tested on.
- It raises **accountability** questions: who in the company checks the flagged list?

Linh's team starts a daily manual check of unflagged reviews and asks the vendor for test results by language. Nobody on the team is a programmer, but their questions lead to a safer service.

## Common Mistake
Many people think AI ethics is only a job for engineers or lawyers. In fact, many harms happen because of how a tool is bought and used, not only how it is built. A manager who uses a tool for a task it was not designed for, or a staff member who copies a chatbot answer without checking it, can cause harm with a well-built system. Everyone who touches AI has a part in using it responsibly. (Legal duties are covered in course AI-30. This course stays at a practical level.)

## Key Takeaways
1. AI now affects decisions about jobs, money and health, so its mistakes reach real people, often at large scale.
2. The eight key terms are bias, fairness, hallucination, misinformation, privacy, safety, transparency and accountability.
3. Builders, buyers and everyday users all make choices that affect whether AI is used responsibly.

## Hands-on Exercise
**Task:** Match the 8 key terms to 8 short scenarios, then check your answers with the answer key.
**Tools:** Pen and paper, or any notes app.
**Steps:**
1. Read these scenarios:
   a. A chatbot gives a lawyer a court case that never happened.
   b. A photo app works badly for people with darker skin tones.
   c. A company cannot say who is responsible when its AI tool rejects a customer by mistake.
   d. An employee pastes a client's bank details into a public chatbot.
   e. A fake AI-made video of a politician spreads on social media.
   f. A shopping website does not tell users that its "assistant" is an AI.
   g. A fitness app suggests an extreme diet to a teenager.
   h. A scholarship tool selects equally qualified students from rural and city schools at similar rates.
2. Write the key term that fits each scenario best.
3. Check with the answer key: a hallucination, b bias, c accountability, d privacy, e misinformation, f transparency, g safety, h fairness.
4. For any answer you got wrong, write one sentence about why the key's term fits better.
**What good looks like:** At least 6 of 8 correct on the first try, and a short, clear reason for each correction. You notice that some scenarios touch more than one term, such as b (bias and fairness).
**Time:** about 10 minutes
